## Course unit overview

This is a two-semester unit delivered through weekly small group tutorials in semester one and into semester 2. This is supported by online material.  The main focus is on acquiring and/or analysing data to test one or more hypotheses in a mini-project related to the student’s chosen pathway, and then presenting it in the form of a written report. Students will work with a dataset and learn how to organize, present and interpret data. This unit will include guidance on how to structure a report to present a narrative placing the work in context, explaining the approach, interpreting the data and assessing the extent to which the hypothesis is supported.  There will be an element of teamwork in working with a dataset as a group and in presenting a summary to peers. Students will also receive guidance in employability, specifically CV writing, and consider behavioural issues related to EDIA in a professional environment.

## Aims

The aim of this year-long course unit is to build on first year content in the development of professional and transferable skills to support the future employability of students in the Earth and Environmental Sciences.  These skills include those related to communication, to the acquisition and treatment of data to test hypotheses, to project planning and organisation, and teamwork. A particular emphasis is on reflecting on and incorporating feedback to improve a draft of a piece of work. These skills are delivered primarily within the context of undertaking a mini-project leading to submission of a report for assessment and feedback. Specific elements related to careers and to Equality, Diversity, Inclusion and Accessibility (EDIA) are also included.

## Syllabus

There are 10 weekly meetings in semester 1 and 4 weeks in semester 2. In semester 1, one meeting will be a careers event and one will be a plenary devoted to EDIA issues. Each pathway has a bespoke project, with the following waypoints (or similar agreed by the teaching team) guiding all tutors.

- Week 1:  Introduction to the project including the hypothesis to be tested and the nature of the test.
- Week 4:  The approach to statistical analysis of the data will have been discussed and students should be in a position to undertake their own analysis and report back findings.
- Week 7:  Statistical discussion complete, guidance on choosing and formatting figures, guidance on structuring a report to present a narrative, guidance on complying with a template will all have been delivered.
- Week 8:  submission of draft (no extension applied)
- Week 9:  return of individual feedback, discussion of general points

(Students revise reports)

- Week 1 semester two:  Students explain how they acted on the feedback received and review revisions of one another’s reports, giving further feedback.
- Week 2 Planning of group presentation (Submit final report)
- Week 3 Rehearse group presentation

At some point: Plenary session where groups present their results.

## Teaching and learning methods

Teaching is done in small groups led and supported by a staff member with pathway-relevant expertise,  with online content specific to elements of report writing and statistical analysis.

Students are given continuous feedback on their progress from staff and there will be detailed feedback on a draft report implementing the write – feedback – revise pattern for developing writing skills.  Further feedback will be provided after final submission.
