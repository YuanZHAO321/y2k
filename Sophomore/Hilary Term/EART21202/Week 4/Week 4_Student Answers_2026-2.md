## Slide 3

- Q1. Answer

- This question is asking you to draw on your knowledge and understanding of aerosol effect on radiative forcing.

- To forecast the effect of changing ammonium sulphate levels, the key properties that need addressing are:

- i) source: where are the sources and what is the emission rate by location?

- ii) sink: what are the sinks, what are their rates, how do these determine the spread of ammonium sulphate aerosol and the atmospheric lifetime?

- iii) aerosol-radiation interaction and aerosol-cloud interaction: what is the direct effect of ammonium sulphate aerosol – what affect does it have on planetary albedo?, what is the indirect effect of ammonium sulphate – how does it change cloud albedo and cloud lifetime?

- Since aerosol have an average lifetime of days, then following an instantaneous decrease in ammonium sulphate precursors, a simulation of one years could suffice to predict effect on radiative forcing whilst also allowing assessment of seasonal variation

- The production of ammonium sulphate will need to be simulated as it is a secondary aerosol, then the aerosol-radiation interaction and aerosol-cloud interaction needs simulating.

- Uncertainties arise from the baseline scenario and the perturbed scenario due to uncertainties in aerosol properties: source strengths by location, sink strength by location, aerosol-radiation interaction (albedo and number-size distribution) and aerosol-cloud interaction (number-size distribution and hygroscopicity).

## Slide 5

- Q2. Answer

- This question is asking you to draw on your knowledge and understanding of the effect of ozone and land-use change on radiative forcing.

- Radiative forcing is the change in radiation balance at the tropopause between the incoming solar radiation and the outgoing earth’s radiation (net irradiance).

- Since 1750 ozone concentrations in the troposphere have increased due to human emissions of precursors. Ozone is a greenhouse has with a lifetime around 3 weeks, so it is not well mixed throughout the troposphere and is considered short-lived in comparison to methane and carbon dioxide. Ozone affects radiative forcing by absorbing short- and long-wave radiation, thereby reducing the radiation flux returned to space from Earth when concentrations increase. Being a greenhouse gas, it exerts a positive radiative forcing, though 4 times less than carbon dioxide, with 0.5 W m-2 since 1750.

- Since 1750 land-use change has changed land covered originally in forest and shrub to farm. The overall effect has been to increase planetary albedo since the new land cover acts to increase surface albedo. In mid-latitudes, replacing forest with farm increases the effectiveness of snow reflectance, whilst in semi-arid regions, replacing forest and shrub with farmland increases the effectiveness of soil reflectance. By increasing albedo in these ways, land-use change has a negative effect on radiative forcing, though it is 4 times less than aerosol, with -0.25 W m-2 since 1750. The effect of land-use change on radiative forcing will continue so long as demand for agriculture continues.
