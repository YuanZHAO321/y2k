## Slide 2

- Q1.

- How does climate change impact human health? Which populations are most vulnerable?

## Slide 3

- Q1. Answer

- How does climate change impact human health? Which populations are most vulnerable?

- Heat-related illnesses: for example, more heatwaves can increase deaths among older people and outdoor workers.

- Diseases spread by insects: Climate change contributes to the spread of insect borne diseases; for instance, rising temperatures expand mosquito habitats, increasing the transmission of illnesses like malaria.

- Air pollution: Smoke from wildfires and ozone pollution can make respiratory problems worse.

- Mental health:  Climate change impacts mental health by increasing anxiety and stress, especially for those facing displacement due to rising sea levels, extreme weather, or environmental change.

- Most at risk: Those most vulnerable to climate change include low-income households and populations in developing countries, who often face greater exposure and fewer resources to adapt.

## Slide 4

- Q2.

- What are the key climate risks facing the UK? How should policymakers adapt?

## Slide 5

- Q2. Answer

- What are the key climate risks facing the UK? How should policymakers adapt?

- This can be found in the IPCC worldwide adaptation slide.

- Flooding: Rising sea levels and heavier rainfall increase flood risks.

- Adaptation: Strengthen flood defences, improve drainage systems, and implement natural flood management (e.g., wetland restoration).

- Heatwaves: More frequent extreme heat events (think about the UK stats I showed in the lecture as examples)

- Adaptation: Increase tree cover, improve building insulation, and develop heatwave emergency plans.

- Agriculture: Droughts threaten crops and food production.

- Adaptation: Invest in drought-resistant crops, improve water management, change in fertiliser use, and support sustainable farming techniques.

- Policy measures: Urban areas face rising climate risks in many cases.

- Adaptation: Expand green spaces, enforce climate-resilient building practices, and invest in early warning systems for extreme weather.

## Slide 8

- Q4.

- How does climate change disproportionately affect vulnerable populations, such as Indigenous communities, low-income households, and developing nations?

## Slide 9

- Q4. Answer

- How does climate change disproportionately affect vulnerable populations, such as Indigenous communities, low-income households, and developing nations?

- Financial burden: Poorer communities have fewer resources to recover from climate disasters and often least well able to adapt

- Relocation risks: Coastal and island communities face displacement. This could also be due to economics (e.g. water quality affecting fishing productivity)

- Health disparities: Poor air quality and extreme heat disproportionately affect low-income urban populations.

- Policy solutions: Climate frameworks, equitable adaptation funding (see the Paris Agreement for more details)

## Slide 10

- Q5.

- What are the key differences between RCP2.6, RCP4.5, RCP6.0, and RCP8.5? What policy choices can push us toward the lower emissions scenarios?

## Slide 11

- Q5. Answer

- What are the key differences between RCP2.6, RCP4.5, RCP6.0, and RCP8.5? What policy choices can push us toward the lower emissions scenarios?

- RCP2.6: Requires drastic emission cuts, net-zero targets, aggressive mitigation (Paris Agreement goal), but note there is now an RCP1.9 as mentioned in the lecture

- RCP4.5 & RCP6.0: Intermediate scenarios with partial mitigation; likely warming of 2-3°C.

- RCP8.5: High emissions, worst-case/business as normal scenario (warming >4°C).

- Policy solutions: Renewable energy adoption, energy production, reforestation (see lecture 9 for mitigations for a range of economic sectors)

## Slide 13

- Q6. Answer

- How do increasing global temperatures contribute to extreme weather events such as hurricanes, heatwaves, and flooding? Give specific examples.

- Heatwaves: More frequent and intense due to rising average temperatures

- Hurricanes & Storms: Warmer oceans provide more energy for storms

- Flooding: Increased precipitation intensity leads to flash floods

- Overall, climate change amplifies extreme events, necessitating better forecasting and resilience planning.
