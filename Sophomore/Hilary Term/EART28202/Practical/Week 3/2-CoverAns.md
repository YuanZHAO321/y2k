# **EXERCISES  IN  STRAIN  ANALYSIS**

The following exercises are designed to take you through some simple strain analysis techniques using situations that you might well encounter while mapping. The first focuses on recovering the 3d state of strain from measurements of 2d strain on three differently oriently planes; the other three explore widely used techniques to determine 2d strain.

## **Exercise 3**

The Fry method is a simple but very powerful technique for analysing the 2d strain of initially equidimensional objects ( _e.g._ , circular clasts, equant grains in a monomineralic rock). Here we apply it to the oolitic ironstone shown in Figure 2 in order to estimate the ellipticity and orientation of the strain ellipse on the thin section plane.

## _Method_

1. On a sheet of tracing paper mark the centres of all the oolites, and number these points. HINT: the exercise will proceed faster if you number the oolites in the centre of the image first and work outwards.

2. On another sheet of tracing paper mark a central reference point. Place this over oolite number 1 and trace the centres of all the other oolites on to the overlay.

3. Move the overlay (without rotating it) so that the overlay reference point lies over oolite number 2, and trace the centres of all the other oolites on to this overlay.

4. Repeat step 3 for all the other oolites. After a few oolites an elliptical point vacancy starts to emerge around the overlay reference point. When it does so, if you want to speed matters up then the grain centres at distances well away from this vacancy need not be marked.

5. The elliptical vacancy has the aspect ratio and orientation of the strain ellipse. Determine the long and short axes of this ellipse by eye and calculate _Rs_ = (1+ _e_ 1) / (1+ _e_ 3). Also measure the angle between the horizontal and the long axis of the ellipse.

The Fry method as used in this exercise relies on the measured objects being all of about the same size and to be fully dispersed (not clustered). It is easily modified for use with initially equidimensional objects of varying size (see Erslev, 1988, _Journal of Structural Geology_ 10:201-209), and formal strategies for fitting the ellipse to the data have also been developed (see Erslev and Ge, 1990, _Journal of Structural Geology_ 12:1047-1059).

The _Rf_ / ´ method as used in this exercise requires all of the measured objects to have the same initial ellipticity and for the initial orientation of the long axes of these elliptical objects to be random. The fact that in real world situations one or both of these conditions are not generally satisfied complicates the application of the method.
