- 1 The minor folds are not in constant orientation and so are F1.

- 2 The schistosity is axial planar to the minor folds and so is S1.

- 3 The axial traces are located by the folded lithological contacts and changing the minor fold asymmetries. They are folded and hence are F1 folds.

- 4 The F2 “axial traces” are not folded implying that they have not been affected by any later folding event.

- 5 D2 has reoriented the F1 folds to produce a fold interference pattern.

- 6 The crenulation cleavage (S2) is only developed between the two F2 axial traces and it is not axial planar to the F2 folds. It is, however, consistent with the orientation of a cleavage generated within a shear zone which has the two F2 “axial traces” as its boundaries. The orientation and curvature of S2 implies that the slip vector has a dextral strike-slip component while, in principle, the fact that the angle between S2 and the shear zone boundaries is less than 45° suggests transpression, that is, that the slip vector also has a reverse dip-slip component.

- 7 The different limbs of the F1 folds were reorientated by different amounts during the D2 shearing, which is also consistent with there being a dip-slip component to the D2 slip vector.

- 8 The dyke crosscuts the F1 axial traces but is boudinaged across the D2 shear zone implying that it was intruded between D1 and D2. The boudinage and apparent offset of the dyke across the shear zone is consistent with a dextral strike-slip component to the slip vector, while the fact that the apparent offset of the dyke is larger than that of the fold traces is consistent with there being a dip-slip component.

- 9 The dyke opened normal to its margins.

- 10 The orientation of the vein quartz suggests that these veins are tension gashes formed during D2 shearing .
