The pore fluid pressure is given by the sideways shift of the Mohr circle that is required to get it to touch the failure envelope = 62.2 MPa.

Alternatively, this may be solved from the failure criterion. Working in effective stresses:

s 1, _eff_ = _a_ + _b_ s 3, _eff_ Î s 1 - _pfluid_ = _a_ + _b_ (s 3 - _pfluid_ ) and so

_pfluid_ =   ( _a_ + _b_ s 3 - s 1 ) / ( _b_ - 1)

From (a) we have: _a_ = 210 MPa     ; _b_ = 4.075 From (d) at 3 km depth we have s 3 = 69 MPa, and so with s 1 = 300 MPa, then

_pfluid_ =   62.2 MPa

The petroleum industry commonly uses the pore pressure parameter, l , to assess pore pressures. l is defined,

l ¬ _pfluid_ / s _V_

For normally pressured pore fluids (fluids connected all the way up to the surface) we see from (f) that

l ¬ _pfluid_ / s _V_ =   30 / 69   =   0.43

A value of l greater than this means that the fluids are over-pressured (liable to “erupt” if breached by a borehole). In scenario (g) the fluids are highly overpressured:

l ¬ _pfluid_ / s _V_ =   62.2 / 69   =   0.90

The maximum value of l ® 1 because is _pfluid_ > s _V_ then the rocks are in tension and rocks are very weak in tension.
