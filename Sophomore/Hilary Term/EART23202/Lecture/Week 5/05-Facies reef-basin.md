### **Carbonate reefs and build-ups Definition**

##### **Carbonate build-ups are laterally-restricted structures, which have usually undergone organically-mediated growth. They can be grossly divided into:**

**Organic (skeletal) reefs, built by organisms with a rigid calcareous frame, may be matrix or skeleton supported and deposited in warm or cold water and able to withstand high energy wind/wave action**

**Reef (mud) mounds are inorganically and/or biogenically constructed but lack a rigid skeletal framework and unable to withstand high energy wind/wave action**

### **Summary**

- **Carbonate build-ups are laterally-restricted structures with organicallymediated growth**

- **Distribution of reef-building organisms strongly controlled by environmental factors (light, wave energy….): preferential growth in shallow water (antecedent topography)**

27 – EART23202: Slope/basin carbonate environments

### **Summary**

**Slopes are dominated by (storm) current deposition and gravity-driven deposition. Sediment textures are highly variable; sediment composition reflects source area (reef and/or shoals at platform margin, reworked upper slope).**

**Basinal sediments are dominated by pelagic deposition of carbonate mud, commonly mixed or interbedded with siliciclastic shales/mudrocks**

**Biogenic component in pelagic sediments is planktonic. Organisms and the resulting sediments varied over time in response to evolution. Reading: Tucker (1990), p. 228-236, 238-262, 274-283**
