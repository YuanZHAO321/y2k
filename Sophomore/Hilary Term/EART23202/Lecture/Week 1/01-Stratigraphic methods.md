# **Stratigraphic principles**

**Superposition: each bed is younger than the one below it Cross cutting: a bed (or unit) is younger than the one it cuts across Original horizontality: consolidated strata that are tilted now were originally deposited horizontal**

**Continuity: in a basin, the same bed is of identical age in every part of the basin; beds terminate where deposition was not possible, or when beds were eroded or deformed after deposition**

**Inclusion: if an element A is included in an element B, then A is older than B Paleontological identity: all beds including the same fossils are of the same age**

**Woodcock & Strachan (2012)**

# **Introduction to stratigraphy Summary**

**Preserved stratigraphy records key events in Earth history: tectonic events, changing sedimentary environments, biological evolution, climate change.**

**Stratigraphic methods and the standard geological time scale provide a temporal framework for these events. They aim to determine geological time.**

**Stratigraphic methods provide relative time (e.g. through lithostratigraphy) or absolute time (e.g. through radiometric dating in geochronology).**

**Use of stratigraphic methods depends on the materials available and the time scale considered. A combination and integration of various techniques works best.**
