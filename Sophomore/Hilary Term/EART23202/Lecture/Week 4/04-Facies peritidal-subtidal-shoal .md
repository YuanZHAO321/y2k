## **Summary**

**Peritidal carbonates form in depositional environments around the tidal range: from shallow subtidal to supratidal.**

**Peritidal carbonates have characteristic sedimentary structures: algal mats, structures indicating partial exposure, and early diagenetic modification (roots, precipitation of dolomite and sulphate evaporites). Structures vary depending on climatic regime (humid vs. arid).**

**Protected subtidal areas include lagoons and open shelves.**

**Typified by low-energy deposition (but storm deposits possible) and mudstones to packstones, float/rudstones; high organic productivity, bioturbation; faunal content depends on connection to open ocean.**

**Carbonate sand shoals occur in high-energy areas in the shallow subtidal: near the shelf edge, in the shoreface, and interior areas of strong currents. Shoals tend to be elongate structures analogous to clastic sand waves; include bedforms characteristic of strong currents. Reading: Tucker (1990), p. 81-85, 93-100, 101-137, 137-164**
