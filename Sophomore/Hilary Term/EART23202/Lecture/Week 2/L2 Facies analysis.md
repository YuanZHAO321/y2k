### Facies

- Facies: a body of sediment or rock characterised by a particular combination of lithological, physical and biological characteristics that distinguishes it from units above, below or adjacent to it.

- Grain type and composition

- Texture (grain size, sorting, roundness)

- Sedimentary structures

- Colour

- Biological structures and content (traces fossils, body fossils)

## Slide 7

### Facies and problems

- Facies are  defined based on observations

- It is commonly suggested that a single facies type is characteristic of a depositional environment BUT facies often non-unique

- It is therefore more logical to tentatively assign processes to individual facies

- Bear in mind that similar facies may be created by different processes!

- Cross Section of Point Bar. At any point in time in the natural history of a meandering stream system, each of the environments in a point bar sequence is present, including channel lag, fining upwards sand accretions, and ridge and swale topography on the surface. As the channel migrates laterally, the sequence accumulates and is eventually buried beneath overbank material such as natural levee sediments. (From Davis 1983, p. 256)

## Slide 26

### Stratigraphy and Walther’s Law

- ‘The various deposits of the same (environmental) area and, similarly, the sum of the rocks of different environmental areas were formed beside each other in time and space, but in crustal (i.e. vertical) profile we can see them lying on top of each other….it is a basic statement of far-reaching significance that only those environmental areas can be superimposed that are observed side-by-side at the present time’

- Walther’s Law 1894.

## Slide 27

- Walther’s Law: Applied to progradation of a river delta

- The vertical succession records the

- outbuilding of (in this case) a river

- delta, over time, past a fixed point

- in space. Therefore the beds above

- each other represent depositional

- areas that were laterally adjacent
