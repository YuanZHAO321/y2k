## Course unit overview

This unit comprises two residential field courses run over a two-week period at Easter in Scotland. One is a 6-day (excluding travel) field mapping trip that consolidates and develops the mapping skills obtained in EART28101 Introduction to Geological Mapping by applying them in a refolded, low to medium-grade metamorphic terrain. The focus is upon producing a map and accurately constructed cross-section that as a package contains not only bedding/younging data but also observations of cleavages, structural lineations, and minor fold geometry. The format of the mapping trip is a mix of staff-led field days, and days when the students map independently.

The other field trip is a 5-day (excluding travel) excursion that focuses on methods of acquiring and analysing sedimentary and igneous/contact metamorphic field data through a series of part-day or day-long exercises. In delivering the material on this trip, attention is also paid to matters that may be significant in an Earth Resources context. The focus is upon collecting and recording observations on measured sections or carefully drawn diagrams that are chosen to facilitate and communicate a process-based interpretation of those observations. All exercises are staff-led in the sense that the exercises are set up and explained by staff, and staff are present throughout, but the students apply the techniques/collect their data independently.

On both field trips, field days are typically from 9am to 5pm, and students are expected to work on their field data in the evenings

## Aims

The aim of this unit is to develop a range of geological field data collection techniques that have been previously introduced at elementary level on the 1st year Earth & Planetary Sciences field courses and in EART28101 Field Course: Introduction to Geological Mapping. The unit comprises two back-to-back field courses, one covering geological mapping techniques in a structurally complex sequence of metasedimentary rocks, and the other covering sedimentary and igneous field data collection. The primary emphasis is upon making observations that can be recorded on a maps, measured sections, or field-drawn diagrams, and which can be used to reconstruct the sedimentary, igneous, metamorphic and structural context and events that account for those observations. The unit is a key element of preparation for EART37000 Independent Project in Geological Field Mapping but is also highly useful for those students who have a significant field element to their 3rd year project work (which is a possibility in EART35000 Independent Project in Energy and Resources, EART36000 Independent Project in Geochemistry, and EART31100 Independent Project in Planetary Science)

## Learning outcomes

On the successful completion of the course, students will be able to:

Mapping field trip:

measure fold axes, fold axial planes, cleavages, and cleavage/bedding intersections belonging to different deformation events, and use the asymmetry of minor structures to establish where they are on larger scale folds

produce a geological map of a tectonically deformed region that shows key structural data, how mapping unit contacts have been affected by the deformation, and the locations of fold axial traces, faults and igneous intrusions

construct a profile plane cross section, showing the data used to construct it, to illustrate the geometry of the folding within a mapping area

Sedimentary/Igneous field trip:

construct measured sections in sedimentary successions at a variety of scales

subdivide sedimentary sequences into facies based on a consideration of the types of processes responsible for the sedimentary structures, contact relations, and bedforms observed

assemble facies into facies associations as a basis for inferring depositional environments

apply appropriate terminology when describing the mineralogical and textural features of mappable units of igneous and metamorphic rocks in the field

deduce the first order volcanic and/or magmatic ascent and emplacement processes and histories of igneous rocks based on field relationships and textural observations

## Teaching and learning methods

Teaching within this unit uses a range of methods including staff-led traverses in the mapping areas, independent mapping, and one-on-one interactions with staff to discuss progress and data collection strategies. Each field trip has a bespoke field handbook (hard copy) that describes the geological context of the field area, and provides detailed notes (with examples) on making, presenting and interpreting the types of field observations that are the focus of the trip. The handbooks and supporting information about the field areas are also uploaded to Blackboard

On each field trip students are strongly encouraged to obtain feedback on their progress during the field trip by showing their work to staff several times. Both field trips are residential and staff are accommodated on the same site as the students to facilitate this. All assessments are submitted on the field trip. Post-field course written feedback is provided, and can be augmented (on arrangement) by one-on-one discussion with staff

Successful field geologists are able to collect observations and think strategically about what they doing for themselves. Hence much of the teaching strategy within this unit is designed to get students to the point where they can be self-reliant in the field
