Most magmas undergo cooling and crystallization within the Earth’s crust, in magma chambers, sills and/or dykes. Some magmas are transported to the Earth’s surface where they feed volcanic eruptions.

- Fractional crystallization produces igneous rocks with different chemical compositions and mineral assemblages.

- The rate of crystallization produces different igneous textures.

The ultimate source of most of Earth’s magma is **partial melting of the silicate mantle.**

In some cases it is possible to produce magma by partial melting of crustal rocks.

##### Mantle xenoliths are brought to the surface by kimberlite magmas

Kimberlite magmas form deep in the mantle. They ascend very rapidly at rates of 10-40 m/s. They drill out xenoliths of dense mantle rock and transport them to the surface.

Mantle xenoliths from kimberlites are an economically important source of diamonds

Magma compositions related to one another by fractional crystallization through a **liquid line of descent (LLD)**

Reminder: changes in gradient on the LLD indicate changes to the crystallizing mineral assemblage

**Primary** melts are derived from the mantle. They have very high MgO contents and have undergone no (or very little) fractional crystallization.

**Primitive** melts have high MgO contents, but are not necessarily primary – they may have undergone some fractional crystallization
