### **Question 2 (20 marks)**

- a) Write one or two sentences to define each of the following terms:

   - i) Primary melt

- [2]

A basaltic melt with high MgO, generated by partial melting of mantle peridotite, that has not undergone any crystallization.

- ii) Equilibrium crystallization [2]

Crystallization where the crystals and melt remain within the system (i.e., no melt or crystals are removed from the system at any point) and where the crystals and melt are always in chemical equilibrium.

- iii) Mantle potential temperature [2]

The temperature that the mantle would have at the Earth’s surface, if it ascended along an adiabat (i.e., following the convective geotherm) without melting.

- b) What geochemical information would you use to tell the difference between mid-ocean ridge basalts and the basalts being erupted at Loihi seamount, a pre-shield stage submarine volcano in Hawaii? Your answer should refer to at least two different types of geochemical data. [8]

Mid-ocean ridge basalts (MORB) are tholeiitic. Basalts being erupted at Loihi and other preshield stage ocean islands (OIB) are alkali basalts.

1. Use TAS plot to distinguish between tholeiitic and alkali basalts. This requires major element compositions, specifically the SiO2, Na2O and K2O contents. For a given SiO2, content, the MORB tholeiitic basalts will have lower total alkali contents (Na2O+K2O) than Loihi alkali basalts.

2. Use trace elements. On a spider plot showing incompatible trace elements in order of incompatibility on the x axis, and normalized element concentration on the y axis, MORBs tend to have a slightly positive slope, whereas OIBs tend to have a slightly negative slope. The alkali basalts will be produced by a lower fraction of partial melting than the MORBs, so would expect the concentrations of incompatible trace elements to be higher in samples from Loihi than in typical MORB.

3. Isotopic compositions. MORB would be expected to have lower<sup>87</sup> Sr/<sup>86</sup> Sr and higher 143Nd/144Nd than alkali basalts from Loihi. This is because MORB samples the depleted upper mantle, whereas ocean islands also sample a component of the primitive lower mantle.

- c) Predict the igneous textures that might be observed if the magmas being generated at Loihi cooled and crystallized in a large magma chamber instead of erupting to the surface. Your answer should include a labelled sketch. [6]

An alkali basalt magma cooling and crystallizing in a large magma chamber would produce a gabbro. The essential minerals in an alkali gabbro would be plagioclase and clinopyroxene; type minerals are olivine and possibly some nepheline; might expect accessory oxides like magnetite.

The expected texture would be a coarse-grained cumulate. Early-crystallizing olivine is dense, so might sink to the floor of the chamber to form an olivine-rich layer. Alternatively, plagioclase might be the cumulus phase. Clinopyroxene could be a cumulus or intercumulus phase. If the cooling rate is sufficiently slow, the cpx might show exsolution lamellae.

The mineral assemblage, and composition of the minerals, will depend on height within the cooling intrusion: would expect higher modal proportions of mafic minerals at the base of the intrusion, and higher proportions of plagioclase towards the sandwich horizon.

An appropriate sketch should show a coarse-grained gabbro containing plagioclase + cpx (with or without other minerals). Any of cumulate/interstitial/poikilitic/ophitic texture would be appropriate; labels should be consistent with the sketch.

- a) A granite contains large megacrysts of alkali feldspar that are much larger than crystals of the other minerals in the rock. Suggest TWO possible ways in which this texture could form. For each case, explain what information (if any) the texture would provide about the order of crystallization.          [10]

   1. The megacrysts could be the intrusive equivalent of phenocrysts: they grew during an initial stage of very slow cooling in the magma. If this is the case, then megacrysts would tell us that alkali feldspar was the first mineral to begin crystallizing from the melt.

   2. The megacrysts could reflect differences in crystal growth rates between alkali feldspar and the other minerals. Alkali feldspar is hard to nucleate but easy to grow, so it tends to form few but very large crystals. Other minerals are easier to nucleate but grow more slowly, so tend to form more but smaller crystals. If this is the case, then the crystal size would not tell us about the order of crystallization and other textural evidence would be needed to interpret the order of crystallization.

- b) The alkali feldspars in Figure 3 show a characteristic intracrystalline texture. Give the name of this texture and briefly outline why it forms. Explain what information this texture provides about the cooling rate of the magma. [8]

Perthitic texture. It is an exsolution texture, caused when the alkali feldspar solid solution begins to separate into Na-rich and K-rich regions. This texture can only form under very slow cooling rates. If the cooling rate is fast, then no lamellae will form. The thickness of the exsolution lamellae is proportional to the cooling rate: the thicker the lamellae, the slower the cooling.

- c) The major constituent minerals of a granite outcrop are quartz, alkali feldspar, plagioclase, and biotite. Based on this mineralogy, evaluate the likely origin of the magma, and suggest a tectonic setting in which this granite could have formed. Give reasons for your answer. [12]

This mineral assemblage is characteristic of I-type granite, meaning it is most likely derived from the remelting of igneous crustal rocks. Metallunimous I-type magmas are capable of crystallizing hornblende and biotite, which is consistent with the observed mineral assemblage. This sample can’t be derived from melting meta-sedimentary crustal rocks, because this would produce a peraluminous (Al-rich) magma that would crystallize muscovite and other Al-rich minerals, which are not observed in this sample.

A likely tectonic setting is a continental margin such as the west coast of the USA. H2O release from the downgoing slab induces flux melting of peridotite in the mantle wedge, producing basalt which crystallizes at the base of the crust or intrudes into the crust. Partial melting of these originally intruded mafic magmas then produces the I-type magmas which can form large granite batholiths or erupt explosively at the arc volcanoes.

- c) The San Francisco Volcanic Field is situated in a rift zone where lithospheric extension is occurring. What is the most likely mechanism of mantle melting that explains the magmatism in this location? Explain the process by which melting occurs, and state the tectonic setting that is most often associated with this mechanism of melting. [12]

The most likely melting mechanism is adiabatic decompression melting. This happens when the lithosphere is thinned, allowing the convecting asthenospheric geotherm to extend closer to the Earth’s surface and potentially intersect the geotherm. This process most commonly occurs at mid-ocean ridges, where oceanic lithosphere is effectively thinned to zero due to plate separation. In a continental setting like the San Francisco Volcanic Field, the process of rifting serves to thin the lithosphere but doesn’t reduce its thickness to zero (so we don’t observe wholesale plate spreading). A first-class answer might comment that in this continental setting where rifting is only partial, the extent of partial melting may be quite small compared to a mid-ocean ridge.

- d) The SP Crater lava flow contains olivine phenocrysts that show normal compositional zoning with high-forsterite cores and less forsteritic rims. Briefly explain the magma chamber process that might produce this zoning. [8]

Phenocrysts grow during slow cooling in the magma chamber. As crystallisation of olivine proceeds, the melt MgO content decreases and the composition of olivine in equilibrium with the melt becomes less forsteritic – this is governed by the olivine phase diagram. The normal zoning reflects the process of crystallization: the forsteritic olivine cores must have grown first, from a more primitive melt, and the more evolved olivine rim grew later. This is not fractional crystallization (otherwise all the phenocrysts would be removed as soon as they formed) but nor is it perfect equilibrium crystallization, because under true equilibrium conditions the whole crystal should be in chemical equilibrium with the melt.

A very good answer might comment that the forsteritic olivine cores could have grown in a different magma and been stirred into the SP Crater magma just before eruption. That is, the cores were carried into the magma chamber by a mafic recharge magma, and the rims grew later and are in equilibrium with the SP Crater melt composition.
