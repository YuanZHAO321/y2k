#### Age progression and lifecycle of Hawaiian volcanoes is due to plate motion over the plume

The substantial compositional differences between magmas erupted from volcanoes of differing age leads to the idea that there is a distinct life-cycle to Hawaiian volcanism.

The life-cycle of each volcano tracks the motion of the lithosphere over the plume stem.

margaret.hartley@manchester.ac.uk

EART 22201

#### What controls the primary melt composition being produced?

Primary melt composition (X) varies as a function of temperature (T), pressure (P) and the extent of partial melting (F)

Directly above the plume stem: Tholeiitic basalts

- Highest mantle temperature

- Higher degree of partial melting, F

- Melting occurs over a wide interval of pressures

- High volume of primary melt produced

At the edges of the plume head: Alkali basalts

- Lower mantle temperature

- Lower degree of partial melting, F

- Melting is confined to relatively higher pressures

- Small volume of primary melt produced

margaret.hartley@manchester.ac.uk

EART 22201

#### Summary: Time-progressive variation in melt production for a Hawaiian volcano

###### Pacific plate moves over the Hawaiian plume.

1. The first melts are produced when the edge of the plume starts to impinge on the lithosphere.

   - Mantle temperature begins to increase

   - Low degrees of partial melting, at high pressure, produces alkali basalts.

2. Plate motion brings the volcano over the plume centre. Mantle temperature is at its highest.

   - Higher degrees of partial melting, over a range of pressures, produces tholeiitic basalts

3. Plate motion moves the volcano over the plume tail.

   - Mantle temperature decreases

   - Low degrees of partial melting, at higher pressures, produces alkali basalts.

   - Some small-fraction melts do not erupt, but instead solidify as intrusions in the lithosphere

4. Plate motion moves the volcano away from the hotspot.

   - Return to ambient mantle temperature. Mantle melting stops.

5. Mass removal by erosion of the volcanic edifice. Intrusions that were previously frozen in the lithosphere may be re-melted by adiabatic decompression melting.

   - Very low degrees of melting produce strongly silica-undersaturated alkaline magmas with unusual compositions

#### REE partition coefficients in minerals

The rare earth elements (REEs) exhibit very coherent partitioning behaviour in silicate minerals. This makes them very powerful geochemical tracers.

**LREEs** are incompatible in most silicate minerals.

**HREEs** are highly compatible in **garnet** .

- Therefore, partial melts of garnet peridotite mantle are depleted in HREE.

Eu<sup>2+</sup> is more compatible in plagioclase than any other REE.

- If plagioclase has crystallized from a melt, then the residual liquid will have a negative Eu anomaly.

#### Different mantle sources can explain the compositional differences between MORB and OIB

###### **MORB tholeiites:**

- Positive gradient

- Depleted in incompatible trace elements

#### Large igneous provinces (LIPs)

- LIPs are voluminous outpourings of magma erupted over a relatively short period of geological time (~1 Ma or less)

- • The formation of many LIPs is attributed to mantle plumes

- Some LIPs are associated with continental break-up (e.g. CAMP, Parana-Etendeka)

- LIPs erupted through continental lithosphere are often referred to as continental flood basalts (CFB)

PDD = Pripyat-Dneipr-Donets CAMP = Central Atlantic Magmatic Province
