Magma compositions related to one another by fractional crystallization through a **liquid line of descent (LLD)**

**Primary** melts are derived from the mantle. They have very high MgO contents and have undergone no (or very little) fractional crystallization.

Garnet peridotite melting at 2 GPa 1450 °C: 1% melting alkali basalt 1500 °C: 16% melting tholeiitic basalt Spinel peridotite melting at 1 GPa 1350 °C: 15% melting alkali basalt 1450 °C: 20-30% melting tholeiitic basalt

#### Summary: MORB compositions and crystallization

###### **What are the expected characteristics of a primary melt?**

- It is the product of partial melting of mantle peridotite (ol + opx + cpx + an aluminous phase)

- Melt composition should have high and predictable Mg/Fe ratios that are in chemical equilibrium with mantle olivine

###### **What are the expected characteristics of a fractionated melt?**

- It has undergone some degree of fractional crystallization

- Melt composition should have low Mg/Fe ratios that are not in chemical equilibrium with mantle olivine

Typical mid-ocean ridge basalts have low Mg/Fe ratios that are not in chemical equilibrium with mantle olivine. This is characteristic of fractionated melts. Therefore, MORBs underwent fractional crystallization before they erupted.

In basalts, the order of crystallization depends on both the melt composition and the pressure of crystallization. In almost all MORBs, olivine crystallizes before plagioclase, which is an indicator that MORB crystallization occurs at low pressure.
