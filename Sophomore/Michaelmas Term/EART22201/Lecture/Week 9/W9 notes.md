#### “Magmatic sedimentation” does not occur in silicic magmas

1. Large, euhedral crystals grow free-floating in the magma

2. High-silica magma is too viscous for crystal settling to occur

**No crystal accumulation at the floor of the chamber**

3. The rest of the magma solidifies **Early-formed euhedral crystals randomly distributed in the rock**

Granites do not form cumulate textures

#### Formation of I-type granitic magmas at continental margins

Evidence for fractional crystallization:

Zoned plutons represent stages of fractionation. Early intrusions are tonalites; later intrusions are granites.

###### Stages of forming an I-type granitic magma:

1. Partial melting of wet peridotite in the mantle wedge produces mafic magmas

2. Mafic magmas crystallize in the lower crust: gabbros and diorites

3. Later intrusions of hot mafic magma cause partial melting of the igneous lower crustal rocks, producing intermediate and evolved magmas

4. Intermediate/evolved magmas ascend to upper crust and form batholiths.

Evidence for melting of continental crust:

- Fractional crystallization creates tonalites to granites

87Sr/86Sr increases with distance from the magmatic arc - indicates greater assimilation of continental crust

- Variable assimilation of continental crust modifies the isotopic signature

Sierra Nevada batholith hosts gold lode deposits:

- Gold is found within quartz veins, faults, and shear zones

- Hot, mineral-saturated fluids mobilized gold from elsewhere in the crust and concentrated it into economically viable deposits

- Granitic magmas supply the hot hydrothermal fluids
