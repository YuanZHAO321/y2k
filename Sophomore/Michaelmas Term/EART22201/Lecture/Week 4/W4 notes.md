#### Nucleation: the formation of a stable new crystal and the start of crystallization

<!-- Start of picture text -->
not stable<br>ΔG stable<br>s<br>ΔG*<br><!-- End of picture text -->

The total Gibbs free energy ΔGT of a crystal is the sum of its surface energy ΔGs and its volume energy ΔGV.

The total free energy increases until the critical radius rc is reached, after which further growth causes ΔGT to decrease.

<!-- Start of picture text -->
r radius, r<br>c<br>ΔG ΔG<br>V T<br><!-- End of picture text -->

A crystal is stable once it has reached the critical radius. ΔG* is the activation energy required to form a stable crystal nucleus.

Crystals smaller than the critical radius may continue to grow, or may be resorbed back into the melt.

#### Porphyritic texture

#### Glomeroporphyritic texture

phenocrysts clustered into aggregates

Phenocrysts grew first, slowly, in a magma chamber. Larger phenocrysts had longer to grow than smaller phenocrysts. Groundmass crystals grew last, very quickly, when the magma was erupted to the surface.

#### Poikilitic texture

<!-- Start of picture text -->
olv<br>opx<br><!-- End of picture text -->

Many crystals of mineral A are fully enclosed by one large crystal of mineral B.

Mineral A = chadacrysts (means “contained crystals”) Mineral B = oikocryst (means “house crystal”)

Order of crystallization:

1. Mineral A

2. Mineral B

#### Two ways that ophitic textures can form

(a) Plag and cpx nucleate at the same time, but cpx grows faster and encloses the slowgrowing plagioclases.

Order of crystallization: 1. Plag + cpx

(b) Plag nucleates first. Cpx nucleates second. Cpx grows faster and encloses the slowgrowing plagioclases. Order of crystallization: 1. Plag

2. Plag + cpx

#### Seriate texture: gradual and continuous range in crystal size

Porphyritic: phenocrysts + groundmass

Porphyritic: phenocrysts + microphenocrysts +

groundmass

Order of crystallization: Largest phenocrysts likely to have grown first. Groundmass crystals grew last.

Interpretation: Multiple episodes of nucleation as the magma ascends through stacked magma chambers in the crust.
