# **Practical 3: An introduction to the rock-forming minerals**

**On successful completion of this practical, you should be able to:**

- Identify common rock-forming minerals in hand specimen and thin section

- Describe rock-forming minerals in plane-polarized and cross-polarized light

- Describe the mineralogical and optical properties of common igneous minerals, including: relief, colour, pleochroism, cleavage, twinning, extinction angle.

- Create accurate sketches of important igneous textures and mineralogical features

- Interpret the order of crystallization using observations of igneous textures

# _81108X contains plagioclase and quartz in addition to alkali feldspar_

The alkali feldspar in this rock has a **perthitic** texture, which is created when the alkali feldspar solid solution exsolves (separates out) into sodium-rich and potassium-rich lamellae within the mineral. In this thin section, the exsolution lamellae are well-developed. Check that you can identify this texture and make a labelled sketch of what you observe. What kind of alkali feldspar is this: sanidine, orthoclase or microcline? What does the internal texture tell you about the cooling rate of this igneous rock?

This is orthoclase: it has simple twinning and perthitic exsolution. All the crystals have distinctive blebs of Narich albite, and some of these blebs have lamellar twins. The exsolved lamellae are quite coarse, which suggests that the rock must have cooled slowly. But the cooling rate was not slow enough to cause Al:Si ordering in the alkali feldspar – otherwise we would have microcline.

_In 81108X some of the feldspars show concentric zonation. The plagioclases are altered to sericite, and the alkali feldspars have perthitic exsolution._

Examine the relationship between the hornblende and the aegirine-augite. Some of the hornblende forms rims around the aegirine crystals. Why might these two minerals have this textural relationship (ask a demonstrator)?

The hornblende must have crystallized later than the augite. Pyroxenes are dry, whereas amphiboles can take water into their crystal lattices. Progressive crystallization of anhydrous minerals caused the remaining silicate liquid to become more hydrous, allowing hornblende to start crystallizing. Hornblende has a very similar crystallographic structure to pyroxenes, so it uses the clinopyroxene as a convenient nucleation template.

Olivines are enclosed by large anhedral crystals of plagioclase and/or augite (cpx). This is a **poikilitic** texture. Some hornblendes contain small inclusions of the mineral **apatite** , a phosphate mineral. Apatite has a hexagonal prismatic habit. In PPL it is colourless and has high relief. In XPL, it has low first-order interference colours. Make sure you can identify the apatite.

Make notes on the optical characteristics of the biotite: pleochroism, cleavage, appearance close to extinction. Notice the small, dark circles inside the biotite crystals. These are called **pleochroic haloes** . Ask a demonstrator how the pleochroic haloes are formed.

Biotite has two pleochroic colours: brown and straw yellow. It has one perfect cleavage. It has straight extinction, and a speckled appearance when close to extinction. The interference colour is high third-order. The pleochroic haloes form around tiny inclusions of the mineral **zircon** (the zircons themselves are mostly too small to see). Zircon can accommodate a tiny amount of uranium in its crystal structure. When the uranium undergoes radioactive decay, it emits alpha particles that cause radiation damage to the surrounding biotite lattice. This creates the pleochroic haloes.

Some of the biotite, muscovite and quartz crystals have undulose extinction. Quartz is often present as small interlocking grains, which is evidence of recrystallization. What process could have caused these features?

Undulose extinction and recrystallization are evidence that this rock has undergone some minor deformation.

The plagioclase crystals often have a dusty appearance: they have been hydrothermally altered to a clay mineral called **sericite** . What other features do you observe in the plagioclase? Make a labelled sketch to illustrate them.

Plagioclase has multiple lamellar twins and, in this thin section, it is compositionally zoned. In this section, the sericite is usually confined to the crystal cores.

# <u>DWR10</u>

This rock contains an opaque oxide. This is **spinel** . What are the two silicate minerals?

The silicate minerals are plagioclase and orthopyroxene. Opx is identified by its low first-order interference colours, and straight extinction in elongate crystals and one-cleavage sections. Some of the opx crytals have brightly birefringent exsolution lamellae of clinopyroxene. There are also some small clinopyroxene crystals in some of the thin sections.

What can you learn about the order of crystallization by looking at the texture of this rock?

The texture is poikilitic: opx and spinel chadacrysts are enclosed in large oikocrysts of plagioclase. Some of the spinels are enclosed within larger opx crystals. These textural relationships suggest that spinel was the first mineral to start crystallizing; then spinel and opx crystallized simultaneously. Plagioclase must have started crystallizing later, because it fills up the interstitial spaces between the other minerals.
