# **Practical 2: Optics and mineralogy**

**On successful completion of this practical, you should be able to:**

- Describe the relief of a mineral

- Determine whether a mineral is isotropic or anisotropic from its optical properties

- Identify the pleochroic scheme of a mineral

- Identify and describe different types of extinction in anisotropic minerals

- Measure extinction angles

- Estimate birefringence using the Michel-Levy chart

- Predict the optical properties of anisotropic minerals from their lattice parameters and optical symmetry

There are three parts to this practical. Each part should take you about 40 minutes. You can complete them in any order.

## **How to systematically examine a thin section and make observations.**

1. Start by looking at the whole thin section. Start by holding the thin section up to the light to get an overview of the whole section. Make a note of any distinctive features you can see, such as large crystals, distinctive colours, or a fabric.

2. Place the thin section on the microscope stage. Select the lowest available magnification.

3. Look over the whole thin section in PPL. Make a note of any distinctive features you, and any minerals you can identify. Pay particular attention to relief, colour, and cleavage. Be sure to rotate the stage as you proceed, and note any changes in the minerals’ appearance.

4. Look over the whole thin section in XPL. Make a note of any distinctive features, and any minerals you can identify. Pay particular attention to interference colours, extinction angles, and twinning.

5. Use the objective lenses to zoom in on any parts of the thin section that are particularly finegrained, or that you think are particularly interesting.

## **Part 1 (40 minutes): Thin section 16722, 15,** **_or_** **<u>986</u>**

These rocks are **blueschists** . They are metamorphic rocks, formed by high-pressure, low-temperature metamorphism of mafic igneous rocks – but minerals in them are excellent for exploring the optical properties of minerals.

Examine the thin section and find examples of a mineral that is isotropic in cross-polarized light. This is garnet.

- i) What kind of indicatrix does garnet have? Why must this be the case?

It must be an isotropic (spherical) indicatrix. This is the only type of indicatrix with the same refractive index in all directions, and it is the only one that fits with the constraint that the mineral must be in extinction at all times in XPL.

- ii) Describe the properties of garnet in plane-polarized light.

The garnets in these thin sections tend to have a pale pink colour, and they are not pleochroic. Garnet has high relief.

Now find the brightly coloured pleochroic mineral. This is a sodic amphibole called glaucophane. Examine this mineral carefully in PPL and XPL. Make sure you look at different crystals in different parts of the thin section.

- iv) What are the three pleochroic colours of glaucophane? From your observations, can you predict the shape of the indicatrix (isotropic, uniaxial, biaxial) in glaucophane?

Blue, lilac, and pale yellow (or colourless). You should be able to find all three colours, although you don’t have enough information to work out which is , which is  and which is .

Three pleochroic colours requires three different refractive indices, so glaucophane must have a biaxial indicatrix.

Glaucophane has a prismatic to acicular habit with crystals elongated parallel to the crystallographic z axis. You should be able to find three distinctive sections through the crystal: (a) basal sections with two cleavages (extremely rare in thin section 986); (b) elongate sections with one cleavage and inclined extinction; (c) elongate sections with one cleavage and straight extinction.

## **Part 2 (40 minutes): Thin section DS** _Dippin Sill, Isle of Arran, Scotland_

This is an **olivine dolerite** (or olivine gabbro). There has been some alteration along the fractures in the olivine in this sample, either to an orange-brown or to a green material. Examine this mineral carefully in PPL and XPL. Make sure that you look at several different crystals in different parts of the thin section.

- i) Briefly describe the features of the olivine in PPL. You should include information on the relief, colour, pleochroism (or lack of pleochroism), cleavage, and anything else of interest.

Olivine is colourless with no pleochroism; has quite high relief. Curving fractures are common, and some crystals have faint cleavage traces. Some crystals show evidence of hydrothermal alteration along the fractures and around the crystal edges.

- ii) Briefly describe the features of the olivine in XPL. You should include information on the birefringence, type(s) of extinction, and twinning (or lack of twinning).

Olivine has bright second- to third-order interference colours. It does not have twinning. It has straight extinction with respect to the crystal edges, but this can only be seen in crystals with well-developed faces.

- iii) What is the highest interference colour that you can find in olivine? What value of birefringence does this indicate? _You will need to use the Michel-Levy chart._

The highest interference colour should be around third-order blue to green, which corresponds to a birefringence of about 0.038 to 0.040. Your answer will depend on the highest interference colour you identified.

The mineral that is reddish brown in PPL is a pyroxene. Examine this mineral carefully in PPL and XPL. Make sure that you look at several different crystals in different parts of the thin section.

- iv) Describe the features of the pyroxene in PPL and XPL.

In PPL, this pyroxene has a reddish brown colour, and there is some faint zoning visible. You may observe some faint pleochroism. It has two cleavages at 90°, although most sections show only one of the cleavage traces. It has moderate to high relief.

In XPL, this pyroxene has first to second-order interference colours – pink-orange and blue-turquoise colours are common. Some of the one-cleavage sections have straight extinction, but others have inclined extinction. Some crystals have twin planes, although this is not a common feature so you may not have seen it.

- v) Find a pyroxene crystal with inclined extinction, and measure the extinction angle.

The maximum extinction angle is around 40°.

- vi) Is this orthopyroxene (orthorhombic) or clinopyroxene (monoclinic)? Use your observations to support your answer.

It must be clinopyroxene. Orthorhombic minerals can only show straight extinction. Monoclinic crystals can show straight or inclined extinction, depending on whether or not the section contains the y axis.

Notice the mineral that is opaque in PPL. This is an oxide mineral, probably magnetite. Minerals that appear black in PPL are referred to as opaque. Be careful not to confuse opaque minerals with those that only appear black (extinct) in XPL, which are referred to as isotropic.

vii) What is the low-relief mineral? Briefly describe its key features in PPL and XPL.

Plagioclase. In PPL it is colourless and low relief. In XPL it has low first-order interference colours up to white, and multiple lamellar twins that go extinct in different orientations.

viii) If you have time, estimate the modal mineralogy of this rock.

A reasonable estimate would be ~15% olivine, ~35% clinopyroxene, ~40% plagioclase, ~10% opaque oxide. Estimating modal proportions by eye is challenging, so your numbers may be slightly different.
