Here are some examples with answers of exam questions requiring short answers and/ or part calculation. There will also be essay-type questions.

Question 1

a) Write the reaction for gypsum dissolution in water (10% marks)

b) The Ksp at standard temperature and pressure in water is 2.51 x 10-5 mol/L . What does Ksp mean? (5% marks)

c) Write the equation for the Ksp of gypsum? (5% marks)

d) Assuming the solution is ideal (dilute) with Ca2+ = SO42- = 1 calculate the solubility of gypsum in water. (25% marks)

e) Given that Ganges river water contains [Ca2+] = 0.634 x 10-3 m and [SO42-] = 0 088 10-3 m, calculate the Ion Activity Product (IAP) and use it to assess the saturation level of gypsum in this river. (25% marks)

f) Barium sulphate (BaSO4) is 500 times less soluble in water saturated in CaSO4 compared to pure water alone. Account for this change in solubility. (30% marks)

(a) Ca(SO4) 2H2O ⇋ Ca2+ + SO42- + 2H2O

(b) Ksp is the solubility product

(c) Ksp = [Ca2+][SO42-] = 2.51 x 10-5

(d) For y moles each of the constituent ions

Ksp = y x y = y2 = sqrt(2.51 x 10-5) = 5.01 x 10-3 mol/L gypsum

(e) IAP = [0.643 x 10-3] [0.088 x 10-3] = 56.58 x 10-9 IAP << Ksp so undersaturated wrt to gypsum.

(f) If a solution saturated in barium sulphate comes in to contact with gypsum, then the calcium sulphate can dissolve and therefore increase the sulphate concentration. This means the solution becomes supersaturated in barium sulphate and barite will begin to precipitate and “replace” the gypsum. This continues until the [Ca2+]/[Ba2+] ratio reaches equilibrium, then the replacement processes ceases. This process is known as the common ion effect, where the common ion (in this case sulphate anions) causes the solution to become supersaturated in the less soluble compound (in our example barium sulphate).

Question 3

(a) A stream emanating from an abandoned mine has a pH of 2. Assuming the stream water is well-mixed with air, calculate its pE and, using the pE/PH diagram for the Fe-H-O system provided, predict the oxidation state of iron dissolved in the water.

The appropriate half reaction to consider is:

¼ O2 + H+ + e− → ½H2O

Where pEo = +20.75; PO2 = 0.2 atm

[50%]

(b) For several kilometres downstream the stream bed is coated in a bright red-orange precipitate. What is the chemical nature of this deposit, how could it have been formed and why has it spread so far from the mine entrance.

[50%]

Outline Answers

(a) K = 1 / (PO2)1/4 (H+) (e-) = 1020.75

pE = pEO + 1/n log (red/oxid)

pE = 20.75 + log (PO2)1/4 (H+)

pE = 20.75 + 1/4 log 0.2 - pH

pE = 20.75 - 0.17 - 2 = 18.58

Iron will be present as dissolved Fe3+ ions

(b) The precipitate is mainly iron hydroxide Fe(OH)3 (+ some iron oxides) which is insoluble under higher pH conditions (>pH 3.5). Fe2+ more soluble than Fe3+. This suggests the stream is being slowly neutralised (increasing pH) possibly by dilution (e.g. tributaries, groundwater inflow and rain water), but also by reactions with river sediments or various minerals.

Question 4

a) For pH = 7.10 and pCO2 = 5.01 x 10-4 atm, determine the concentrations for all three aqueous carbonate species. KCO2 = 3.39 x 10-2, K1 = 4.47 x 10-7, K2 = 4.86 x 10-11. Include all the appropriate reactions and equations. (70% marks)

b) For the previous question, you determined that one of the aqueous carbonate species had a greater concentration than the other two. How did you know before you did the calculation that this particular species would have the greatest concentration of the three? (30% marks)

Outline answer

(a) CO2 + H2O ⇋ H2CO3

KCO2 = [H2CO3] / pCO2 = 3.39 x 10-2

[H2CO3] = (3.39 x 10-2)(5.01 x 10-4) = 1.70 x 10-5 mol

H2CO3 ⇋ HCO3- + H+

K1 = [H+][HCO3-] / [H2CO3] = 4.47 x 10-7

[HCO3-] = (4.47 x 10-7)( 1.70 x 10-5) / (10-7.10) = 9.55 x 10-5 mol

HCO3- ⇋ CO32- + H+

K2 = [H+][CO32-] / [HCO3-] = 4.86 x 10-11

[CO32-] = (4.86 x 10-11)(9.55 x 10-5) / (10-7.10) = 5.62 x 10-8 mol

(b) [HCO3-] would have the greatest concentration because the pH (7.10) fell between pK1 (6.35) and pK2 (10.33), within the pH range over which HCO3- predominates.

Question 5

(a) Write a balanced reaction for the oxidation of Fe2+ to amorphous iron hydroxide (Fe(OH)3) by O2, using half reactions. Show each step in the balancing process. (50% marks)

(b) For the half reaction MnO2(s) + 4H+ + 2e- ⇋ Mn2+ + 2H2O

Write the equation in terms of (i) K and (ii) pe (50% marks).

Outline answers

(a) Fe2+ + 3H2O ⇋ Fe(OH)3 + e- + 3H+

O2 + 4e- + 4 H+ ⇋ 2 H2O

Multiply first reaction by 4 to balance e-:

4Fe2+ + 12H2O ⇋ 4Fe(OH)3 + 4e- + 12H+

Add half reactions together:

4Fe2+ + O2 + 10H2O ⇋ 4Fe(OH)3 + 8H+

(b) K = [Mn2+]/[H+]4[e-]2

logK = log[Mn2+] – 4 pH + 2 pe

pe = 2 pH – 0.5 log[Mn2+] – 0.5 logK

(Note: for the reaction A = B + e- the relationship is K= [B]/[A][e-]

Taking logs and rearrangement then: logK = logA - logB - pH – pe)

Question 6

A stream containing dissolved manganese (Mn) has an Eh value of +0.80 Volts.

What is the ratio of Mn3+ to Mn2+ in the stream?

Note that the half-reaction Mn2+ = Mn3+ + e has an E0 of +1.51 Volts

Outline answer

The measured Eh of the stream is less oxidizing than the half-reaction, so we would expect most Mn to be in the form Mn2+.

The Nernst equation allows us to quantify this:

pE = pE0 – log[red]/[oxid]

pe = 16.9Eh

pE = 16.9 x 0.8 = 13.52

pE0 = 16.9 x 1.51 = 25.52

n = 1

13.52 = 25.52 - log([Mn3+]/[Mn2+])

-12 = - log([Mn3+]/[Mn2+])

[Mn3+]/[Mn2+] = 1 x 10-12
