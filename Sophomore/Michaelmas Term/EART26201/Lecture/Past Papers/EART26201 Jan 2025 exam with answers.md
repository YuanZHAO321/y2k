- With reference to the phase diagram below, describe and contrast equilibrium and fractional crystallisation of a liquid with the bulk composition XAn=0.6 						      [20 marks]

During equilibrium crystallisation, the first plagioclase crystals form when the system composition intersects the liquidus at ~1500°C and have a composition of XAn~0.9. As the system cools, both the liquid and plagioclase evolve to lower XAn contents. At ~1330°C the final liquid solidifies to XAn=0.6 plagioclase. Liquid and plagioclase remain in equilibrium throughout. Phase rule can be used to estimate phase proportions for top marks.

During fractional crystallisation, the first plagioclase crystals also form at ~1500°C and have a composition of XAn~0.9. However, these plagioclase crystals leave the system as soon as they are formed, such that the liquid composition evolved all the way down the liquidus to XAn=0 at ~1120°C, where the final liquid crystallises to form XAn=0 crystals.

Two key differences between equilibrium and fractional crystallisation are that the system fully solidifies at a higher temperature (1330°C) during equilibrium crystallisation that fractional crystallisation (~1120°C), and that equilibrium crystallisation cannot produce crystals with XAn contents below 0.6, while fractional crystallisation produces crystals with compositions all the way to XAn=0

Question 2

Detail how oxygen isotopes in different archives (e.g. foraminifera, ice cores, pore waters) can be used to reconstruct past ocean temperature. Include in your discussion a description of the processes that can cause uncertainty in the temperature determination and how some of these may be circumvented.

[25 marks]

Shown in the 1950’s (Epstein 1953) that fractionation of d18O between calcite and seawater related to temperature. Fractionation factor, , experimentally determined for calcite-water system. e.g. 103ln = 2.78(106T-2) – 2.89 (O’Neil 1969). Similar sort of relationship for aragonite. BUT: assemblages of dead organisms do not represent assemblages of living because a) selective dissolution of thin walled specimens; b) differential removal of easily transported specimens; and c) contamination of assemblages by transport from elsewhere.  Some marine sedimentary regions are thought to limit these effects. The oxygen isotope system must be corrected for the change in seawater caused by the ‘ice-volume’ effect. This can be done by sampling benthic forams – e.g. bottom waters that are at 4C – no reason to think they would be colder in ice ages – therefore only change in these species is due to ice-volume effect – gives calibration. Care must be taken with species selection because some produce carbonate not in equilibrium with water – called vital effect – choose species that are in full equilibrium with water or have constant/calibrated vital effect. Also must take care that the test habitat is constrained – ie planktic forams may have different depth habitats depending where and when they live – distorting surface temperature determination – factors include water density (T and salinity) life cycle, seasonality and salinity of seawater. Excellent archive is pore water because it traps ocean water directly although diffusive effects need to be considered.
