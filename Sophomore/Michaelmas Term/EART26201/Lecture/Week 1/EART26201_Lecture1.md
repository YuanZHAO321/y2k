### The geochemist’s periodic table

- Element abundances vary hugely within and between different planetary bodies

- Grouped by abundance

- Major elements: >1 wt.%

- O is Earth’s most abundant element and dominant anion

- Other elements are often expressed as oxides (MgO)

- Make rock-forming minerals

- Minor elements: 0.1–1 wt.%

- Trace elements: <0.1 wt.%

## Slide 4

### Chemical differentiation

- Separation of larger bodies into chemically distinct parts

- The solar system is differentiated:

- Terrestrial planets (Mg, Fe, Si, O)

- Jovian planets (H & He in gas giants; H, He, C & N in ice giants and comets)

- Earth is differentiated:

- Core (inner & outer)

- Mantle (asthenospheric & lithospheric)

- Crust (oceanic & continental)

- Atmosphere

- Trace elements track differentiation

## Slide 13

### Factors affecting trace element partitioning

- Ionic radius

- Avoid inducing lattice strain by introducing ions of different sizes

- Charge

- Avoid introducing highly charged ions

- Avoid introducing charge imbalance

- General rules of partitioning

- Large cations are typically incompatible

- Small divalent cations are often more compatible than small univalent cations

- Ionic radius more important than charge

## Slide 14

### Goldschmidt’s Rules for element substitution

- K+	1.33 Å

- Rb+	1.47 Å

- Mg2+	0.66 Å

- Ni2+	0.69 Å

- Ions of one element can extensively substitute for those of another if their radii differ by less than 15%

- If two ions have similar radii and the same charge, the smaller ion is preferentially incorporated into the solid

- If two ions have similar radii but different charges, the ion with the higher charge is preferentially incorporated into the solid

- Mg2+	0.66 Å

- Fe2+	0.74 Å

- Liquid:	    K+     	1.33 Å,	    Y3+	0.90 Å

- Solid:	    Ba2+  	1.47 Å,	    U4+	0.94 Å

## Slide 16

### Types of incompatible trace element

- Typical solid–liquid partitioning

- Three key groups for igneous petrology

- High field-strength elements (HFSE)

- Too highly charged to fit into crystal lattices

- Largely immobile in aqueous fluids

- Large-ion lithophile elements (LILE)

- Too large to fit into crystal lattices

- Highly mobile in aqueous fluids

- Rare earth elements (REE)

- La to Lu; very similar chemical properties

- Related behaviour during key geological processes like melting and crystallisation

- Equilibrium crystallisation

## Slide 28

### Composition of the core

- 16% of Earth’s volume; 31% of Earth’s mass

- The inner core is solid; the outer core is liquid (S waves; geodynamo)

- Fe and Ni dominate (~90 and ~5 wt.%); Fe/Ni of the core is similar to chondrites

- Density is 5–15% lower than pure alloy; light elements must be present (H, C, S, O or Si)

- Siderophile elements are depleted in the mantle but enriched in the core

- Chalcophile elements are enriched in the core because sulfide is siderophile

## Slide 29

### Composition of the mantle

- 83% of Earth’s volume; 68.3% of Earth’s mass

- Bulk Silicate Earth (BSE) ≈ CI chondrite − Core

- BSE sometimes referred to as Primitive Mantle

- O, Si, Mg and Fe dominate alongside Ca and Al

- Mantle composition determined from meteorites, xenoliths and ophiolites

## Slide 30

### Composition of the crust

- Insignificant contribution to Earth’s volume; 0.7% of Earth’s mass

- 99 wt.% of the crust is composed of O, Si, Al, Fe, Mg, Na and K

- Uniquely divided between oceanic crust and continental crust

- Oceanic crust: rich in Ca, Mg and Fe; covers 70% of Earth’s surface

- Continental crust: rich in Si, Na, K and incompatible elements; covers 30%

- Unique in the solar system

## Slide 32

### Continental crust formation

- Mean composition is andesitic; grades from basaltic at the base to granitic at the top

- 1 wt.% of BSE, but contains up to 50% of BSE’s incompatible elements, including radioactive elements like U, Th and K

- Depleted in HFSE, suggesting formation in subduction zones: HFSE are immobile in aqueous fluids released from subducting slabs that drive flux melting (unlike LILE)

- Removing the continental crust from BSE probably created the Depleted Mantle

## Slide 33

- Oceanic crust formation

- Mean composition is basaltic, but stratified from ultramafic (base) to basaltic (top)

- Slightly enriched in incompatible elements with respect to BSE

- Relatively depleted in highly incompatible elements; oceanic crust is mostly created by melting the Depleted Mantle

- Relatively enriched in compatible elements like Ni and Cr

- Contains sulfide deposits rich in chalcophile and technologically important elementslike Cu and the Platinum Group Metals
