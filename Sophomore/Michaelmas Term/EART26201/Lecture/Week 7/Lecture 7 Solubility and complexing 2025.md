# **Solubility and Complexing**

The solubility of minerals in aqueous solutions are essential for our understanding of fluid properties, mass transport and growth/dissolution processes of minerals in Earth’s crust and upper mantle.

Complexing is a key mechanism for mobilising ions (particularly metal ions) in solution.

## **Replacement minerals**

- If a solution saturated in barium sulphate comes in to contact with gypsum, then the calcium sulphate can dissolve and therefore increase the sulphate concentration.

- This means the solution becomes **_supersaturated_** in barium sulphate and barite will begin to precipitate and “replace” the gypsum.

- This continues until the [Ca<sup>2+</sup> ]/[Ba<sup>2+</sup> ] ratio reaches 250,000 required for equilibrium, then the replacement processes ceases.

- Replacement of one mineral with another is extremely common in nature and affects sulphates, carbonates and sulphides. Ore mineralogy.

- In each case introduction of a _common ion_ (in our example sulphate anions) causes the solution to become supersaturated in  the less soluble compound (in our example barium sulphate).

- The rule is that the _less soluble_ compound always replaces the _more soluble_ one - pseudomorphs

## **Ion Complexing**

Most fresh (meteoric) waters (stream, river, lake and groundwaters) waters are dilute (ionic strength, I<0.1) the ions are highly dissociated.

In concentrated natural waters like seawater (I=0.7) there is interaction between cations and anions causing the molecules to form groups or clusters, these are known as **_complex ions._** This is a very effective way to mobilise (particularly) metal ions.

## **Ion Complexing**

- **Ion pairs** - neutral molecules composed of two ions of opposite charge (e.g. CaCO3 and CaSO4) – weak bonding low stability.

- • **Coordination complexes** – composed of a central atom surrounded by anions or neutral species (ligands). E.g. Cu(H2O)62+

- **Chelates** – complexes between metal ions and dissolved anions or organic matter (ligands or chelating agent). Metal chelates are important for mobilising metals in soils and for the uptake of metals in plants.

## **Complex ions: Gold extraction**

- Complex ion formation is used to extract **gold** from low-grade gold containing rock. Gold is very unreactive.

- A small concentration of Au+ ions are formed by oxidation of Au. This oxidation takes place in the presence of CN<sup>–</sup> ions (usually as NaCN)

- -

- The formation constant of Au(CN)2 is very large.

- The Au is oxidized by bubbling air through a suspension of Au :

- containing ore in the presence of CN<sup>–</sup>

   - 4Au(s) + 8CN<sup>–</sup> (aq) + O2(g) +2H2O(l) ⇋ 4Au(CN)2–(aq) + 4OH–(aq)

- The pH of the resulting slurry is raised by adding lime (CaO) or another alkali to ensure that cyanide ions do not change into toxic hydrogen cyanide gas.

- Gold recovered onto carbon or by electrolysis

## **Summary - 1**

- The reaction between an acid and a base produces a **salt** and **water** .

- The **solubility** of the salt in water depends upon a number of factors including T, P, pH and activity.

- The solubility can be expressed by applying the Law of Mass Action to obtain a **solubility product K** . The higher the K **sp** sp

- The **Ion Activity Product** is a simple calculation that allows geochemists to assess  whether a given mineral is close to or far from equilibrium with its solubility products.
