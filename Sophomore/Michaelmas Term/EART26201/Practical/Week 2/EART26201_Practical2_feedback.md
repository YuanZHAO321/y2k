- a) Juvinas and Nakhla are both achondrite meteorites. Neither have chondrules and both have broadly basaltic textures and compositions: Juvinas contains pyroxene and plagioclase while Nakhla contains olivine, pyroxene and plagioclase. Briefly describe how achondrite meteorites form and how they are related to chondritic meteorites.

Achondrites are silicate meteorites from the crusts and mantles of differentiated parent bodies. That is, they are sourced from asteroids that have differentiated into metal-rich cores and silicate-rich crusts and mantles. In contrast, chondritic meteorites are from undifferentiated parent bodies.

- i) Did Juvinas and Nakhla form at the same time? If not, why might the meteorites have different ages? What does this tell us about where they formed? (You will need to write at least a couple of sentences to fully answer this question)

_(Clue, exposure dating of the surface of the Nakhla meteorite suggest_

_that it spent only 11 Ma in space before falling to Earth)_

Juvinas formed very early in the history of the solar system, not long after the condensation of the solar nebula. It was likely ejected from its parent body shortly after differentiation had occurred. In contrast, Nakhla formed relatively recently and must have formed though magmatic activity on a recently active planetary body (i.e. a terrestrial planet). Indeed, its short exposure age suggests that it was ejected into space by a very recent impact. In fact, Nakhla actually forms part of the SNC group of meteorites that are thought to have been sourced from Mars.

## **Question 2: Tracking crustal contamination at Cerro Galán, Argentina (60 minutes)**

- Cerro Galán is a large calc-alkaline volcano in the high Altiplano of the Argentinian Andes. Magmas erupted from arc volcanoes like Cerro Galán are often complex mixtures of basaltic melts from the mantle and dacitic/rhyolitic melts from the continental crust. Isotopic tracers can be used to determine the relative contributions of mantlederived basalts and crust-derived dacites/rhyolites to erupted magmas.
