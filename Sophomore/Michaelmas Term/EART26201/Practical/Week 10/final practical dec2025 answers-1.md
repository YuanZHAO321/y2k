2) Carbonates from the last glacial maximum record a global ocean 18O of +1.5‰. Assuming this difference is entirely due to loss of ocean water that is locked up as snow/ice precipitation, calculate the mass of water lost to the ice cap using 18O from q1 22.2‰. Assume 18O today is 0‰.

To reduce the ocean today by -1.5‰ we need to calculate f for:

18O = 18Oo +  ln f

where 18Oo = 1.5‰, 18O = 0‰ and 22.2‰ to give:

0 = 1.5 + 22.24 ln f

-1.5/22.24 = -0.06757 = ln f

f = e-0.06757 = 0.9347

This is the mass fraction of the ocean remaining which causes a change in ocean isotopic

composition by 1.5‰.

What would this mass correspond to in terms of sealevel drop?

Mean depth of Worlds Ocean = 3794m

Crudely, if the oceans are reduced to 93.47% of current volume, then average depth will be 0.9347*3794m = 3546m, corresponding to a sea level nearly 250m lower at the last glacial maximum. Sea level height at LGM approx. 120m lower. So double the observed sea level change but this is to be expected because 1.5‰ is probably not the change in sea water composition - from the lecture, approx. 0.7-0.8‰ is change in water composition (direct measurement from pore fluids) which reduce expected sea level change to 0.75/1.5x248=124m. Remarkably consistent with 120m!

Note: assumption that ocean temperature constant – thermal expansion/contraction of oceans also plays a significant role in ocean volume.
