H diffusion is about five orders of magnitude faster than Mg–Fe interdiffusion, so H will attain thermodynamic equilibrium much sooner. H diffuses faster because it is a very small ion (a proton) that can pass through gaps in the olivine crystal lattice quicker that larger ions cannot fit through. As a trace component it also does not have to exchange with lattice-forming species like Mg and Fe; charge balance is achieved by the presence of vacancies, whereby Mg and Fe ions are sometimes missing from the lattice.

## **Question 2: Estimating timescales of magma mixing from olivine (60 minutes)**

_(Thanks to Fidel Costa in Singapore and Maren Kahl in Heidelberg for providing materials)_

- Mg–Fe interdiffusion in olivine is commonly used to estimate the timescales over which different magmas mix with each other before volcanic eruptions. Magma mixing in magma chambers brings magmas with different Mg and Fe contents into contact with each other. As a result, the Mg and Fe contents of olivine crystals carried by these magmas progressively homogenise by diffusion, eventually reaching equilibrium.

- If diffusion is interrupted before thermodynamic equilibrium has been achieved (and the diffusion coefficient is known), we can determine the time between when diffusion started and when it was interrupted. In this case, we can determine the time between when the magma mixed and when it cooled during eruption.

- The sheet titled _Question 2 model_ contains a simple diffusion model that solves Fick’s second law for Mg–Fe interdiffusion in olivine crystals. The calculations relate the composition of zoned olivine crystals expressed in terms of forsterite content ( _X_ Fo) along profiles from crystal rims (at 0 µm) to crystal cores (at 250 µm). Olivine crystals may have either lower rim _X_ Fo (so-called normal zoning that reflects cooling before eruption) or higher rim _X_ Fo (so-called reverse zoning that reflects heating before eruption).

- The plot in the sheet titled _Question 2 model_ shows how the initially stepped profile of _X_ Fo (the black line) changes in response to diffusion over various lengths of time (shown as different coloured lines).
