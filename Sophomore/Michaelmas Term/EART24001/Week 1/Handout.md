# **Reporting the Orientation of Planes and Lineations**

### **Planes**

There are two ways to specify the orientation of a plane

(1) strike and dip:   030/40SE

(2) dip and dip direction:   40/120

• When specifying the orientation of the plane as strike and dip

(a) the strike should be specified as a **three** figure number, and be stated first; it is also good practice to specify it as a number between 000° and 180°

(b) the amount of dip should be specified as a **one or two** figure number

(c) the general direction of dip should be stated

• When specifying the orientation of the plane as dip and dip direction

(a) the amount of dip should be specified as a **one or two** figure number, and be stated first;

(b) the dip direction should be specified as a **three** figure number, and it must not be converted to a number between 000° and 180°

• Either way of specifying the orientation of a plane are fine, and each will be encountered in the practical classes so that you become accustomed to them both. Preference for one over the other generally depends upon the type of compass clinometer used to make the measurement. The Silva compass clinometers are better suited to making measurements of strike and dip.

### **Lineations**

There are two ways to specify the orientation of a line

(1) plunge and plunge direction:   23/060

(2) pitch on a specified plane:   37NE on 030/40SE

• When specifying the orientation of the line as plunge and plunge direction

(a) the amount of plunge should be specified as a **one or two** figure number, and be stated first;

(b) the plunge direction should be specified as a **three** figure number, and it must not be converted to a number between 000° and 180°

• When specifying the orientation of the line as a pitch on a specified plane

(a) the amount of pitch should be specified first and be accompanied by an indication of which end of the strike line it has been measured from

(b) the orientation of the plane on which the pitch was measured must also be stated (as strike and dip or dip and dip direction)

• Linear data should ultimately be expressed as plunge and plunge direction, that is, when specifying the orientation of a line as a pitch on a plane (as one might do in a number of measuring situations), the pitch must subsequently be converted into a plunge and plunge direction using the stereonet.

• Note that ‘pitch’ is formally the same as ‘apparent dip’

### **When specifying the orientation of a plane or line ALWAYS specify what the plane or line is.**

Some conventional shorthand:

(1) for bedding or cleavage surfaces use the letter S, _e.g._ , S0 bedding, S1 first cleavage, S2 second cleavage, _etc_ .:   S1 065/42SE

(2) for lineations the nature of the lineation must be specified, _e.g._ ,

• S1/S0 intersection lineation of first cleavage on bedding:   S1/S0 23/246 (Z)

• L1 mineral elongation or stretching lineation (specified) formed in the first deformation:   L1 23/328

- F1 the orientation of a fold axis belonging to a fold formed in the first deformation:   F1 23/246 (Z)

## **strike / dip**

## **dip / dip direction**

dip direction  030 + 90 = 120°   Î   40 / 120

The horizontal line (contour) which can be drawn on a dipping surface is referred to as a _strike line_ (= _stratum contour_ ); the compass-bearing of this line is referred to as the **_strike_** of the surface.

The **_dip_** of a plane is the angle which that plane makes with the horizontal as measured at 90° to the strike.

## **plunge / plunge direction**

The **_plunge_** of a line is the angle between that line and the horizontal.

The **_plunge direction_** is the compass-bearing towards which the line is tilted.
