# **SPECIFYING  THE  GEOMETRY  OF  FOLDS  (1)**

## **CYLINDRICAL  AND  NON-CYLINDRICAL  FOLDS**

• A curved surface, the shape of which can be generated by taking a straight line and moving it whilst keeping it parallel to itself, is called a cylindrically folded surface. The line capable of generating the surface is referred to as the **_fold axis_** .

• An important property of cylindrical folds is that the fold shape on parallel cross-section planes, whatever their orientation, is constant.

• Apart from providing some indication of the scale of the fold, and some basic observations about its shape ( _e.g._ , antiform/synform, anticline/syncline), there are four things which are required to specify the geometry of a cylindrical fold fully:

(1)  the orientation (plunge/plunge direction) of the fold axis;

(2)  the orientation (strike/dip) of the axial plane;

(3)  representative orientations of each limb of the fold;

- (4)  the interlimb angle.

• Depending on the plunge of the fold axis, the size of the interlimb angle, and the dip of the axial plane, the following adjectives are widely applied to describe the geometry of folds.

## **Plunge of fold axis**

0°-10° non-plunging 10°-30° gently-plunging 30°-60° moderately-plunging 60°-80° steeply-plunging 80°-90° vertical

## **Interlimb angle**

180°-120° gentle 120°-70° open 70°-30° close 30°-0° tight 0° isoclinal

## **Dip of axial plane**

0°-10° recumbent 10°-30° gently-inclined 30°-60° moderately-inclined 60°-80° steeply-inclined 80°-90° upright

Strictly, the axial plane is the plane which contains the fold axis and the line in the profile plane which joins the points of maximum curvature on adjacent beds. If the beds on each limb are of the same thickness the axial plane bisects the interlimb angle; if the beds on each limb are of different thickness, it does not.
