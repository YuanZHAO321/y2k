## Course unit overview

The unit is delivered primarily through a set of “hands-on”, map-based exercises. In the first half of the unit particular attention is focused on the use of strike lines (a) to complete geological outcrop maps based on limited exposure information, (b) to analyse the geometry of geological structures such as unconformities, fold, faults and igneous intrusions, and (c) to construct vertical cross-sections that illustrate the structures. The use of stereographic projection techniques to illustrate 3D geometries in 2D and to solve for a number of angular relationships that are commonly encountered in geometrical analyses are then introduced. The unit concludes with a set of more advanced exercises designed to analyse the interrelationships between folds, faults and igneous bodies. Those who are contemplating taking EART28202 Structural Geology will find this unit very helpful preparation

## Aims

The aim of this unit is to develop a working understanding of a range of methods used to present and interpret data on geological maps. There is a heavy focus on those methods needed to analyse the geometry of geological structures such as folds and faults. The skills developed are particularly fundamental for mapping-based fieldwork and for the interpretation of geological structures, and they have been widely recognized as core skills for geologists for as long as the science has existed

## Teaching and learning methods

The material will be primarily delivered in practical classes through a number of carefully selected, paper-based, map exercises. During the classes, helpful hints on solution strategy and practical demonstrations will be given; the key practical demonstrations will also be recorded as videos to enable them to be revisited outside the practical classes. Staff and postgraduate demonstrators will be on hand in the classes to guide students through the exercises as they work on them; it is through these exchanges that most formative feedback is delivered. During the practicals interim solutions to the exercises will be made visible in timely fashion. At the end of each class all exercises and solutions will be posted on Blackboard. After a couple of classes there will be an extra exercise that students will be strongly encouraged to attempt outside the practical class hours. Accompanying all classes there will be extra practice exercises – there is no assessment requirement to do the extra practice exercises but they are designed to be helpful to those students who do. The practical classes will be supported by a few PowerPoint presentations designed to provide some key background material
