# Dunes

- Large, asymmetric bedforms Not ‘large ripples’ – ripples don’t grow into dunes- transition is abrupt • Never first (lowest velocity) bedform to appear

- don’t form in sediment <~0.2 mm; require hydrodynamically rough boundary

- • most common bedform in sand bed rivers; also occur in gravel bed rivers

   - Bedform migrates downstream

   - Bedform becomes asymmetric

# Dune planform varies with flow strength

## At lower velocities:

- dunes low, widely spaced and 2-D

- long, straight to sinuous crests

- lee slope near angle of repose (25°- 30°)

- min. height 0.05 m

- form ratio 50-200

- sediment transport dominated by rolling, sliding and avalanching

As velocities increase gradual transition to 3-D dunes:

- height increases without increase in  spacing

- complex crest-lines: sinuous to lunate

- form ratio 15-20 (i.e. shorter and higher)

- 3-D

- lee slope angle <25°

- l 0.6 m - 100 ’ s m, h 0.05 m - >10 m

- sediment transport includes rolling, sliding, saltation and intermittent suspension

Observation: at higher velocities…….

# Observation: Transition from dunes to upper plane bed

(Mohrig, 2006)

#### Washout occurs when:

• characteristic excursion lengths of a significant number of size classes become  long relative to the distance of the lee face (i.e. suspension is dominant mode of sediment transport)

- since particles bypass lee face they are not incorporated into bedform

- dune crests planed off and height decreases

As flow velocities increase dunes become longer and lower, ‘ washing out ’ until finally they disappear entirely, giving way to a planar bed surface over which abundant suspended load as well as bed load is transported close to the bed in a ”traction carpet”

# Important points to remember

Lower plane bed only forms in coarse sand or coarser

Ripples form only in medium sand or finer Ripple spacing: increases with grain size No clear relationship between ripple size and either flow strength or water depth although planform may vary with flow strength and/or flow duration

Dune spacing: increases with depth increases with velocity (washing out) Dune height: increases with depth increases and then decreases with velocity (washing out)

Upper plane bed will form in any grain size at very high shear stresses where there is a dominance of transport in suspension Antidunes require a free surface and that the flow is supercritical (i.e. Fr~1)
