# **What is a fossil?**

**Fossil** : The remains of a once-living organism. [... ] The term includes skeletons, tracks, impressions, trails, borings, and casts. Fossils are usually found in consolidated rock, but not always (e.g. woolly mammoths living 20 000 years ago were recovered from the frozen tundra of Siberia). In its original sense, fossil meant anything dug up from the earth, including ores, precious stones, etc. The modern use of the word dates from the late 17th century. Source: Allaby, M., 2013. # **How do we describe directions on an animal?**

Anatomical terms of location

Sources: Hine, R., 2015. A dictionary of biology. Oxford University Press; Kent, M., 2006. The Oxford Dictionary of Sports Science & Medicine. Oxford University Press.

**Anterior** : Designating the part of an animal that faces to the front, i.e. that leads when the animal is moving.

**Posterior** : Designating the part of an animal that is to the rear, i.e. that follows when the animal is moving.

**Dorsal** : Describing the surface of a plant or animal that is farthest from the ground or other support, i.e. the upper surface. In vertebrates, the dorsal surface is that down which the backbone runs.

**Ventral** : Describing the surface of a plant or animal that is nearest or next to the ground or other support, i.e. the lower surface. In bipedal animals, such as humans, it is the forward-directed (anterior) surface.

**Median/axial** : Situated in the middle or oriented towards the midline; the opposite of lateral.

**Lateral** : In anatomy, applied to structures away from the midline or on the outer surface of the body.

**Proximal** : Closer to the trunk (e.g. the elbow is proximal to the wrist) or the origin of a structure.

**Distal** : Describing the part of an organ that is farthest from the organ's point of attachment to the rest of the body. For example, hands and feet are at the distal ends of arms and legs, respectively. Compare proximal.

# **How do we describe where and how animals live?**

15

**Pelagic** : Describing organisms that swim or drift in a sea or a lake, as distinct from those that live on the bottom (see benthos). Pelagic organisms are divided into plankton and nekton.

**Benthic** : Occurring on the bottom of a sea or lake. Benthic organisms may crawl, burrow, or remain attached to a substrate.

**Plankton (Planktonic)** : The collection of pelagic organisms that drift or float passively with the current in a sea or lake. Plankton forms an important food source for many other members of the aquatic community and is divided into zooplankton and phytoplankton.

**Nekton (Nektonic)** : Pelagic organisms that actively swim through the water.

**Epifaunal** : Applied to benthic organisms that live on the surface of the sea bed, either attached to objects on the bottom or freemoving. They are characteristic of the intertidal zone.

**Infaunal** : Applied to benthic organisms that dig into the sea bed or construct tubes or burrows. They are most common in the subtidal and deeper zones (i.e. the area seaward of the low-water mark).

**Sessile** : Describing animals that live permanently attached to a surface, i.e. sedentary animals. Many marine animals, e.g. sea anemones and limpets, are sessile.

**Corralum** : A solitary coral individual, or the whole skeleton of a colonial coral, made up from individual skeletons (corallites).

**Septum (pl. septa) "fence"** : A dividing wall within the skeleton of various animals (adj. septate). Here vertical (i.e. up-down) partitions arranged like spokes of a wheel.

**Theca “case”** : the skeletal wall of a coral. Theca may in turn be covered by an outermost skeletal sheath called the epitheca.

**Ruga (pl. rugae) “roughness”** : Irregular small projections (adj. rugose). In corals these are on epithica and are ~growth lines. **Apex** : “tip” or top (adj. apical).

**Calice** : The upper part of the skeleton of a coral (the corallum), in which the polyp sits.

**Tabula (pl. tabulae) “table”** : a horizontal division within the skeleton - a sheet secreted by polyp as coral grows.

**Dissepiment “partition”** : a horizontal, or nearly horizontal, plate of tissue supporting tabulae.

**Axial structure** : Septa fuse in the middle - the axial structure - which occupies core region.
