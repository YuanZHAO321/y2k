## Implication of sediment mass balance: Exner equation

- Threshold concept: Erosion vs deposition is controlled by the rate of change (in space and time) of sediment load

• In particular, it is NOT true that fast flows are erosional and slow flows are depositional (e.g., as implied in the common application of the Hjulstrom diagram)

## Significance of turbulence for erosion and deposition

- Turbulence results in variation in bed shear stress along and across flows

- Forces can vary by factors of four or even more with time

- Time scales for change in forces on a sediment particle can be very short, fractions of a second

- Bed shear stress governs the power of the current to move sediment; erosion and deposition depend on the change in boundary shear stress in the downstream direction

- In general, sediment transport rate (qs; the amount of sediment that is moved by a current) increases with increasing bed shear stress

> Variation in  o along and across the flow due to turbulence leads to a pattern of erosion and deposition on the bed of a mobile sediment

## The type of flows that produce bedforms

_Definition of a bedform: produced by the accumulation of mobile sediment grains into some shape or morphological element due to local changes in deposition and/or erosion in response to the interaction with a flowing current of air or water_

#### Bedforms interact with flow

- create form drag which slows flow (drag coefficient Cf), sheds turbulence

The flows that make bed configurations can be complex and vary with:

- direction of flow

- duration of flow

- strength of flow (variation through time)

Other important factors

- grain diameter and density of sediment

Re = Uhρ/μ

where: Re = Reynolds number U = mean flow velocity ρ = density of water μ = molecular viscosity of water h = mean water depth
