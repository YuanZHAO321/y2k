# Rotational structures within turbulent flows

## Eddies

Components of turbulence that rotate about axes that are perpendicular to the mean flow direction

Eddies breaking the water surface and flattening

Vary in diameter from fractions of mm to almost the depth of the flow

Move downstream with the current at a speed of approximately 80% of the water surface velocity (U¥)

Eddies move up and down within the flow as the travel downstream and lead to variation in boundary shear stress over time and along the flow direction

# Rotational structures within turbulent flows

Roller eddies Some eddies are created by the topography of the bed

In the lee of a negative step on the bed the flow separates from the boundary (“s” in the figure) and reattaches downstream (“a” in the figure)

Secondary flows Large scale rotational structures

Involves a rotating component of the motion of fluid about an axis that is parallel to the mean flow direction

A roller eddy develops between the point of separation and the point of attachment

Asymmetric bed forms develop similar eddies

Commonly there are two or more such rotating structures extending parallel to each other

Important in meander bends

# Turbulent boundary layers

Key difference between laminar and turbulent flows:

- in turbulent flows the velocity distribution is much more uniform over most of the thickness of the flow, but changes much more sharply very close to the boundary

Outer layer: fully turbulent layer

- motion of eddies even out differences in time-average velocity from layer to layer

- Viscous sublayer: region near the boundary • gradient of velocity very steep

- turbulence can’t even out differences in velocity

- • viscous shearing more important than turbulence

- • thickness very small % of flow (mms to cms)

# Dynamically rough and smooth boundaries

- roughness elements on the bed (sediment particles and/or bed forms) embedded within viscosity-dominated layer

- no overall, continuous viscositydominated layer

- • viscously-dominated layer only local to the surface of the roughness elements

- • roughness elements stick up into turbulent part of flow

# Forces on a particle

A flow exerts forces on all of the sediment grains exposed at the surface of a loose sediment bed

If the force exerted by the flow on a given sediment particle is momentarily great enough, the particle is set into motion

# Forces on a particle

### Two kinds of forces: viscous forces and pressure forces

Flow around an isolated spherical particle on an otherwise planar boundary

##### Viscous forces

- tangential to the solid surface

- • due to shear next to boundary (no slip condition) (surface drag)

- • shearing leads to viscous resistance force

##### Pressure forces

- perpendicular to boundary

- • fluids exert pressure within themselves and on all solid boundaries

   - Hydrostatic (buoyancy)

   - Hydrodynamic (lift and form drag)

Total resultant force on grain surface = viscous forces + pressure forces

# Balance of forces on a particle

The nature of the forces on a bed particle depends very strongly on the height of the hemisphere relative to the thickness of the viscosity-dominated layer

Flow and forces around a bed particle when embedded in viscous sublayer

Flow and forces around a bed particle when it protrudes high above the viscous sublayer

• streamlines diverge smoothly in front of the hemisphere, converge at the midsection, and diverge again behind the hemisphere (surface drag aka viscous drag aka skin friction drag)

• fluid pressure high upstream and relatively low downstream net downstream pressure force (form drag)

- streamlines diverge smoothly in front then separate from surface in midsection, leaving stagnant region behind the hemisphere (flow separation)

- fluid pressure in stagnant region is low everywhere- net downstream pressure force very large (form drag)

##### viscous drag + pressure drag

##### almost all pressure drag

In both cases, the pressure is relatively low at the top of the hemisphere and relatively high around its base, so there's a lift component to the resultant force

# Significance of turbulence for erosion and deposition

- Turbulence results in variation in bed shear stress along and across flows

- Forces can vary by factors of four or even more with time

- Time scales for change in forces on a sediment particle can be very short, fractions of a second

- Bed shear stress governs the power of the current to move sediment; erosion and deposition depend on the change in boundary shear stress in the downstream direction

- In general, sediment transport rate (qs; the amount of sediment that is moved by a current) increases with increasing bed shear stress

Variation in to along and across the flow due to turbulence leads to a pattern of erosion and deposition on the bed of a mobile sediment
