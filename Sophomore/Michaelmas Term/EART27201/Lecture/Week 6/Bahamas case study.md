### Case Study: Sediments of the Great Bahama Bank

# The Bahama Banks

- Chart shows Bahama Banks outlined by 200m depth contour, but much of water on banks <10m deep

- Slopes up to 40<sup>o</sup> away from bank margins

- Platform cut by deep channels e.g. Florida Straits (800m), channels to N and E (up to 2000m)

- Platform origin unclear but there are up to 5000m of shallow water carbonate going back to Jurassic, resting on continental basement

- Thin cover (<3.5m) Recent sediment deposited during rising sea levels since last glacial retreat

# The Great Bahama Bank

- Average temperature of Bank water: 22-31<sup>o</sup> C

- o

- Salinity typically 36-45% (normal seawater 35%o).  Highest in centre of Bank.

- Slightly elevated salinity occurs because of sheltered nature of Bank

- Factors favouring carbonate production:

   - Warm water

   - Elevated salinity

   - Protection from terrigenous influx

# Lithofacies

- The Bank can be divided into areas floored by particular sediment types

- These are known as lithofacies

- Each lithofacies can be related to particular physical conditions of environment (e.g. water depth, nutrient supply, current energy)

- Starting with highest energy lithofacies…..

# Coralgal Lithofacies

- Reefs made of corals and coralline algae.  Occur in discontinuous belt along windward margin

- Elsewhere small patches of corals and algae occur and these and reefs are surrounded by a mantle of coarse debris of eroded material = coralgal sand/gravel

• Google Earth image showing Andros Island, narrow lagoon with patch reefs and shelf margin facing the Tongue of the Ocean

# Oolite Lithofacies

- Sediment at least 90% ooids

- Ooids in environment of formation – at least periodically accreting precipitated carbonate while held in suspension in shallow turbulent environment

- Occur in shoals near bank margins

- Mobile substrate, so little life can survive

• Great Bahama Bank north of Andros Island showing carbonate sand shoals associated with embayment of the Tongue of the Ocean.  Ooid sands are concentrated in the area of Joulters Cays, the chain of islands north of Andros

## Oolitic Lithofacies, Grapestone Lithofacies

- Two lithofacies that grade into one another

- Grapestones are aggregate grains – a number of sandsized particles that have got cemented together by finegrained carbonate on the sea floor

- Oolitic lithofacies typically 50-60% ooids, grapestone lithofacies <50% ooids

- Both contain a diversity of other grains especially skeletal remains

- In places both these lithofacies are colonized by a marine grass, _Thalassia_ .  This has an important role in stabilizing sediment and preventing erosion.

### Pellet Mud Lithofacies and Mud Lithofacies

- In this context mud means carbonate mud

- Pellet muds typically contain 30-40% faecal pellets, mostly from worms and small gastropods

- Mud lithofacies typically <20% pellets

- Mud mostly produced by disintegration of carbonate encrustations of calcareous algae

- These lithofacies occupy lowest energy areas in lee of Andros Island

# Effects of burial and diagenesis

- Some influential materials may disappear e.g. marine grass, _Thalassia_ – may lead to misidentification of energy of environment.

- These sediments are 60-90% aragonite

- Corals, most molluscs, ooids, mud all aragonite

- Only coralline algae, echinoids and some molluscs are calcite

- Much of sediment will be subject to recrystallization or complete solution as a result of instability of aragonite.
