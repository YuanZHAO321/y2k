### Why study sedimentary rocks?

###### . **Grand Canyon National Park, USA**

Sediments and sedimentary rocks contain:

- all fossils and the entire record of Earth’s geological history

   - evidence for environmental change and the Earth’s response to that change, e.g., changing climate, growth and decline of supercontinents, past mountains and oceans, and the evolution of life

- source of economic minerals and materials

   - reservoirs of fossil fuels (oil and gas), aquafers for water

   - • potential storage sites for CO2, nuclear and other wastes

   - • building and aggregate materials and many other essential raw materials

   - e.g., coal, iron, salt, gypsum, source of cement, pottery clay, placer deposits

### How to define and measure particle size

##### Two approaches:

How we measure size defines what we mean by size e.g., sieving

- standard for coarser-grained sediment (above several 10s of microns)

- sieves choose the largest dimension in the least crosssectional area

Relate size to some other more easily defined property e.g., settling

- standard for finer-grained sediment (silt and clay, but also sand)

- larger particles fall through water faster than smaller particles

- assumes particles are spheres, with density of quartz, settling in water at a standard temperature

### Controls on weathering and erosion rates

Climate (temperature and rainfall) and relief of the source area are the main controls

Climate controls the _intensity_ of weathering

- _Chemical reactions more rapid at higher temperatures_

- _Areas of high rainfall provide water needed for chemical weathering_

- Humid Tropics: chemical weathering is most rapid: intense leaching of both silicates and carbonate

- Temperate climates: chemical weathering less rapid (water in solid phase so can’t mediate chemical reactions) but freeze/thaw becomes important particularly in upland areas, so mechanical weathering dominates

- Arid climates (hot or cold): weathering rates are low

Relief controls the _time available_ for weathering

- High relief = rapid erosion and short time for weathering

- Low relief = rates of erosion slow and longer time for weathering

Most common mineral in sediments in continental crust (av. 25%; can be 100% in sandstones but low % in fine-grained rocks and carbonates)

Main characteristics of quartz: Effectively inert at the Earth’s surface Stable at surface T and P, and under all T and P conditions of sedimentary processes, as well as under almost all igneous and metamorphic conditions

Solubility in pure water is extremely small à Wont chemically weather

### Feldspars (KAlSi3O8 – NaAlSi3O8 – CaAl2Si2O8)

Second most important coarse detrital mineral in sedimentary rocks

- Sharply subordinate to quartz in abundance • av. sandstone has 10-12% feldspar

### Effect of transport on textural and compositional maturity

Sediment texture and composition all reflect the maturity and history of a sediment Most changes are related to distance and time of transport and number of passes through the rock cycle

For example, in rivers changes in grain size, sorting, rounding and sphericity with transport are well documented

With increasing distance and time of physical transportation:

Grain size decreases

We will look at physical transport mechanisms later in the course

### Effect of transport on textural maturity

With increasing distance and time of physical transportation:

Grain size sorting increases

The longer the distance of transport the greater the opportunity to remove fine material (in suspension) and to leave coarser material behind, reducing the range of grain sizes present (improving the sorting and decreasing the sorting coefficient).

(Gomez et. a.l., 2001)

### Effect of transport on textural maturity

With increasing distance and time of physical transportation:

Angularity decreases/ roundness increases rapidly Sphericity increases slowly

Effect of transport on compositional maturity With increasing distance and time of physical transportation: There is loss of feldspar, mica and other clasts in favour of clay matrix and quartz
