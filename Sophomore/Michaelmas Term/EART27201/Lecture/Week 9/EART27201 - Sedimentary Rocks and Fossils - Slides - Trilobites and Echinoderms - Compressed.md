**Tagmata** : A specialized group of segments in the body of an arthropod.

**Cephalon** : The anterior or head region of a trilobite, which consists of at least five fused segments and is generally semicircular in shape.

**Thorax** : A mid-body comprising a varying number of segments.

**Pygidium** : The posterior part of the exoskeleton of a trilobite (Trilobita); it is generally formed by the fusion of several body segments but in some Cambrian forms consisting of a single segment.

**Axial/median lobe; Pleural/lateral lobe** : All three regions were divided by a pair of furrows running the length of the body, giving a trilobite appearance (i.e. a median or axial lobe, flanked on either side by a lateral lobe).

**Glabella** : The raised median part of the cephalon which is very variable in size and structure and it is probable that in life the stomach lay beneath it.

Source right: Fortey, R. and Chatterton, B., 2003. A Devonian trilobite with an eyeshade. Science, 301(5640), 1689-1689.

**Morphology / Anatomy Crinoidea**

**Crinoid** : A living class of echinoderms, whose members are either stalked (sea lilies) or unstalked (feather stars). **Toarcian** : A stage in the European Early Jurassic (183-175.6 Ma ago) Photo by Jenna Davenport.

Source: Benton, M.J. and Harper, D.A., 2020. Introduction to paleobiology and the fossil record. John Wiley & Sons.

<!-- Start of picture text -->

**Ossicles** : In Echinodermata, irregularly fenestrated calcareous plates, rods, and crosses arranged to form a lattice and bound together by connective tissue; together they comprise the skeleton.

**Holdfast** : The rooting structure that fixes an archaeocyathan, a crinoid, a dendroid graptolite or a seaweed to a rock.

**Calyx/aboral cup/theca** : Cup-shaped, plated body of crinoids. The calyx is made up of several rows of plates.

**Brachials (=arms)** : In crinoids (Crinoidea), long, plated, flexible arms made up of a series of articulated ossicles containing a water vessel from which the tube feet arise.

**Morphology / Anatomy Echinoidea**

34

35

Source - Benton, M.J. and Harper, D.A., 2020. Introduction to paleobiology and the fossil record. John Wiley & Sons, except sand dollar, by Didier Descouens and regular sea urchin by Esko Jäntti.

**Echinoid** : A free-living echinoderm in which the body is enclosed in a globular, cushion-shaped, discoidal, or heart-shaped shell built of columns of interlocking, calcareous plates, which bear movable appendages.

**Test** : Protective shell that covers the cells of some protists and the soft parts of certain invertebrate animals.

**Adapical/oral** : In an echinoderm, the lower, adapical or oral, surface is perforated by the mouth.

**Apical/aboral** : The upper, apical or aboral, surface that has the anal opening.

**Ambulacrum** : In Echinodermata, an area of the body surface (covered in most classes by calcitic plates), that overlies one of the radial canals of the internal water vascular system, and bears the tube feet.

**Interambulacrum** : In Echinodermata, that area of the body surface lying between ambulacra.

**Periproct** : In Echinoidea, the area surrounding the anus and covered in leathery skin in which small, calcitic plates are loosely embedded.

**Peristome** : In Echinoidea, the area around the mouth, covered in a leathery skin studded with small plates.
