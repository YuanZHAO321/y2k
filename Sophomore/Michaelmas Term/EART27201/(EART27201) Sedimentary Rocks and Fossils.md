## Course unit overview

Sedimentary rocks are formed from pre-existing rocks or pieces of once-living organisms; they are an essential part of the geological record and their interpretation, alongside the fossils they contain and are comprised of, is vital for reconstructing past environments and processes. This unit introduces students to the fundamental properties of sedimentary rocks and fossils; how to recognise and describe their physical and biological components, use them to subclassify different types of sedimentary rocks, and use the characteristics of these components to make inferences about the age of the rock, the source of the original sediment, the distance transported, the conditions under which it was deposited, and the conditions under which the sediment was converted to rock. We also survey the major groups of microfossils and macrofossils in terms of form and applications in sedimentological contexts.

## Aims

The unit aims to:

--Provide training for students to be able to identify, describe and measure sedimentary rocks in terms of their material properties

--Familiarize students with common major groups of micro and macro fossils, and their applications to inferring the ages of rocks, palaeoenvironments and sediment provenance.

--Equip students with the practical skills to use combined qualitative and quantitative observations in the field and from hand specimens to infer the transport, deposition and diagenetic histories of the sediment and subsequent rock

## Syllabus

The syllabus is:

W1: Lecture: What is a sedimentary rock? Classification and different derivations; Grains, matrix, cement, and porosity. Practical: Porosity and permeability experiment.

W2: Lecture: Clastic sedimentary rocks. Composition, roundness, sphericity, size range to infer provenance, transport, current velocities. Practical: Depositional history of clastic sedimentary rocks from hand specimen and thin sections.

W3: Lecture: Carbonate sedimentary rocks. Allochem types and preservation modes. Practical: Depositional history of carbonate sedimentary rocks from hand specimen and thin sections.

W4: Lecture: Macrofossils I. Trilobites and Brachiopods Common major groups and their characteristics. Their use as environmental indicators and their use in biostratigraphy. Practical: Examination and identification of different macrofossil groups using hand specimens.

W5: Lecture: Macrofossils II. The Molluscs (Bivalves, Gastropods and Cephalopods). Common major groups and their characteristics. Their use as environmental indicators and their use in biostratigraphy. Practical: Examination and identification of further macrofossil groups using hand specimens

W6: Reading Week.

W7: Lecture: Macrofossils III. Corals and Bryozoans. Common major groups and their characteristics. Their use as environmental indicators and their use in biostratigraphy. Practical: Examination and identification of further macrofossil groups using hand specimens.

W8: Lecture: Macrofossils IV. Echinoids and Graptolites. Common major groups and their characteristics. Their use as environmental indicators and their use in biostratigraphy. Practical: Examination and identification of further macrofossil groups using hand specimens.

W9: Lecture: Microfossils. Different types, their extraction and examination, and their use in biostratigraphy. Practical: Examination and identification of different microfossil groups using binocular and petrological microscopy.

W10: Lecture: Physical sedimentary structures. The bedform phase diagram. Toolmarks, load and dewatering features, desiccation cracks, etc. Practical: Flume tank experiment

W11: Lecture: Post-depositional processes (incl. biogenic sedimentary structures). Trace fossils, roots, reduction oxidation conditions, bioturbation index. Compaction (mechanical, chemical), dissolution, cementation, secondary porosity and permeability. Practical: paragenetic histories from thin sections.

W12: Lecture: Introduction to Facies Analysis. Practical: Part 1 of core logging exercise.

## Teaching and learning methods

This course will be delivered through a 1 hour lecture each week followed by a two hour practical, that develops the concepts raised in the lectures. The practical classes provide the opportunity to the students to physically examine rocks and fossils, describe them, and then reconstruct the processes that led to their physical appearance, based on theory developed largely in lectures. It is crucial that students attend all classes and practical classes, as themes build rapidly on one another through the course. Lectures and signposts to relevant reading will be posted on blackboard. Students will be required to learn, primarily, posted lecture notes. No specialist software will be used.
