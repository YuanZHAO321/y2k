## Course unit overview

The deposits discussed in this unit are key components of current and future energy systems and raw materials for a variety of industries. In addition, they have historical value, as they have shaped human society. The unit introduces the fundamental properties and genesis of the deposits and places them in their geodynamic and societal context.

Teaching introduces students to the different petroleum types, how hydrocarbons are generated from source rocks and reservoirs, trapping methods and seals. The unit explores the geochemistry of uranium, formation of uranium deposits, and its use in nuclear energy generation. A selection of metallic and non-metallic mineral deposits are discussed in terms of origin, exploration and exploitation and use.

## Aims

This unit gives students a sound overview of the nature and origin of key metallic and non-metallic mineral deposits, and hydrocarbon systems.

## Learning outcomes

On the successful completion of the course, students will be able to:

Assessed

## Syllabus

- Week 1: Introduction: commodity types, economic and society context, exploration and appraisal
- Week 2: The subsurface environment: temperature, pressure, fluid movements
- Week 3: Physical and chemical properties of gold, typology and genesis of gold deposits (orogenic and placer deposits)
- Week 4: Typology and genesis of banded iron formations as major iron deposits
- Week 5: Physical and chemical properties of platinum-group minerals (PGM), typology and genesis of PGM deposits, PGM accumulation in layered igneous intrusions
- Week 6: Natural radioactivity and natural reactors, uranium deposits
- Week 7: Genesis of diamonds, formation and mining of diamond deposits (kimberlites and placers)
- Week 8: Petroleum systems: source rocks, migration, reservoirs, traps
- Week 9: Evaporite deposits: formation, mining, use
- Week 10: Global trends in geology and formation of commodity deposits

## Teaching and learning methods

The unit is taught through combined lectures and practicals. Practicals serve to consolidate and apply theoretical knowledge, and to provide feedback on students’ work.
