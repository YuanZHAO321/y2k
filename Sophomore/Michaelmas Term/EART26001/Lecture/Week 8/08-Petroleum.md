## **Environmental aspects**

**All industrial activity has an environmental impact – generally negative (e.g. poisons are produced, damage is done, noise and harm to life, if not managed properly). The** **_potential_ harm by industry activity is enormous – cf 2010 incident in Gulf of Mexico. The oil industry is intensely regulated for HSE (health, safety and environment). However, bad HSE practice is bad for business. Therefore, the oil industry often leads governments in safety and environmental practice.**

**Biggest aspect to environmental impact – global warming and climate change: by producing and using petroleum we create an imbalance in the natural carbon cycle. Fossils fuels are unlikely to disappear quickly due to our reliance on them. Fossil fuels are still relatively cheap, and a means for many developing countries to reduce poverty - but corruption is also often associated with petroleum production and export.**

## **Petroleum deposits Key learning points week 8**

**Petroleum (hydrocarbons) form the basis of our current economy as they provide a relatively cheap and versatile fuel source. Related greenhouse gas emissions do however pose serious threats to the environment and climate.**

**Deposits can be viewed in the framework of the petroleum system, which requires:**

- **Matured source rock (influenced by productivity, preservation, dilution, composition, temperature and pressure)**

- **Migration of hydrocarbon fluids**

- **Accumulation of fluids in a trap (consists of porous/permeable reservoir rock and impermeable seal)**

**Detailed structural, (organic) geochemical and sedimentary studies are required to identify all components of a working petroleum system**

**Exploration passes through detailed regional knowledge, seismic studies, and drilling**
