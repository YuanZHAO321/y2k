## **Hydrothermal (orogenic) gold deposits Summary**

**Hydrothermal fluids are major driver for metal transport and precipitation due to metals bonding with anions.**

**Various sources – in orogenic gold deposits, hydrothermal fluids derive from breakdown of water-bearing minerals in metamorphosis and from igneous exsolution. Fluid transport along major crustal structures.**

**Characterization of fluid source, composition and relation to regional and local structure are essential for exploration and deposit evaluation.**

**Robb 2005**

**Deposits known throughout Earth history, associated with all mountain belts (e.g. Andes – exploitation by Spanish empire made Spain a major power since 1500s).**

**Local example: Cononish, Scotland – Paleozoic quartz veins in Proterozoic metamorphic rocks with 11g/t gold**

19 - EART26001: Energy & Resources - 06

## **Magmatic-hydrothermal gold deposits Epithermal and porphyry deposits**

**Local fluid circulation around igneous intrusion involving magmatic-hydrothermal and heated meteoric (hydrothermal) water – epithermal (shallow crustal depth) Porphyries (mainly Cu +/- Mo, W, Au) form at depth from warmer magmatichydrothermal fluids (mesothermal)**

**Robb 2005**

## **Placer gold deposits Summary**

**Placers generally form where sediment transport allows separation of heavy minerals from other sedimentary grains – gold, ilmenite, magnetite, diamonds etc.**

**Understanding sedimentary systems and sediment transport processes are essential in exploration and evaluation of placer deposits**

**The Witwatersrand goldfields are one of the largest paleoplacers in the world, formed in a stable basin with delivery of gold-rich sediment from juvenile, gold-rich crust (cf Barberton). Low atmospheric oxygen further allowed for preservation of detrital U and pyrite. These conditions were not replicated later in Earth’s history.**

## **Gold and its wider impact Environmental impact**

**All beneficiation methods (except bioleaching) have potentially severe negative side effects:**

- **Acid mine drainage from roasting**

- **Pollution of groundwaters and poisoning of humans/livestock by cyanide and mercury**

- **Mine tailings are often left unprotected, letting rain leach remaining chemicals into groundwater, or letting wind carry particles**

- **Witwatersrand tailings contain U: low-level radioactive waste**

**Recycling of tailings still leaves contaminated footprint Greenhouse gas emissions**

## **Gold deposits Key learning points week 6**

### **Gold occurs in a variety of deposit types:**

- **Magmatic-hydrothermal: Epithermal, Porphyries**

- **Hydrothermal: Orogenic (typically metamorphogenic)**

- **Sedimentary: Placers**

**Release of metamorphic hydrothermal fluids, movement along fractures, and formation of gold in veins and disseminations is typical for orogenic deposits. Placers (modern and paleoplacer) result from density separation of dense gold particles from other grains during sedimentary transport.**

**Processing of gold is environmentally very sensitive.**

**Goldfarb 2010**
