## Slide 1

- Burial graphs

- We have learnt that organic-rich source rocks mature under increasing pressure and temperature as they get buried into the subsurface. In exploration for hydrocarbons, a key element is determining when and where these source rocks have reached conditions that allow maturation of organic matter – i.e. when they passed into oil and gas windows. This can be reconstructed using burial graphs that represent the depth and temperature history of a given rock column through time.

- Gluyas 2004

## Slide 3

- Burial graph construction

- Step 1:

- Starting from youngest unit (I), use its basal age (20 Myr) to determine when it was at surface -> line A-B

- All units below will have the same burial rate during this time interval, so draw parallel lines (C-D etc)

- Step 2:

- For the next unit (II), use its basal age (30 Myr) to determine when it was at surface -> line E-C

- All units below will have the same burial rate during this time interval, so draw parallel lines

- Steps 3-4:

- Systematically follow the same principle, working your way back in time.
