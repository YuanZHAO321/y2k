# **2.2 Calculation of pressure with depth**

In the Cheshire Basin a gas well was drilled. The following formation pressures were measured downhole in the well. All depths are in m below ground level.

|500m|58 kg/cm<sup>2</sup>|
|---|---|
|1000m|120 kg/cm<sup>2</sup>|
|1500m|175 kg/cm<sup>2</sup>|
|2000m|300 kg/cm<sup>2</sup>|
|2500m|400 kg/cm<sup>2</sup>|
|3000m|450 kg/cm<sup>2</sup>|
|3250m|500 kg/cm<sup>2</sup>|

Note that values are given in kg/cm<sup>2</sup> , which is an old pressure unit, now largely superseded by the SI unit Pa(scal). It is still widely used in drilling though. This unit relates pressure with weight, which is useful in applications where a pressure is applied to generate a load, as in this case. 1 kg/cm<sup>2</sup> = 98,066.5 Pa.

# 1) Plot formation pressure data against depth.

2) Assuming an average rock density of 2.3g/cm<sup>3</sup> , plot the lithostatic (overburden) pressure on the same graph. This is the pressure exerted by the weight of the overlying rock, so is the product of depth and rock density. The value of 2.3g/cm<sup>3</sup> is assumed to be the maximum overpressure that can be encountered before rupture.

- 4) What is the overpressure in the well, i.e. the difference between measured formation pressures and hydrostatic pressures? What does this tell us about conditions in the subsurface?
