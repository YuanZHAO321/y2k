# **2) Geochemistry**

For the BIF and oolitic ironstone estimate the percentage of iron-bearing minerals in the sample (iron minerals in BIF, ooids in oolitic ironstone). Calculate the amount of pure iron that would be contained in 1m<sup>3</sup> of each sample. Assume the following typical mineralogy: BIF: hematite Fe2O3 Oolitic ironstone: siderite FeCO3

Use the percentage estimation chart at the back (Figure 2).

Molar weights: Fe: 55.85 g/mol O: 16 g/mol C: 12.01 g/mol

Density of hematite: 5.26 g/cm<sup>3</sup> Density of siderite: 3.96 g/cm<sup>3</sup>

Answer:

Hematite: (2x55.85) + (3x16) = 159.7; 2x55.85=111.7/159.7 = 69.9% Fe Siderite: 55.85 + 12.01 + (3x16) = 115.86; 55.85/115.86 = 48.2% Fe

Assume a 1m<sup>3</sup> sample with 30% hematite (determined from thin section) 0.3 m<sup>3</sup> hematite = 0.3 x 10<sup>6</sup> cm<sup>3</sup> Density hematite: 5.26 g/cm<sup>3</sup> i.e. sample contains 5.26 x 0.3 x 10<sup>6</sup> = 1,578,000 g = 1,578 kg hematite of it 69.9% Fe: 1,578 kg x 0.699 = 1,103 kg Fe

Assume a 1m<sup>3</sup> sample with 30% (-40%) siderite (determined from thin section) 0.3 m<sup>3</sup> siderite = 0.3 x 10<sup>6</sup> cm<sup>3</sup> Density siderite: 3.96 g/cm<sup>3</sup> i.e. sample contains 3.96 x 0.3 x 10<sup>6</sup> = 1,188,000 g = 1,188 kg siderite of it 48.2% Fe: 1,188 kg x 0.482 = 572.6 kg Fe
