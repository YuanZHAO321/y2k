# **Cu**

- Most widely used in energy transition

- unmatched thermal and electrical conductivity

- broad range of electronic and industrial applications. Its attributes make it challenging to substitute, although substitutions exist by Al (electrical equipment, power cables), optical Fiber (telecoms), Ti (heat exchangers)

# **Cu**

- 30% increase in production over last 10 years to match demand

- New projects in pipeline to 2025, but beyond uncertain

- Some big mines like Escondida expected to see peak production soon

- Mined Cu grades decreased from 10-20% in 19<sup>th</sup> century to <1% now; more research and technology have unlocked reserves, and processing innovations have allowed lower grade processing

- High water demand for processing, but water is scarce in many producing countries

- Arsenic contamination at lower grades and pollution

# **Cu challenges**

- Challenging to substitute due to its superior performance in electrical applications

- Mines currently in operation are nearing their peak due to declining ore quality (decreases grades, low grade ores economic?) and reserves exhaustion (move to fringes of deposits)

- Declining ore quality exerts upward pressure on production costs, emissions and waste volumes (processing on site, dust suppression, deeper mining = higher costs)

- Mines in South America and Australia are exposed to high levels of climate and water stress

# Critical because:

- Supply

- (projected) demand in energy transition

- Low substitutability

# **Co**

- Use in Li-ion batteries for EV, also alloys, carbide, magnets

- unmatched thermal and electrical conductivity

- broad range of electronic and industrial applications. Its attributes make it challenging to substitute

# **Co**

- Unexpected demand rattled market -> strain on supply

- Production increase 10% in last 5 years

- 74% DRC, by-product of Cu/Ni mines

- 10-20% of DRC mining from artisanal mining

- China main processing country

- Demand depends on cathode technology in batteries (could turn to Ni)

- Expected output from current mines and projects likely to satisfy demand (STEPS, not SDS scenario)

- DRC and China are linked via Co – supply chain issues, China has invested in DRC

# **Artisanal mining (ASM)**

- Positive effect on markets as production can shift quickly

- Negative effect on market, as vulnerable to economic and social events

- Unregulated, unsafe working, child labor, intermediate traders keep prices low and reap profits

- Yet provides income locally – can mitigate root cause of child labor, which is poverty, ideally organized locally, contributing to local development

- Efforts to formalize work via governments, consumer organizations, industry efforts

- Industry has normally secured best licenses; ASM will not happen in remaining licenses with low grade, but tend to occur illegally on industry licenses

# **Co challenges**

- High reliance on DRC for production and on China for refining set to persist, as only a few projects are under development outside these countries

- Significance of artisanal small-scale mining makes the supply vulnerable to social pressures

- New supply is subject to developments in nickel and copper markets as some 90% of cobalt is produced as a byproduct of these minerals

# Critical because:

- Supply

- (projected) demand in energy transition

- By-product

- Social/environmental pressures
