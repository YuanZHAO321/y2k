# **Practical 4: PGE petrography and volumetrics calculation for a PGE deposit**

# **Part A**

Today we will illustrate the petrography of typical PGE-bearing ultramafic and mafic rocks from layered intrusions. For this, we make use of a number of hand specimens (HS) and thin sections (TS). Please use HS and TS to identify the main rock forming minerals (see listing on next page, and remember your first year classes!), and the fabrics of the rocks, including layering and cumulates.

Hand samples illustrate:

- pegmatite fabric (pegmatite = very coarse-grained igneous rock)

- layered fabric

- massive chromitite

(careful, samples are heavy)

There are several thin sections showing the mineralogy and chromite cumulates.

This calculation illustrates the basic simplicity of maths used in reserve/resource calculations. Note that a number of uncertainties such as ore losses are not considered here. Full evaluations would also take a statistical approach. Data modified from Grobler (2019): https://doi.org/10.1007/s00126-0180800-x

# <u>Data:</u>

Consider a prospect on the northern limb of the Bushveld Complex, exploring for PGEs and Cr. The extract from the geological map, block diagram and cross section on the next 2 pages (Figs. 1-3) show the stratigraphy, dip of strata, main faults, and exploration data (boreholes, cross sections). The prospect area is outlined in the red dashed line. Exploration focuses on several units, including the Tennis Ball Marker (TBM, shown in blue on the map and block diagram).

Although in reality the rocks are folded, for this exercise consider the TBM as a simple sheet of rock with a thickness of 0.8 m.

The reef consists of a pegmatitic pyroxenite with the following modal composition: 70 wt% pyroxene (diopside, CaMgSi2O6)

25 wt% plagioclase (anorthite, CaAl2Si2O8)

2 wt% sulfide (pyrrhotite, Fe1-xS)

3 wt% chromite, FeCr2O4
