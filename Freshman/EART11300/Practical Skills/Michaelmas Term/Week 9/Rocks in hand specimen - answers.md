# **C.  Further classification of the different rock types:**

## **Igneous Rocks.**

A simple classification of igneous rocks is based upon the grain size, which tells us how quickly the rock has cooled:

- Medium- to coarse-grained = slow cooling = **plutonic**

- Fine-grained = fast cooling = **volcanic**

Note that if the rock contains larger crystals in a finer-grained “groundmass” (= the rock matrix), then this indicates a 2-stage cooling process, with the larger crystals growing slowly in a magma chamger, and the groundmass crystallising in the eruption.

## **Sedimentary Rocks.**

If the rock is made of particles, then it can be classified according to grain size, which indicates the distance travelled from source to deposition:

- **Proximal** = close to source = coarser grain size.

- **Distal** = far away from source = finer grain size.

The grain size reflects the amount of energy available for transportation. This classification does not work so well for rocks containing fossils whose size is determined by the kind of fossil rather than distance travelled.

## **Metamorphic Rocks.**

Can be classified according to the extent that the rock has changed during metamorphism, which usually depends on the temperature reached, and is known as **metamorphic grade.**

- **Low grade** display the least amount of change. If the rock started out as fine-grained (e.g. a mudstone), then low-grade metamorphism will result in a rock that is still fine-grained.

- **Medium grade** show an intermediate level of change. It will be coarser grained.

- **High grade** show the most change. It will have the largest crystals.
