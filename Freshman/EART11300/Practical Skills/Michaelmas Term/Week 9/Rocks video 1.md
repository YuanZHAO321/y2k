## Igneous rocks

When you are looking at a hand specimen or thin section:

- Some crystals have their own shape (i.e. are bounded by flat crystal faces) indicating unhindered growth from a liquid.

- Igneous rocks tend to have interlocking crystals

- Contain relatively few minerals (normally 5 different mineral  species or less)

- Crystals of each mineral tend to be quite uniform.

- May contain fossil gas bubbles (= vesicles).  Not otherwise porous.

- Crystals don’t usually have a preferred orientation (but there are exceptions, e.g. cumulates and rocks with flowaligned crystals)

- Do not contain organic matter or fossils.

### Further classification of the igneous rocks

**Igneous rocks** can be classified in two main ways:

1. Based upon the chemistry and therefore reflected in the mineralogy.

   - High silica = **felsic** . Contains quartz, abundant feldspar, not many mafic (dark coloured) minerals.

   - Intermediate silica = **intermediate** . Contains abundant feldspar, no obvious quartz, some mafic minerals.

   - Low silica = **mafic** . Contains abundant mafic minerals and some feldspar.

   - Very low silica = **ultramafic.** Contains >90% mafic minerals.
