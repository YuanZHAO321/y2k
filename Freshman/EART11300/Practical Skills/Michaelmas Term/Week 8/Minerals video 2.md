## What is ‘habit’?

- The combination and relative development of crystal faces is called the crystal’s ‘habit’.

- Habit depends partly on crystal structure, but also on the way the crystal has grown, allowing some faces to develop preferentially.

##### Terms used to describe habit:

- equant

- tabular

- platy

- prismatic (moderately elongate)

- acicular (needle-shaped)

- fibrous

- Massive = compact mass of interlocking crystals with no clear division between individual grains.

### Examples: 2. Mica

- Mica is a common mineral in granites and metamorphosed mudstones.

- Its habit is **platy** (occasionally **tabular** ).

- The mica structure is made of layers in which the bonds are short and strong (2 sheets of Si-O tetrahedra sandwiching a sheet of Mg or Al), separated by layers of large potassium ions.

- Because the potassium is weakly bonded, mica grows slowly in this direction, giving rise to very thin crystals.

- But it grows quickly parallel to the layers, because the atoms are denser in this plane, which means that bonds form faster allowing large platy crystals to form.

Looking at a layer side on: weak bonds to potassium = slow growth perpendicular to the layers.

Looking down on a layer: strong bonds = rapid growth in all directions within the layer.
