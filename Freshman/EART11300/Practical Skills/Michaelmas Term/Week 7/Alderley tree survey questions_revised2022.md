Alderley Edge woodland assessment

Scenario: the National Trust have asked you in as environmental consultants to assess the woodland at Alderley Edge. Using the data you collected on your field work you can write a short summary describing their woodlands.

Woodland composition

What is the composition of the woodland (% of each species)?

Sweet chestnut blight is caused by a fungus which attacks bark and can kill sweet chestnut trees. If this disease reaches Alderley Edge, using the calculated tree density and species composition, how many trees per hectare are at risk from this disease?

In heavily infected areas, the fungus will also spread to oak trees. They are not likely to die, but can act as a reservoir for the disease. If the woodland at Alderley Edge was heavily infected, what is the total number of trees per hectare could be carrying the disease?

Tree density estimate calculation:

How to calculate the density estimate for each station:

Worked example for station 1
Distance 1= 1.20 m
Distance 2 = 0.45 m
Distance 3 = 0.85 m
Distance 4 = 2.10 m

Mean distance to a plant (d) = 1.15 m
Tree density at station 1 = 1/1.152 = 0.7561 trees per m2

Tree density is often given in trees per hectare, and

1 hectare = 100 m x 100 m = 10,000 m2

So density at station 1 = 7,561 trees per hectare

Once you have a number for the tree density in trees per hectare for each station, estimate the tree density for the whole site by calculating the overall mean. You can also calculate a standard deviation for this mean.

Standard deviation calculation:

√ ∑(density value-mean density value)2 / (number of replicate stations–1)
