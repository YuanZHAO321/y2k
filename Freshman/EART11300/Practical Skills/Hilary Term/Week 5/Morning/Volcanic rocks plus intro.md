## Slide 3

- Tectonic setting and volcanism

- Volcanism is a consequence of magma generation, i.e. partial melting of the solid mantle.

- Magma is made by different processes in different tectonic settings.

## Slide 4

- Magma chemistry & physics

- Magma silica content changes during fractional crystallization

- Magma viscosity strong depends on silica content (polymerisation)

## Slide 5

- What are (effusive and explosive) eruptions?

- All magmas contain volatiles: H2O, CO2, SO2, etc. The abundance varies between tectonic setting and as a function of composition (ask your demonstrators…).

- Under crustal pressures, volatiles are (partly) dissolved.

- As magmas ascend, volatiles exsolve / degas.

- The viscosity of a magma determines whether gas bubbles can escape or not:

- Low viscosity = easy bubble escape = magma erupts as a liquid (effusive)

- High viscosity = bubbles can’t escape / growth is impeded = magma torn apart (“fragmentation”) as bubbles finally burst = magma erupts as pyroclasts (explosive)

- Your task is to describe samples of according to the following: [20 min per sample]

- Size: What are the approximate dimensions of the sample in cm?

- Shape: Is the sample rounded, angular, elongate or blocky? Does it have the same texture all over?

- Colour: Is the sample light or dark in colour? Is it dull, shiny, or glassy? (Remember that mafic rocks (e.g., basalt) are generally (but not always) dark in colour and felsic rocks (e.g., rhyolite) light in colour)

- Mineralogy: Are there crystals in the sample? If so, how large are they? What shape and colour are they?

- Density: Is the sample light of heavy?

- Vesicularity: Are there bubbles in the sample? If so, what are their properties (size, shape, abundance).

- Eruption style interpretation: Given your answers to the above, infer a possible eruption style. In other words, did the rock form in an explosive or effusive eruption? Thinking about your answers to Part 1 may help here.

## Slide 10

- Sample MV01: Meradalir Valley, Fagradalsfjall, SW Iceland

- Interpretations

- Blue, glassy texture on surface (and fine-grained groundmass)  rapid cooling

- Just one ~6mm vesicle  sample is not a product of explosive activity  sample is a product of effusive activity (lava flow)

- Phenocrysts: green crystals = olivine, white crystals = plagioclase  typical of tholeiitic basalt

- Black/dark brown mafic composition (relatively low silica, high Fe and Mg)  low viscosity basaltic magma
