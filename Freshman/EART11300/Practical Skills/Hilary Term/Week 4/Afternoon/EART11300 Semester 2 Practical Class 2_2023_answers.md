# **Peridotite (RP or R56)**

<mark>This is an igneous rock. Most of the Earth’s mantle is made of peridotite, but these samples actually comes from a large intrusion (from the island of Rum, Inner Hebrides, Scotland). You looked at R56 last week; RP is similar. The main mineral is</mark> **<mark>olivine</mark>** <mark>. It displays quite respectable crystal shapes and well-developed irregular fractures. Draw a few crystals that show a reasonable crytal shape (they will have parallel sides). Is its relief high, or low? High Can you see any cleavage in it? No (maybe weak). Note the range of interference colours represented by the various crystal orientations. What is the</mark> **<mark>maximum</mark>** <mark>colour (on the Michel Levy colour chart) that you can see? It will be a colour in the intermediate range.</mark>

<mark>Now look at the evidence that this is an igneous rock, using the possible attributes of igneous rocks:</mark>

- <mark>Does it have interlocking crystals? No.</mark>

- Do some crystals have their own shape? Yes (not so obvious in R56).

- Does it contain relatively few minerals? Yes, most of the rock is made of olivine plus two other minerals (there are also a few opaque grains and some alteration).

- Are crystals of each mineral quite uniform? Yes.

- Does it contain fossil gas bubbles (= vesicles)?  No, it has no porosity.

- Do the crystals have a preferred orientation? No.

# **Sandstone (R25 or SK)**

This is a sedimentary rock. It contains 2 minerals, one with low interference colour, one with high interference colour. Look at the low interference colour mineral. Does it contain any cleavage? No. This mineral is quartz. Notice how the crystals are all very clear – there is no alteration. This is a useful property of quartz. Other properties of quartz are: low relief, no twinning or zoning (which you will see in some low-interference colour minerals in other thin sections).

The other mineral, with high interference colour, is calcite. The sandy interference colour, with green and pink tinges, is a typial high interference colour. Calcite (and its fellow carbonate mineral, dolomite) is the only common mineral you will see with high interference colour. Notice how the calcite is filling in the gaps around the quartz crystals, forming a cement. Sketch a few quartz grains and annotate your sketch to show the calcite cement.

<mark>Now look at the evidence that this is a sedimentary rock, using the possible attributes of sedimentary rocks:</mark>

- Does it consist of particles (clasts) of pre-existing rocks? Yes, many of the quartz grains have a very irregular shape, indicating that they are not fresh crystals but have been abraded from a pre-existing rock.

- Are the clasts rounded? Some of them.

- Does it contain fossils? No.

- Does it contain organic matter? No.

- Does it contain any porosity? No (the calcite cement holding the quartz grains together has filled any earlier porosity).

The main minerals in this rock are:

2. **feldspar** – see the NH and CP descriptions. In this rock the feldspar is heavily altered (to flecks that are either grey under crossed polars or have bright interference colours). This is common in feldspar, whereas quartz is never altered.
