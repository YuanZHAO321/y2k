Wildfire Tracking & Emissions Calculation Worksheet

Objective:

You will analyse a wildfire of your choice using Sentinel-2 satellite imagery from the Copernicus Browser. Your task is to track the fire's progress, measure the burn area, estimate the proportion of different vegetation types, balance the combustion equation, and calculate CO₂ emissions.

Part 1: Tracking the Wildfire Using Sentinel-2 Imagery

Access the Copernicus Browser: Open the Copernicus Browser and locate the wildfire using the provided link. Use the panel on the left to select between true SWIR (infrared wavelengths) and the true colour

Identify the Fire's Progression: Using Sentinel-2 imagery, track the fire from its first detection to when it is extinguished.

Measure the Burn Area: Use the polygon measuring tool on the right side of the webpage to determine the total area affected by the wildfire.

Estimate Vegetation Composition:

Approximate the percentage of the burned area that was forest or grassland. You could use the colour of the vegetation in the satellite imagery or search for images of the approximate area online.

Burned Area Measurement:

Total Burned Area: 100 km2

Percentage of Forest: 60%

Percentage of Grassland: 40%

Part 2: Balancing the Combustion Equation

The main component of wood and plant material is cellulose (C₆H₁₀O₅). In an oxygen-rich environment, incomplete combustion occurs, releasing carbon dioxide (CO₂) and water (H₂O). If the supply of oxygen is limited, we may also produce carbon monoxide and soot particles.

Unbalanced Equation for oxygen rich environment: C₆H₁₀O₅ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O

Task:

Balance the equation

Balanced Equation:

C₆H₁₀O₅ +6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 5H₂O

6	C	6

10	H	10

17	O	17

Part 3: Calculating CO₂ Emissions

Step 1: Determine Biomass in Burned Area

Forest biomass = 75 tonnes per acre

Grassland biomass = 10 tonnes per acre

Forest cellulose content = 50% of biomass

Grassland cellulose content = 30% of biomass

Calculate the mass of cellulose burned for each vegetation type:

1km2 = 247.1 acres
100km2 = 24,710 acres

Forrest :

24,710 acres * 0.6 = 14,826 acres of forest

14,826 acres * 75 tonnes per acre = 1,111,950 tonnes of biomass

1,111,950 tonnes of biomass * 0.5 = 555,975 tonnes of cellulose

Grassland:

- 24,710 * 0.4 = 9,884 acres of forest

- 9,884 acres * 10 tonnes per acre = 98,8840 tonnes of biomass

- 98,8840 tonnes of biomass * 0.3 = 29,652 tonnes of cellulose

- Total tonnes of cellulose burned:

585,627 tonnes

Final Emission Calculation:

total CO₂ released =

balanced equation has a mole ratio between cellulose and CO2 of 1:6.

Molar mass of cellulose = 162 g

Molar mass of 6CO2 = 44 g * 6 = 264 g

162g cellulose produces 264g of CO2

264/162 = 1.63

585,627 tonnes of cellulose *1.63 = 954,572 tonnes of CO2

total H2O released =

balanced equation has a mole ratio between cellulose and H2O of 1:5.

Molar mass of cellulose = 162 g

Molar mass of 5H2O = 18g * 5 = 90 g

162g cellulose produces 90g of H2O

90/162 = 0.56

585,627 tonnes of cellulose *0.56 = 325,348 tonnes of H2O
