Observing plant stomata under the microscope

Stomata (singular stoma) are tiny pores on leaf surfaces, which primarily function in gas exchange (CO2 uptake and O2 release) for photosynthesis and transpiration (water vapour loss). The stomata are regulated by guard cells that open and close the pores.

Aim: To observe plant stomata under the microscope.

Obtain your leaf samples from the campus and/or from the barley leaves grown with low water/high water. Any broad flat leaf should work.

Each student should have a leaf and a microscope slide.

Paint a THIN layer of clear nail polish on the underside of the leaf in a 1 cm x 1 cm square. (If you do it too thick it will take forever to dry).

Allow the nail polish to dry completely, and then place a piece of CLEAR cellophane tape over the nail polish. Matte tape will not work. Also, you need to make sure that you are not leaving fingerprints on the tape before use.

Using your fingernail, rub the tape down firmly over the nail polish.

Slowly peel the tape off of the leaf. The nail polish should now be stuck to the tape.

Place the tape directly onto the microscope slide and place it under the microscope. Depending on the leaf type, you will generally need to be on at least 40x to see stomata clearly.
