Two liquids with different bonding properties don’t mix to form a single liquid. The denser of the two liquids sinks.

### **CORE FORMATION SEPARATED MANY ELEMENTS, NOT JUST IRON**

Core formation = Differentiation

Where iron metal and molten silicate are mixed, each element has a preference for which liquid it enters. Iron is split between the two liquids, as metal in the iron liquid and as ions (Fe<sup>2+</sup> ) in the silicate liquid .

**IRON LIQUID** Major elements Fe, Ni, S, Si, O Co, Ru, Pd, Ir, Pt, Au, W, etc. **SIDEROPHILE** : iron-seeking

#### **GOLDSCHMIDT’S CLASSIFICATION OF THE ELEMENTS**

- Classification is based on the preference of the element for a silicate, metal or sulphide _liquid_ . All three can coexist as immiscible liquids.

- Elements with two colours can occur in either of the two liquids.

- Chemical behaviour is related to the way an element bonds.

#### **PREFERENCE FOR ONE LIQUID – PARTITIONING**

- Preference of an element for one of the liquids is called the _partitioning_ behaviour of that element.

- For each element, the degree of preference between two liquids depends on the conditions - such as temperature, pressure, and amount of oxygen available. :

- We can measure the preference in terms of a **_partition coefficient_**

Partition coefficient    = Abundance in liquid 1 Abundance in liquid 2

Abundances are measured as weight percent (wt%) values, or ppm, ppb etc. Partition coefficients have no units.

#### <u>Example:</u>

Metal / silicate partition coefficient for nickel      = <u>wt% Ni in metal</u> wt% Ni in silicate

Partition coefficients (also known as distribution coefficients) are given the symbol D. NiD The example above is: met/sil

#### **Partition coefficients are measured in experiments**

http://www.psrd.hawaii.edu/July05/cobalt_and_nickel.html

Blanchard et al. (2015) Earth and Planetary Science Letters 427, 191–201

**_Experiment consisted of liquid silicate and liquid metal at high temperature and pressure._**

Example:  Metal contains 2.5 wt% Ni, Silicate contains 0.1 wt% Ni.<sup>Ni</sup> D =   25 met/sil

- This means that the ratio of Ni in metal compared with Ni in silicate is 25:1.

- **Siderophile** elements have metal-silicate partition coefficients greater than 1.

#### **Example: Ni content of the Earth’s core**

If we know the Ni content of the mantle, and the partition coefficient for Ni, we can estimate the Ni content of the core.

#### **Lithophile elements – preference for silicate**

The metal / silicate partition coefficient for a lithophile element is less than 1. **Example:** For manganese, Mn,<sup>Mn</sup> D =   0.3 met/sil If there is 1045 ppm Mn in the mantle, how much Mn is in the core? Amount of Mn in core / Amount of Mn in mantle  =   0.3 Amount of Mn in core = 0.3 * Amount in mantle = 0.3 * 1045 = 313 ppm in the core

For a **siderophile** (metal-seeking) element,  Dmet/sil > 1 For a **lithophile** (silicate-seeking) element, Dmet/sil < 1

- In general, geochemically useful trace elements are heavier elements which are in low abundance throughout the Earth (remember nucleosynthesis).

**Models for the formation of Earth’s core.** Misra, Fig. 12.11

- a) Homogeneous accretion. Accretion of undifferentiated material followed by melting and core formation.

b) Heterogeneous accretion. The nature of what is accreting changes with time. Material that accretes late, after core formation, does not lose its siderophile elements to the core.

c) Magma ocean model. Metal separates from molten silicate in the magma ocean, sinks to the bottom of the magma ocean, then sinks as diapirs through the solid mantle into the growing core.

##### **K-Pg boundary at Stevns Klint, Denmark**

Boundary layer

_Stevns Klint in Denmark is a world-famous K-T boundary. It is composed of bryozoan limestone (upper part) and chalk (lower) which are both types of limestone. These layers are separated by a narrow layer of clayish material which formed 65 million years ago. The clay is very rich in iridium – a metal abundant in iron meteorites but rare on Earth. This layer formed as a result of meteorite explosion on the Yucatán Peninsula in Mexico. The event marks the end of the Mesozoic Era and is associated with the mass extinction that also ended the reign of dinosaurs. Dark gray spots are chert nodules and the boulders on the coast are also made of this rock type._
