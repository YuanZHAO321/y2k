### **HOW AND WHY DO ROCKS MELT?**

**Why?** – Rocks begin to melt when the Gibbs free energy of formation of a liquid within the system is less than the Gibbs free energy of the minerals that comprise the rock:

**How?** – Rocks do not have a single “melting point”. They melt over a range of temperatures. The equilibrium composition of the melt at a given temperature and pressure is determined by thermodynamics.

#### **REE: Lanthanide contraction**

- The ionic radius of REE ions with charge 3+ decreases steadily with increasing Z.

- Reason is that the _f_ orbital electrons do not completely shield the charge of the nucleus. As the charge on the nucleus increases, the electrons are pulled closer in.

**PARTITION COEFFICIENTS BETWEEN MINERALS AND MELTS** We have previously looked at partitioning between two liquids (lecture 3). Now we will consider how elements partition between minerals and melts.

: **_Mineral / melt partition coefficient_**

Partition coefficient (D)    = Concentration in mineral Concentration in melt

Concentrations are measured as weight percent (wt%) or ppm values.

#### <u>Example:</u>

Olivine / melt partition coefficient for nickel<sup>Ni</sup> D olv/melt

= <u>wt% Ni in olivine</u> wt% Ni in melt

{D is a ratio so it has no units}

Partitioning occurs because a mineral lattice is favourable or unfavourable for a given ion, depending on the size and charge on the ion and the size and charge of the site in the lattice.

Partition coefficient (D)    = Concentration in mineral Concentration in melt

Compatible element:    D > 1 The element prefers to sit in the mineral. It is _compatible in the mineral_ .

Incompatible element:  D < 1 The element prefers to sit in the melt. It is _incompatible in the mineral_ .
