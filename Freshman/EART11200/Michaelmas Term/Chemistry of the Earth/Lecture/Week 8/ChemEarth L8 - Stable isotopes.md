##### **What are stable isotope studies all about?**

• In geochemical processes, different isotopes of the same element can behave slightly differently.

• The result is that the different phases in a reaction can have different isotope ratios.

• The basic reason for isotope fractionation is the differences in mass between the isotopes, which in turn affects bond strengths, kinetic energy and other factors.

##### **Why do isotopes of the same elements behave differently?**

Isotopes of the same element have the same electron configuration, so we would expect them to have the same chemical behaviour.

<!-- Start of picture text -->
12C 13C<br><!-- End of picture text -->

However, two different types of behaviour result in isotopic fractionation:

Thermodynamic (equilibrium) mass fractionation

Bonding energies between atoms are different depending on the mass of the atom that is bonding.

Kinetic mass fractionation

Kinetic energy of an atom or molecule is a function of mass (K.E. = ½ m v<sup>2</sup> ).

#### **Thermodynamic mass fractionation**

- From Lecture 6 on equilibrium:

- “Internal energy, U – for a mineral this is the sum of potential energy stored in bonds and kinetic energy of atomic vibrations.”

• A bond has a stretching or vibrational frequency. Bonding energies between atoms are different depending on the mass of the atom that is bonding.

###### **Bond vibrations in water**

http://u2.lege.net/John_Keely/keelytech.com/wasser.html

#### **Thermodynamic mass fractionation (cont.)**

• Vibrational frequency is inversely proportional to square root of mass, so bonds to a lighter isotope have a greater vibrational frequency than bonds to a heavier isotope. Bonds with lighter isotopes have higher internal energy and these bonds are more easily disrupted.

- If a molecule takes up the heavier isotope it will have lower

- internal energy, and this reduces the energy available for reaction (DG), i.e. the phase is more stable.

- **Isotopes fractionate between phases based on the bonding**

- **characteristics of each phase.**

**Kinetic effects:** Where reactions do not reach equilibrium, the reaction products are preferentially light because bonds to light atoms are broken more easily.

##### **Kinetic mass fractionation**

Fractionation occurs because kinetic energy is a function of mass.

Kinetic energy  =  ½ m v<sup>2</sup> (m = mass, v = velocity)

At a given temperature, the kinetic energy of all molecules is the same. Hence, a molecule with higher mass has a lower velocity.

Differences in velocity lead to:

- differences in diffusion rates

- differences in evaporation rates – faster molecules escape from a surface more

- easily than slower molecules

For<sup>12</sup> C<sup>16</sup> O and<sup>12</sup> C<sup>18</sup> O,

½ (28) (v28)<sup>2</sup> = ½ (30) (v30)<sup>2</sup>

v 28 = 1.035 v30

i.e., the average velocity of<sup>12</sup> C<sup>16</sup> O molecules is 3.5% greater than that of<sup>12</sup> C<sup>18</sup> O molecules. This is true at any temperature.

##### **Stable isotopes: Geothermometry**

Geothermometry – determining the temperature of a mineral reaction. Very useful for a wide range of geologic applications.

**Example:** (Original idea – H. Urey). If we want to examine the geologic record of climate change, we would like to know the temperature of ancient oceans.
