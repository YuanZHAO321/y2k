### **ELECTRON CONFIGURATION**

The chemical properties of an element depend on the way its electrons are arranged around the nucleus – electron configuration

For a neutral atom,   Number of electrons = Number of protons (= Z)

- Electrons are arranged in **SHELLS** around the nucleus

- • Each shell has one or more **SUBSHELLS**

- Each subshell is composed of one or more **ORBITALS**

- • Each orbital contains a maximum of two electrons

- **s orbitals** are • **p orbitals** consist of two lobes •

- spherical Each p orbital is aligned with a different axis (x, y, z)

- Four sets of **d orbitals** consist of four lobes

- Each d orbital is aligned in a different direction relative to x, y, z axes

- The fifth d orbital (dz<sup>2</sup> ) has a unique shape

### Electron orbitals have different energy levels:

Electrons fill the orbitals in order of increasing energy:

#### **GOLDSCHMIDT’S CLASSIFICATION OF THE ELEMENTS**

**Rock-seeking       Metal-seeking    Sulphide-seeking           Gases**

- Classification is based on the preference of the element for a silicate, metal or sulphide _liquid_ . All three can coexist as immiscible liquids.

- Chemical behaviour is related to the way an element bonds (electron configuration).

#### **PREFERENCE FOR ONE LIQUID – PARTITIONING**

- Preference of an element for one of the liquids is called the _partitioning_ behaviour of that element.

- For each element, the degree of preference between two liquids depends on the conditions - such as temperature, pressure, and amount of oxygen available.

: We can measure the preference in terms of a **_partition coefficient_**

Partition coefficient    = Abundance in liquid 1 Abundance in liquid 2

Abundances are measured as weight percent (wt%) values, or ppm, ppb etc.

<u>Example:</u> Metal / silicate partition coefficient for nickel      = <u>wt% Ni in metal</u> wt% Ni in silicate

Partition coefficients (also known as distribution coefficients) are given the symbol D. NiD The example above is: met/sil

### **COMBINING THE FIRST AND SECOND LAWS**

- First Law: dU = dQ PdV

Second Law: dS  dQ/T Combined: dU  TdS - PdV

Since most geological process occur very slowly and small changes are close to being reversible, we can assume that:

**dU = TdS - PdV FUNDAMENTAL EQUATION of THERMODYNAMICS**

U – internal energy T – temperature in Kelvins S – entropy P – pressure V – volume

**GIBBS FREE ENERGY:** The amount of energy available for a system to do useful work at constant T and P.

G    =   H  - TS

For a change in the system,

D **G   =** D **H  - T** D **S**

When D **G = 0** , the system is in **chemical equilibrium** .

When D **G < 0** (i.e. when DG is negative), there is energy available to produce a spontaneous change. **A chemical reaction will proceed in the direction of lower Gibbs free energy.**

Another useful relationship in thermodynamics:

dG = VdP – SdT

#### **GIBBS PHASE RULE:**

An equation relating the number of phases, components and thermodynamic variables in a system.

**f = c –** f **+ 2**

#### c = components

- f = phases

f = “degrees of freedom”

f – number of intensive variables that can be changed independently, without altering the state of the system, i.e. without changing the number of phases. Also termed “variance”.

- f – usually consider the variables temperature (T) and pressure (P).

- The “2” in the above equation applies to a case where X is fixed, and we are considering changes in T and P.

- If pressure is fixed e.g. in a T vs X diagram, the phase rule becomes: **f = c –** f **+ 1**

Kinetic mass fractionation
