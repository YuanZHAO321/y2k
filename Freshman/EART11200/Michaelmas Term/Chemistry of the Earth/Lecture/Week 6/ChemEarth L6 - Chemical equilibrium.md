### **THE LAWS OF THERMODYNAMICS**

### **FIRST LAW OF THERMODYNAMICS**

Principle of the **conservation of energy** – energy is neither created nor destroyed in chemical reactions and during physical changes.

### **- dU = dQ PdV**

#### **FIRST LAW of THERMODYNAMICS**

**Internal energy, U** : for a mineral this is the sum of potential energy stored in bonds and kinetic energy of atomic vibrations.

A small change in internal energy of the system (dU) is equal to the heat flowing into the system from the surroundings (Q) plus the mechanical work done on the system (W):   dU = dQ +  dW

The most important form of work in geologic systems comes from changes in pressure (P) and volume (V): dW = -PdV.

(Note that McSween uses E instead of U)

**SOME OTHER IMPORTANT CONCEPTS IN THERMODYNAMICS ENTHALPY (H):** The total heat content of a substance.

Units: Joules (J)

H   =    U  +  PV

If a change takes place at constant pressure, the change in enthalpy is equivalent to the heat gained or lost by the system, and

dH = dQ

**GIBBS FREE ENERGY:** The amount of energy available for a system to do useful work at constant T and P.

G    =   H  - TS

Gibbs free energy defines the conditions for equilibrium (reversible) and spontaneous (irreversible) change. Hence, G defines in which direction a spontaneous change will occur.

**Josiah Willard Gibbs** (1839-1903) Wikipedia

**GIBBS FREE ENERGY:** The amount of energy available for a system to do useful work at constant T and P.

### G    =   H  - TS

For a change in the system,

D **G   =** D **H  - T** D **S**

When D **G = 0** , the system is in **chemical equilibrium** .

When D **G < 0** (i.e. when DG is negative), there is energy available to produce a spontaneous change. **A chemical reaction will proceed in the direction of lower Gibbs free energy.**

Another useful relationship in thermodynamics:

dG = VdP – SdT

### **GIBBS PHASE RULE:**

An equation relating the number of phases, components and thermodynamic variables in a system.

**f = c –** f **+ 2**

c = components

- f = phases

- f = “degrees of freedom” or “variance”

f – number of intensive variables that can be changed independently, without altering the state of the system, i.e. without changing the number of phases

- f – usually consider the variables temperature (T) and pressure (P).

- The “2” in the above equation applies to a case where X is fixed, and we are considering changes in T and P (One-component phase diagram)

- If pressure is fixed e.g. in a T vs X diagram, the phase rule becomes: **f = c –** f **+ 1**

**f = c –** f **+ 2**

Where f = 2, we can make a small change in either P or T without changing the state of the system.

Where f = 1, if we make a small change in P we must also make a change in T to keep the same state of the system (and vice versa).

Where f = 0, we cannot change either P or T without changing the state of the system.
