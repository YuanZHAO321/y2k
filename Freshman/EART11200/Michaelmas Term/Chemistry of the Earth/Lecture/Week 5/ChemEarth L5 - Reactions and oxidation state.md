#### **CHEMICAL EQUATION**

A chemical equation describes a reaction or a chemical equilibrium:

A + B  →C + D (reaction) A + B = C + D (equilibrium)

The two sides of the reaction must • have the same number of atoms of each element • have the same charge

Mg + Cl2 → MgCl2 2Na + Cl → 2NaCl 2

#### **CHEMICAL EQUATION**

A chemical equation describes a reaction or a chemical equilibrium:

A + B  →C + D (reaction) A + B = C + D (equilibrium)

The two sides of the reaction must have the same number of atoms of each element, and must have the same charge.

Mg + Cl2 → MgCl2 2Na + Cl → 2NaCl 2

**Balanced equations:**

4Na + O → 2Na O 2 2

2Na + 2H O → 2NaOH + H 2 2

Write the formulae for each substance:

Ca + H2O → Ca(OH)2 + H2

**Step 2**

Check that each element is balanced.

There is one Ca atom on each side – OK, Ca is balanced.

Check for one unbalanced element, for example, O. Adjust the number of each atom or molecule needed, but never change a formula. We need two O atoms on each side:

Ca + 2H2O → Ca(OH)2 + H2

Check the remaining element, H. There are 4 H atoms on each side – OK, equation is now balanced.

**MOLE:** SI unit for “amount of substance”. Unit is written as **mol** .

This number is the **Avogadro constant.**

The **Avogadro constant** is the number of constituent particles, usually atoms or molecules, that are contained in one mole of the substance. (Number of molecules per mole.)

The **Avogadro constant** is defined such that the mass of one mole of a substance, expressed in grams, is equal to the atomic or molecular mass of the substance.

### **OXIDATION – REDUCTION REACTIONS (REDOX REACTIONS)**

Some reactions involve a change in the VALENCE of an element.

Valence – charge on an ion. Example: Fe<sup>2+</sup> (divalent iron ion), Fe<sup>3+</sup> (trivalent iron ion)

Lecture 2

-- Hematite,  Fe2O3 Fe<sup>3+</sup> 2<sup>O2-</sup> 3 Magnetite,  Fe3O4 -- Fe<sup>2+</sup> Fe<sup>3+</sup> 2<sup>O2-</sup> 4

Hematite   =     Magnetite   +   O2 6 Fe O =       4 Fe O +    O 2 3 3 4 2

Valence can also be described in terms of OXIDATION STATE.

Oxidation state of iron is Fe<sup>2+</sup> (ferrous), Fe<sup>3+</sup> (ferric).

The above reaction involves a _change of oxidation state_ , so it is described as an oxidation-reduction reaction or a REDOX reaction.

#### **OXIDATION – REDUCTION REACTIONS (REDOX REACTIONS)**

Hematite        = Magnetite        +   O2 6 Fe<sup>3+</sup> =     4 Fe<sup>2+</sup> Fe<sup>3+</sup> 2<sup>O2-</sup> 3 2<sup>O2-</sup> 4<sup>+   O</sup> 2

• An Fe<sup>3+</sup> ion in hematite gains an electron to form Fe<sup>2+</sup> , and is therefore reduced. Reduction = decrease in oxidation state. Fe<sup>3+</sup> +   e<sup>**-**</sup> =      Fe<sup>2+</sup> (reduction of Fe<sup>3+</sup> )

• In the reverse reaction, the Fe<sup>2+</sup> ion in magnetite loses an electron to form Fe<sup>3+</sup> , and is therefore oxidized. Oxidation = increase in oxidation state. Fe<sup>2+</sup> =      Fe<sup>3+</sup> +   e<sup>**-**</sup> (oxidation of Fe<sup>2+</sup> )

Mineralminers.com

REDOX reactions do not necessarily need to involve oxygen.

Oxidation corresponds to LOSS of an electron (Fe<sup>2+</sup> →   Fe<sup>3+</sup> ) Reduction corresponds to GAIN of an electron (Fe<sup>3+</sup> →   Fe<sup>2+</sup> )

Example 1: This reaction shows how zinc metal can react with copper ions in a solution (aq) to form copper metal and dissolved zinc ions:

Zn +  Cu<sup>2+</sup> s aq<sup>=    Zn2+</sup> aq<sup>+  Cu</sup> s

Zn is oxidized to Zn<sup>2+</sup> Zn  =   Zn<sup>2+</sup> +  2e<sup>**-**</sup> ions (loss of electrons): Cu ions are reduced to Cu (gain of electrons):    Cu<sup>2+</sup> +  2e<sup>**-**</sup> =   Cu

**Half reactions**

Example 2: Reaction of chlorine gas with metallic iron:

2 Fe + 3 Cl =  2 Fe<sup>3+</sup> + 6 Cl<sup>**-**</sup> 2

Fe<sup>0</sup> is oxidized to Fe<sup>3+</sup> 2 Fe  = 2 Fe<sup>3+</sup> + 6 e<sup>**-**</sup> (loss of electrons): **Half reactions** Cl<sup>0= 6 Cl</sup><sup>**-**</sup> 2<sup>is reduced to Cl- (gain of electrons): 3 Cl</sup> 2<sup>+ 6 e</sup><sup>**-**</sup> Note that the charge must balance in redox reactions, as well as the atoms
