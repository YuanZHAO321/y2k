### **ELECTRON CONFIGURATION**

The chemical properties of an element depend on the way its electrons are arranged around the nucleus – electron configuration

For a neutral atom,   Number of electrons = Number of protons (= Z)

- Electrons are arranged in **SHELLS** around the nucleus

- • Each shell has one or more **SUBSHELLS**

- Each subshell is composed of one or more **ORBITALS**

- • Each orbital contains a maximum of two electrons

### Simple view of atom – electrons are in layered shells

http://www.bbc.co.uk/schools/gcsebitesize

### More detailed view – electrons are in orbitals

- **s orbitals** are spherical

- 3 possible **p orbitals** in each p subshell

- each p orbital consist of two lobes

- each p orbital is aligned with a different axis (x, y, z)

http://perso.numericable.fr/vincent.hedberg/atomic_theory/2_atomic_theory_hl.htm

- five possible **d orbitals** in each d subshell

- each d orbital is aligned in a different direction relative to x, y, z axes

- four sets of d orbitals consist of four lobes

- the fifth d orbital (dz<sup>2</sup> ) has a unique shape

http://perso.numericable.fr/vincent.hedberg/atomic_theory/2_atomic_theory_hl.htm

- seven possible **f orbitals** in each f subshell

- shapes are complicated

### **ELECTRON CONFIGURATION CONTROLS CHEMISTRY**

- Only electrons in the outermost orbitals participate in chemical bonding.

- Elements that share the same configuration of electrons in their outer shells have similar chemical properties.

- Groups in the periodic table have the same outer shell electron configuration.

- • The general rule is that filled outer p shells are the most stable.

### **IMPORTANCE OF ELECTRON CONFIGURATION IN THE EARTH**

- Electron configurations control the behaviour of elements in solids (topic for today):

- Mineral lattice structure

- Bonding type (which controls mineral properties such as hardness)

- Mineral compositions

- Trace element substitutions

Electron configurations also control the behaviour of elements:

- In melts (magmas, lavas, the outer core)

- In fluids (hot fluids circulating in the mantle and crust)

- In waters (rivers, lakes, oceans)

- In gases (the atmosphere)

- In living organisms

**Ionic bonds also in Transition Metals, Lanthanides and Actinides**

**Terminology related to ions CATIONS** : Ions with a **POSITIVE** charge **ANIONS** : Ions with a **NEGATIVE** charge **VALENCE** : The number of electrons that an element will give up or accept. **Valence electrons:** Electrons available for chemical bonding. The concept of valence applies to ions with both positive and negative charges: Monovalent ions: Na<sup>+</sup> Cl<sup>-</sup> Divalent ions: Mg<sup>2+</sup> Fe<sup>2+</sup> O<sup>2-</sup> Trivalent ions: Al<sup>3+</sup> Fe<sup>3+</sup> Tetravalent ions: Ti<sup>4+</sup>
