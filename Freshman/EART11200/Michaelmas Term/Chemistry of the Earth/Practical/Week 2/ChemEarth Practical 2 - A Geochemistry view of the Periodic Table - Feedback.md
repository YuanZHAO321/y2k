2. An Angstrom (Å) is a convenient unit for measuring the size of atoms and ions, but it is not an SI unit. An Angstrom is 10<sup>-10</sup> metres. How many nanometres is this?

Nano means 10<sup>-9</sup> , so an Angstrom is 0.1 nm

3. Explain the similarities and differences between Group 1 and Group 2.

The two groups show the same trend of increasing ionic radius with increasing atomic number. The data are for monovalent (1+) ions for Group 1, e.g. Na<sup>+</sup> , and divalent (2+) ions for Group 2, e.g. Mg<sup>2+</sup> . The extra charge in Group 2, which is centred on the nucleus, pulls the electron cloud closer to the nucleus, thus reducing the ionic radius.

4. Which Group 2 ion has the same electron configuration as Na<sup>+</sup> ? What additional element forms a trivalent (3+) ion with the same electron configuration? How would the size of that ion compare with the size of the Na<sup>+</sup> ion?

Mg<sup>2+</sup> and Al<sup>3+</sup> both have the same electron configuration as Na<sup>+</sup> (1s<sup>2</sup> 2s<sup>2</sup> 2p<sup>6</sup> ). With the additional net charge on the nucleus (3+), the Al<sup>3+</sup> ion contracts even more, and is much smaller than Na<sup>+</sup> and Mg<sup>2+</sup> .

### i) What factors might influence the formation of manganese minerals (and minerals of other transition elements) with different charges on the ions?

One of the main factors is the amount of oxygen available. We will talk more about “oxidation state” in a later lecture. If more oxygen is available, a transition element ion will have a higher charge, e.g. when there is plenty of oxygen available, iron will tend to form Fe<sup>3+</sup> rather than Fe<sup>2+</sup> .The amount of oxygen available also influences things like the state of sulphur (e.g. more oxygen results in sulphate ions, SO4<sup>2-</sup> , rather than sulphide, S<sup>2-</sup> ). The charge on a transition element ion also depends on the mineral in which it is substituting. For example, where Mn is substituting for Fe and Mg in a silicate mineral it will probably be Mn<sup>2+</sup> , with the same charge as the main elements in the mineral (Fe<sup>2+</sup> and Mg<sup>2+</sup> ). The size of an ion also has an important effect, for example Mn<sup>3+</sup> would not easily substitute for Al<sup>3+</sup> because it’s much bigger.
