3. **Delta notation,**  is used for comparing an isotope ratio to the ratio measured in a standard.

  =    (Rsample -  Rstandard) / Rstandard  * 1000    ‰

For oxygen isotopes (<sup>18</sup> O and<sup>16</sup> O), the expression is:

## <sup>18</sup> O  =    ((<sup>18</sup> O/<sup>16</sup> O)sample - (<sup>18</sup> O/<sup>16</sup> O)SMOW) / (<sup>18</sup> O/<sup>16</sup> O)SMOW ) * 1000  ‰

### **B. Stable isotope geothermometry: Determining the temperature of equilibration of two phases using their stable isotope compositions.**

**1. Oxygen isotope equilibria:** We will determine the oxygen isotope composition of muscovite in a rock that equilibrated with water. The water has a known <sup>18</sup> O value and the temperature of the reaction is known. (This is taken from Question 6 of Misra, p. 278.)

Fractionation of oxygen isotopes between muscovite and water in the temperature range 500 – 800 °C is described by the equation:

1000ln musc-H2O  =  ((1.90 * 10<sup>6</sup> )/T<sup>2</sup> )  -  3.10

If the water has <sup>18</sup> O = -9 ‰, and the temperature is 500 °C, what is the value of <sup>18</sup> O for the muscovite? (Remember that T must be in Kelvin)

T = 500 + 273 = 773 K

1000ln musc-H2O  =   ((1.90 * 10<sup>6</sup> ) / 773<sup>2</sup> )  -  3.10  =  0.0798

1000ln musc-H2O  =    musc – H2O  = musc – H2O

0.0798     =     musc –  (-9) musc     =    0.0798 – 9     =    -8.9202  ‰

Remember to include units.

c) What is the temperature of formation of the lead-zinc sulphide deposit? How might we interpret the different temperatures given by different samples?

The temperatures recorded by different samples vary between 270 and 375 °C.  Differences might arise because the temperature does truly vary across the deposit. Temperature might vary with space and / or time.

Also, there is a sampling problem. It is difficult to select samples that represent equilibrium between the two minerals. The sphalerite crystals analyzed were strongly zoned because they grew in successive generations of fluid. The paper by Rye (1974) discusses the problems selecting sphalerite and galena samples that represent equilibrium pairs. So there could be some uncertainty because of sampling.

There is an uncertainty associated with measuring the values of <sup>34</sup> S in the laboratory. The Rye paper states that “the uncertainty of calculated temperatures due to uncertainty for <sup>34</sup> Ssphal-gal values (±0.1 ‰) is about ±15°C at 350°C.” The measured temperature range is much greater than this error.

We could take the average of all four analyses and say that the formation temperature is 322 °C, with a standard deviation of 43. However, the number of analyses is small so taking an average is not very meaningful. Overall, it should be clear that just measuring one mineral pair is not enough.
