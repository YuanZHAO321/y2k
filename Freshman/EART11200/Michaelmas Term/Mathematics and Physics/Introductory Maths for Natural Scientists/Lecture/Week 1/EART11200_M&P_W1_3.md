## Avogadro’s number

The mass of a proton (1.66 E-24 g) is so small that we need another way to express quantities of atoms, for example a defined number of atoms we can measure in grams.

Take hydrogen, the simplest, smallest atom of just one proton and one electron. How many hydrogen atoms would weigh one gram? (# of H atoms in 1 gram) x (mass of 1 H atom) = 1 gram A 1/ (mass of 1 H atom) = (# of H atoms in 1 gram) = 6.02E23 = N This is known as Avagadro’s number.

_This scales with mass number, A:_  24  _N_  _A_  .166  10 _A_  1 _A_ gram

`Atomic mass’ (mass of 1 mole of an element)

_So `A_ ’ _grams of every element  contains Avogadros_ ’ _number of atoms. Which leads to the concept of atomic mass._

## <mark>Molecular mass</mark>

The concept of atomic mass extends to molecules too.

_Sum of mass nos, means add up the atomic masses of all atoms in molecule_

 s 24 -1 _N A_  sum of mass no  .166  10  molecular mass[g mole ]
