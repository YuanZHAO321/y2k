# The ideal gas law

The atmosphere is made up of a mixture of gases. An ideal gas obeys the law PV = NR T gas (PV/T = constant) where P is pressure, V is volume of the gas, T is temperature (in Kelvin), N is number of is the universal gas moles of the gas, and R gas constant (8.314 JK<sup>-1</sup> mol<sup>-1</sup> ) Throughout most of the atmosphere we can assume the ideal gas law is obeyed.

# Be careful with units!

Look back at week 1 if you need to remind yourself about moles. Pressure units: atmospheric pressure is expressed in SI units of hPa (hectoPascal), which is equivalent to the mbar (millibar). Convert to Pascals in calculations (see week 1). Temperatures must always be expressed in Kelvin for calculations

## Composition of atmosphere by moles

The atmosphere is a mixture of gases.

Each gas exerts a partial pressure _pi_ The ideal gas law holds for each gas: _V_ = _N RT pi i_

The total pressure is the sum of partial pressures:

_p=p1+p2+p3+…+pN_

We can expand on this idea to reach the concept of ppmv (parts per million by volume), often used to express the concentration of pollutants in the atmosphere.

_R_ =8.314 J K<sup>-1</sup> mol<sup>-1</sup>

_T_ =temperature

_P_ =pressure

_V=_ volume

# Dalton’s law of partial pressures

This states that the total pressure exerted by a mixture of gases is equal to the sum of the pressures exerted by each individual gas.

_p=p1+p2+p3+…+pN = Σpi_
