# Structural Geology

- Structural geology is the study of the threedimensional distribution of rock units with respect to their deformational histories.

- Today we will look at a **seam of gold** , which is a plane of rock high in Au.

- Examples of planes:

   - Bedding, cleavage, joint surfaces, fault planes, fold axial planes, quarry faces

Geologists will measure some of these properties using a COMPASS-CLINOMETER, which you will have seen

## Geometries: Measurement conventions

- The inclination of a planar structure in geology is measured by strike and dip.

   - The **strike** is the line of **intersection between the planar feature and a horizontal plane** , taken according to the right hand convention,

   - the **dip** is the magnitude of the **inclination, below horizontal** , at **right angles** to strike.

      - For example; striking 25 degrees East of North, dipping 45 degrees Southeast, recorded as N25E,45SE.

   -

• **Alternatively, dip and dip direction may be used as this is absolute** . Dip direction is measured in 360 degrees, generally clockwise from North. For example, a dip of 45 degrees towards 115 degrees from north, recorded as 45/115.
