Question 4

Q4.1: Before you look at Tysall and colleague’s data, do you expect the latency to mate to be normally distributed within groups? Why or why not. No. Normal distributions have long tails in both directions. Latency cannot be less than zero, so the distribution of latencies cannot have a long left tail. And, it is truncated at 2 hours on the right, so it cannot have a long right tail. So, unless all of the latencies are far from zero and far from 2 hours (ie, no flies mated very quickly and none failed to mate), the distribution of latencies cannot be normal. More generally, waiting times tend to be right-skewed. This is true because, if the probability of the event occurring is constant, then many events will occur early in trials because there are lots of individuals waiting for events, and fewer will occur later in trials when most individuals have already experienced the event and are no longer waiting.
