Question 5

Q5.1: Test the hypotheses that i) controlling for pronotum width, males have larger tibial plates than females; and ii) the size of the tibial plate increases with pronotum width faster in males than in females. Report your results clearly and concisely. Does your result support the hypothesis that tibial plates are a signal of male quality in the matador bug? Area of the tibial plate increased with pronotum width (1.99 log(cm2)/log(cm); p < 0.0001). We found weak evidence that males had smaller tibial plates than females (-0.052 log(cm2); p = 0.0848). We found no significant evidence that the size of the tibial plate changes differently in male and female matador bugs (p = 0.2068). This does not support the hypothesis that tibial plates are signals of male mate quality in this species.

Question 6

Q6.1: Test the hypothesis that tibia length in A. alipes changes with forewing length. Report your results clearly and concisely. Logged tibia length increased with logged forewing length (0.5085; p < 0.0001).

Q6.2: Test the hypothesis that tibia length in A. alipes changes with hindwing length. Report your results clearly and concisely. Logged tibia length increased with logged hindwing length (0.4584; p < 0.0001).

Q6.3 (Super-challenge): Test the hypothesis that tibia length in A. alipes changes with hindwing length when controlling for forewing length. Report your results clearly and concisely. Can you explain why the result in Q6.3 is different from the result in Q6.1? (The answer is not just that forewing length is in the model – that is given. The question is, why does that matter?) We found no evidence that tibia length increased with hindwing length when forewing length was in the model (p = 0.8632). This is because forewing length and hindwing length are strongly correlated – they contain much of the same information. So, we cannot learn anything about tibia length from hindwing length that we have not already learned from forewing length.
