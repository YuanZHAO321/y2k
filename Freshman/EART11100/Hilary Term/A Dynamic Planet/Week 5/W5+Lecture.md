## Slide 19

- Summary

- The Earth’s crust can be resolved into one of three end-member  “Andersonian” tectonic states: Extension, Compression or Shearing.

- Where stresses are sufficient to strain the crust,

- Extension leads to normal faulting or stretching (thinning)

- Compression leads to thrust faulting or folding (thickening)

- Shearing leads to strike-slip faulting and fault-bending (no thickness change).

## Slide 27

- Behaviour of the lithosphere

- The lithosphere is rigid enough to comprise a number of relatively coherent plates, that move relative to each other as a result of asthenospheric deformation.

- The asthenosphere is weak, and undergoes low rates of ductile deformation as a result of thermal gradients (convection).

## Slide 28

- Behaviour of the lithosphere

- There are three main mechanisms for uplift and subsidence (basin formation)

- But often all three mechanisms operate simultaneously

- Isostasy – the consequences of changes in lithospheric thickness

- Flexural loading (and unloading) of the lithosphere

- Dynamic effects – effects of flow in the mantle

## Slide 29

- Behaviour of the lithosphere

- 1. Isostasy – the consequences of changes in lithospheric thickness

- The lithosphere can be modelled as a less dense, rigid “blocks”  of continent and ocean floating in a “fluid” mantle. I.e the mantle behaves as a Newtonian Fluid.

## Slide 30

- Behaviour of the lithosphere

- Because   “a body immersed in a fluid is buoyed up by a force equal to the weight [volume] of the displaced fluid”…

- …changes in thickness of the floating blocks will result in changes in buoyant force acting upward upon it.

- Where

- b=thickness of the “root” of the mountain belt

- h=the height (asl) of the mountain belt

- ρm and ρc are the mantle and crustal densities

- (“Airy” isostacy model)

## Slide 31

- Behaviour of the lithosphere

- What are the consequences for surface topography when changes in thickness occur to continental crust?

- …changes in density of the floating blocks will result in changes in buoyant force acting upward upon it.

- Where

- ρp=density of lithospheric column, at a height, h, ASL

- ρ0=reference density for the lithosphere

- W=compenation depth

- (“Pratt” isostacy model)

## Slide 32

- Behaviour of the lithosphere

- 2.  Flexural loading (and unloading) of the lithosphere

- In reality the lithospheric plates are not made up of discrete blocks that behave independently of eachother.

- The lithosphere is rigid enough to resist changes in the load caused by thickening /thinning or density contrasts over wavelengths of < 100 km.

- But changes in load over wavelengths of > 100 km are fully compensated for by elastic flexure of the lithosphere

- “Local” isostacy

- Flexure

## Slide 33

- Behaviour of the lithosphere

- Up to 10,000 years ago, Scotland was loaded with a major Ice Cap.

- Now that it has melted, the “Airy Isostacy” model would have Scotland “rebounding” upwards”

- But… southern England is also sinking downward in response to Scotland uplifting.

- This is because the crust does not behave as individual blocks.

- “Local” isostacy

- Flexure

## Slide 34

- Behaviour of the lithosphere

- 3. Dynamic effects – effects of flow in the mantle

- This is the easiest to understand….

- Upwelling of hotter, less dense (and more voluminous) material in the mantle results in upward flexure of the overlying lithosphere

- Sinking of cooler, more dense (and less voluminous) material results in downward withdrawal of the lithosphere.
