## Slide 16

- Think of the polar vortex as an extension of the jet stream in the stratosphere

- O3 hole time line, 1

**Notes:**

1. Chlorine and nitrogen compounds in the stratosphere react to form stable compounds such as HCl and ClONO2.
2. These are transported towards the poles
3. During the cold polar winter, a strong descent of air occurs and the core of the vortex becomes separated from the air farther equatorward.
4. The vortex core becomes cold enough for polar stratospheric clouds (nitric acid and water ice crystals) to form. (1) in the figure above
5. These clouds provide surfaces for reactions that release the Cl into more active forms such as Cl2 and HOCl. (2)
6. These species build up over the polar winter in the dark.  As clouds of nitric acid and water ice form, the stratosphere becomes denitrified and dehydrated.

Following sunrise in spring:
1. These compounds are photolysed to form Cl, ClO and other highly reactive compounds. (3)
2. The Cl compounds act as the X in the modification of the Chapman cycle and can catalytically destroy O3. (4)
3. The Cl compounds are regenerated and so catastrophic loss of ozone can occur.
4. Since there is no ozone in the polar vortex then there is no absorption of UV and so the stratosphere cannot readily warm.  This maintains the vortex and prevents ozone rich air from lower latitudes to mix into the core and extends the lifetime of the vortex and the period of low ozone.

## Slide 22

- OH is an important oxidant in the atmosphere.

- OH has a short lifetime in the atmosphere (1 s) and nearly constant concentrations (reformed as quickly as it is destroyed).

- Presence of OH in troposphere helps convert NO2 (primary pollutant) to HNO3 (secondary pollutant), and remove it from the gas phase.

- Other pollutants (e.g., methane, carbon monoxide, VOCs) have similar reactions with OH, thus cleansing the atmosphere of them.

**Notes:**

NO2 decreases because of presence of OH.

During daylight hours, a photochemical reaction occurs in the atmosphere where different wavelengths of light interact with water and terpenes (secreted from plants) in the air to produce simpler by-products known as Reactive Oxygen Species (ROS). One of the main types of ROS is the hydroxyl radical.
In addition, during the entire 24-hour cycle, OH is formed through the reaction between terpenes and ozone.”

## Slide 46

- Friction and the Coriolis force both lead to the current being at a right angle to the wind.

- To the right of the wind in the NH; left of the wind in the SH.

- Ekman spiral

- Northern Hemisphere (NH)

- NOAA Ocean Service

**Notes:**

http://oceanservice.noaa.gov/education/kits/currents/media/supp_cur05e.html

The Ekman spiral, named after Swedish scientist Vagn Walfrid Ekman (1874-1954) who first theorized it in 1902, is a consequence of the Coriolis effect. When surface water molecules move by the force of the wind, they, in turn, drag deeper layers of water molecules below them. Each layer of water molecules is moved by friction from the shallower layer, and each deeper layer moves more slowly than the layer above it, until the movement ceases at a depth of about 100 meters (330 feet). Like the surface water, however, the deeper water is deflected by the Coriolis effect—to the right in the Northern Hemisphere and to the left in the Southern Hemisphere. As a result, each successively deeper layer of water moves more slowly to the right or left, creating a spiral effect. Because the deeper layers of water move more slowly than the shallower layers, they tend to “twist around” and flow opposite to the surface current.
