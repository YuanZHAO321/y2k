## Slide 3

- What is metamorphism?

- From “meta” (change) and “morph” (shape)

- Changes in the mineralogy and texture (shape and size of minerals) that occur in the solid state (strictly no melting).

- Response to the addition of thermal energy (heat) and mechanical energy (work)… change to an equilibrium state.

- It doesn’t change the bulk chemical composition of the rock

-  it is a closed system process.

-  i.e. one that involves transfer of energy but not material.

- The exceptions are…

-  Volatiles (H2O & CO2) are lost (H2O abundant in crystal structure of clays, CO2). 	They can also sometime be added.

-  Metasomatism – metamorphism caused by the addition or removal of 	elements by the pumping of hot, saturated pore waters.

- What is metamorphism?

- Metamorphic rocks are volumetrically more abundant in the Earth’s crust (and upper mantle) than igneous and sedimentary rocks put together…

## Slide 7

- What is metamorphism?

- All types of igneous and sedimentary rocks can (potentially) be subjected to metamorphism.

- Metamorphic rocks can be derived from any kind of precursor rock (protolith) and therefore collectively show a very large range in bulk compositions.

- A rule of thumb is that increasing metamorphic grade = increases size of crystals (but depends on grain size of protolith, obviously!).

## Slide 12

- Causes of metamorphism: stress

- Stress (S) is defined as the average force per unit area that some particle of a body exerts on an adjacent particle, across an imaginary surface that separates them

- i.e. Stress (S) = Force (F) / Area (A).

- It is intuitive that a cube of sandstone with sides of 25 cm submitted to a compressive force of 10 tonnes (10,000 kg) may deform insignificantly, compared to the same force being applied to a cube of the same material but with sides 1cm (which likely would be pulverised).

- Therefore the behaviour of material is measured against units of stress (Pascals=Newtons/m2) rather than force.

## Slide 16

- Causes of metamorphism: stress

- Compressive force at convergent plate margins  deviatoric stress.

- Extensional force at divergent plate margins  deviatoric stress.

- Results in fracturing (faulting) in cold, brittle upper crust.

- Results in plastic deformation (folding) in warm ductile middle & lower crust. (Heat reduces the strength of rock.)

- Hot rocks are weak, so they resolve (and dissipate) deviatoric stresses by deforming plastically.

- So deviatoric stresses have minimal effect on mineral stabilities…

## Slide 23

- Metamorphic facies

- Metamorphic facies are sets of mineral assemblages that form under distinct pressure and temperature fields.

- Broadly, we can divide metamorphism into three categories

- Regional metamorphism, where T and P increase.

- Contact metamorphism where T increases and P does not.

- Dynamic metamorphism where P increases but T does not.

## Slide 35

- Dynamic metamorphism

- Dynamic metamorphism happens when stress pressure is applied, but little temperature.

- This stress is usually deviatoric (not burial – so we don’t plot on a Temp./Depth profile), and is common…

- Along faults, in the immediate zone where stress is focussed.

- Usually results in grain size reduction:

- Brittle grain size reduction (cracking, no recrystallisation)

-   cataclastic rocks (breccia, etc.)

- Ductile grain size reduction (dynamic recrystallisation)

-   mylonites

## Slide 40

- Why don’t the minerals retrograde?

- Retrograde reactions generally fail to happen because ...

- 1.	rocks lose energy as they cool, so do not receive the necessary activation energy for the reaction to happen.

- many prograde reactions involve release of volatiles

- (devolatilisation reactions):

- dehydration reactions:  involve release of H2O

- decarbonation reactions:  involve release of CO2

- (e.g. 	calcite + quartz  wollastonite + CO2)

- But once lost from the rock, the volatiles are not available for the retrograde reaction:

- wollastonite + CO2  calcite + quartz

- This is useful, because it allows us to look at the metamorphic rocks and reconstruct the processes happening deep in the Earth’s crust.
