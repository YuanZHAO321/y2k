## Slide 20

- The Rock Cycle

- The Rock cycle is the concept that rocks change their physical and chemical properties when they are forced out of their equilibrium conditions.

- Changes in T and P, mainly driven by tectonics

- Aided by the hydrological cycle, gravity, and sometimes life!

- As with all (bio) geochemical cycles, it is not a simple “circular” cycle

## Slide 21

- Recap: Structure of the Solid Earth

- Differentiated planet – undergone partial melting during formation.

- “Popular” “chemical” model

- Based on major changes in composition

- Crust, Mantle and Core (inner and outer)

- “Mechanical” model

- Based on changes in the “rheological” (m. study of flow) behaviour

- Lithosphere, Asthenosphere, Outer      Core and Inner Core

- The latter more useful for understanding tectonics and the Rock Cycle

## Slide 22

- Recap: Structure of the Solid Earth

- The asthenosphere is weak, and undergoes low rates of ductile deformation as a result of thermal gradients (convection) – it is plastic.

- Overlying lithosphere is rigid enough to comprise a number of relatively coherent plates, that move relative to each other as a result of asthenospheric deformation – it is brittle.

- Divergent: leading to thinning of the lithosphere

- Convergent: leading to thickening of the lithosphere

- Conservative: little change in lithospheric thickness
