## Slide 11

- Carbonates

- The major biological sediment, and sedimentary rock type generated in marine (and lake) settings are carbonates.

- Carbonates are mainly limestone (CaCO3) and dolomite (CaMg(CO3)2) (others exist).

- precipitation largely mediated biologically, by the extraction of Ca, Mg, C and O dissolved in water.

- Much less commonly, some organisms form shells from silica (SiO2), e.g. radiolaria.

- The skeletons are grown to provide the organism with a rigid structure, or to protect it (a home).

## Slide 12

- Carbonates

- Because production is largely biologically mediated, production needs:

- Light (the photic zone, absence of 	suspended mud)

- Warm water

- and is inhibited by

- unusual (low) pH

- unusual salinities

- Thus, carbonate is produced (primarily) in shallow marine environments

## Slide 24

- Carbonate Ramps

- Carbonate ramps happen when

-  Carbonate production is LOWER

-  Water is cooler, water is contaminated by clastics, or the water is brackish 	or hypersaline (the carbonate platform is less “happy”).

- Morphologically, they are characterised by

- A ramp that dips uniformly towards much deeper water

- No barrier reef, just local patches of reef in places

- Lower carbonate productivity means that the sediment is more readily reworked and moved away from shallow water by wave and tidal processes.

## Slide 27

- Evaporites

- Chemical sediments are precipitated directly from sea and lake water

-  without being mediated by biogenic activity

- it is wholly inorganic.

- Direct precipitation of dissolved minerals from seas and lakes occurs because the water becomes supersaturated with respect to that mineral.

- How does this supersaturation happen?

## Slide 28

- Evaporites

- In areas of excess evaporation, versus inflow of fresh (rain) water from rivers, dissolved minerals become hyperconcentrated.

- These minerals are predominantly minerals of common Earth surface metals

- Calcite and polymorphs (CaCO3) and dolomite (CaMg(CO3)2)

- The salt group (Halogens) – NaCl (halite), KCl (sylvite), MgCl, etc.

- Gypsum (CaSO4·2H2O) and anhydrite (CaSO4).

- The Dead Sea has is hyperconcentrated in halite (table salt), at 40ppm (5ppm more than normal oceans). This is enough to promote inorganic precipitation of salt.

## Slide 29

- Evaporites

- If Oceans were perfectly mixed, these pools of hyperconcentrated water would be mixed around.

- However, hypersalinity develops readily in

- seaways (and especially lakes) that have poor/no connection to the open ocean,

- and experience an excess of evaporation versus inflow of fresh water.
