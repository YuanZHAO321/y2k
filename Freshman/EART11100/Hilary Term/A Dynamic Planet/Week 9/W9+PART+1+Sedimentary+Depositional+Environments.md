## Slide 5

- The difference between an erosional and depositional landscape

- Because erosional landscapes are dominated by UPLIFT, and WEATHERING AND EROSION, they

-  tend to have topographic relief, and

-  are dominated surfaces covered in weathering products (“regloith”), or

-  or dominated by bare eroding bedrock.

- By contrast, depositional landscapes are dominated by SUBSIDENCE and DEPOSITION of sediment, are

-  low areas, either flooded by the lakes or sea, if they haven’t been filled with sediment, or

-  very flat (little relief) if they have been filled up with sediment.

-  characterised by bedrock buried under 10 to 100s m of sediment.

## Slide 7

- Where does deposition happen?

- When weathering and erosion generate clastic sediment (W7), it is transported, and then ultimately deposited somewhere else.

- Deposition happens because the capacity of the transporting medium (usually water, but also wind) to carry the sediment decreases.

- This can happen locally for short periods of time, anywhere.

## Slide 8

- Where does deposition happen?

- However, for the long-term storage of sediment, we need to generate space for that sediment to be deposited in.

- This “space” is often referred to as “accommodation”, and is generated by subsidence of the Earth’s crust (W5).

- Long term storage of sediment occurs in sedimentary basins

-  the subsidence of the sedimentary basin also ensures burial of the sediment with later sediment

-  this burial takes the sediment to higher pressures and temperatures, where it can also be converted from sediment into a sedimentary rock.

## Slide 9

- Where does deposition happen?

- The modern global distribution of subsiding areas (sedimentary basins) is shown in green.

- These are areas where there is potential to accumulate sediment long-term (at the moment).

- Other areas are actively uplifting, or are still “high”, and sources of clastic sediment.

- These include areas on land, and in the sea, close to continents.

- The “deep” ocean (floored by ocean crust) is not considered basin, because

- there are few mechanisms for getting much sediment that far out.

- oceanic crust with any sediment on will subduct into the mantle anyway.

## Slide 10

- Where does deposition happen?

- As with the whole Earth System, deposition is governed by a dynamic equilibrium

- between the amount of sediment delivered, and the amount of space made available to deposit the sediment by subsidence.

- Erosion is primarily driven by fluvial (but also glacial and wind) processes in high areas (W7).

- As transporting river systems enter a sedimentary basin, it will begin to deposit its sediment load to “fill” the available space.

- if the volume of space being generated ≥ volume of sediment being delivered, then all the sediment will be deposited.

-  if the volume of space being generated < volume of sediment being delivered, then the excess sediment will continue its journey down system to a place where there is sufficient space.

## Slide 28

- Clastic depositional environments: deep water

- Submarine (“deep water”) fans are the largest sedimentary deposits on Earth.

- They are the final resting place for any particle that has been eroded from a “high area”.

- The processes occurring in these deep water conditions were a mystery until very recently

- They pose a major risk to submarine infrastructure.
