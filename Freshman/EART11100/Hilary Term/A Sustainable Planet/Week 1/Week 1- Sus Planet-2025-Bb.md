## Slide 27

### Sustainable cities – key facts

- Half of humanity – 3.5 billion people – lives in cities today and 5 billion people are projected to live in cities by 2030.

- 95% of urban expansion in the next decades will take place in developing world

- 883 million people live in slums today and most them are found in Eastern and South-Eastern Asia.

- The world’s cities occupy just 3% of the Earth’s land, but account for 60-80% of energy consumption and 75% of carbon emissions.

- 90% of urban dwellers breathe unsafe air, resulting in 4.2 million deaths due to ambient air pollution. >50% of the global urban population were exposed to air pollution >2.5 times higher than the safety standard (data from 2016).

## Slide 43

### Life on land – key facts

- Around 1.6 billion people depend on forests for their livelihood, including 70 million indigenous people.

- Forests contain more than 80% of all terrestrial species.

- Between 2010 and 2015, the world lost 3.3 million hectares of forest.

- 2.6 billion people depend directly on agriculture, but 52% of the land used for agriculture is moderately or severely affected by soil degradation.

- Over 80% of the human diet is provided by plants. Only three cereal crops – rice, maize and wheat (all grasses) – provide 60% of energy intake.

- Microorganisms and invertebrates, especially those in soil, are key to ecosystem services, but their contributions are still poorly known and rarely acknowledged

## Slide 59

### What did this paper find?

- It focused on ways in which humanity could USE CO2, as opposed to reducing the emissions, as part of a climate mitigation strategy

- The paper estimated the amount of CO2 that could potentially be used from 10 broad technologies: (1) CO2-based chemical products, including polymers; (2) CO2-based fuels; (3) microalgae fuels and other microalgae products; (4) concrete building materials; (5) CO2 enhanced oil recovery (CO2-EOR); (6) bioenergy with carbon capture and storage (BECCS); (7) enhanced weathering; (8) forestry techniques, including afforestation/reforestation, forest management and wood products; (9) land management via soil carbon sequestration techniques; and (10) biochar.

- Lots of uncertainty!

- But certainly lots of potential e.g. forestry may remove 500-3600 MT of CO2 per year  in 2050...
