## Slide 14

### Kimberlite pipes

- Diamonds form at:

- high pressures (above 30 kb) at depths of 150 km

- high temperatures (1000°C)

- Kimberlites = igneous rocks highly enriched in magnesium and volatiles (CO2 and H2O)

- Originate in the Earth’s mantle (150+ km depth)

- Form ‘pipes’ as magma transports diamonds to the surface

- Also form in Lamproite pipes (shallower deposits) and at the site of asteroid impacts (Popigai, Russia)

- Secondary deposits = diamond placers

## Slide 15

- Of the kimberlites discovered to date, only 3% are significantly diamondiferous (i.e., containing macrodiamonds at a grade >1 carat per hundred tons)

- Less than 100 kimberlites have been commercially mined for diamonds

### Oil Shales/Sands

- Fine-grained rocks containing significant quantities of kerogen

- Formed in swamps and shallow marine environments

- Kerogen-waxy, insoluble, contains a mixture of heavy hydrocarbons, can be converted to oil by heating above 500°C

- Major oil shales are in USA and Russia

## Slide 73

### Coalbed methane

- CBM is extracted by releasing pressure in coal seams, usually by natural gas production or by pumping water from the coal bed. As the pressure is reduced, gas is released and can be extracted.

- Most of the gas is adsorbed on the surface of the coal: it can contain two to three times more gas per unit of rock volume than conventional gas deposits.

## Slide 74

### Shale gas

- Shale gas is extracted from "source rock“ - has naturally low permeability.

- The gas is held in fractures, pore spaces and adsorbed on to the organic material of shale.

- ‘Crack’ the rock using hydraulic fracturing method 'fracking'.

- Fracturing fluid, a combination of water and sometimes chemicals, is pumped at high pressure into the rock to create narrow fractures that allow the gas to flow
