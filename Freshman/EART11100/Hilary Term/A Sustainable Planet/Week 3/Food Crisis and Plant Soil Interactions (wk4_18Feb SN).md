## Slide 64

- Example short answer question:

- 1. You are a consultant advising farmers on ways to reduce greenhouse gas emissions from their croplands: what advice would you give to reduce nitrous oxide (N2O) emissions and why?

- Nitrous oxide (N2O) is a potent greenhouse gas and it is vital to reduce emissions from agricultural lands.  The production of N2O is a microbial-driven process undertaken by soil bacteria.  There a several factors that control the production of N2O in soils, some of which can be managed by agricultural practices.  The most important control is oxygen availability. N2O is produced in soil when oxygen concentrations are low, and this usually occurs when soil pores are full of water. Therefore, ensuring fields are well-drained will help ensure soils remain aerobic, and prevent production of N2O.  Other factors that increase water holding capacity of soils may also help, for example maintaining good soil structure and preventing compaction of soils.

- N2O is also dependent on the availability of a suitable substrate. Nitrate (NO3) is the most important substrate because this is required for denitrification to occur. Since most fertilisers contain nitrate, the over-use of fertiliser N may stimulate N2O production when the conditions allow. Fertilisers that contain NH4 (such as many organic wastes) may also be a problem because conversion of NH4 to NO3 (nitrification) can generate N2O.  Thus, efficient and modest use of all fertilisers, organic and inorganic, should be recommended.
