### Biodiversity

- The degree of variation in life

- Dependent on SCALE. Gives rise to:

- alpha diversity (within a habitat, ecosystem etc)

- beta diversity (between ecosystem comparison –the number of unique species)

- gamma diversity (within a region, with several ecosystems)

## Slide 13

### Biodiversity

- The number of species (species richness)

- The relative abundance of species (evenness)

- diversity sensu stricto uses an index of both richness and evenness, e.g. Shannon index

- H’ = diversity; pi = evenness of species i; s = richness

- Range form quite specific to quite broad – user defined.

- e.g. grasses; forbs; sedges; heathers

## Slide 42

### Phosphorus is ‘sticky’

- It is strongly absorbed to the surfaces of particles by ionic forces

- It forms complex molecules, some of which are very insoluble or chemically complex

- Phosphorus is more difficult for plants to take-up than e.g. nitrate

- As a result, applications of phosphorus to soil can build-up, leading to a legacy effect if the additions stop

### Remember the link between nitrogen availability of phosphatase activity?

- Elemental cycles (C, N and P for example) do not operate independently

- E.g. P demand is related to N supply. Plant growth can be co-limited by N and P, as well as by each of the elements

- So adding N to a system (on purpose or accidently) may have consequences for how P is cycled

## Slide 54

### Summary

- Biodiversity in its broad sense encompasses many aspects of variation in life (genes, genotypes, species, genera, functional types).

- Some of this variation is effectively captured by measuring ‘traits’

- Plant functional type has a key role in determining free-living and symbiotic microbial function and diversity in grasslands.

- The mechanisms behind these relationships are poorly understood

- The nature of the relationship between diversity and function is not always positive and linear

- Biodiversity is being valued increasingly by society and politicians, and even by economists.
