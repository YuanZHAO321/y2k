## Slide 18

### Sedimentary Deposits

- Three types:

- 1) Bog Iron Deposits

- Small, no longer mined but historically important

- Fe mobilised as Fe2+ in swamps (“bogs”)

- Transported in organic–rich near–surface waters

- Enter oxygen–rich lake (or coastal waters) oxidize to Fe3+ and precipitate

- Ore: Goethite (FeOOH) and magnetite (Fe3O4)

## Slide 19

- 2)  Ironstones

- Continuous sedimentary beds – extend 100’s km

- Mostly shallow near-shore marine sediments

- Shallow-oxidising Ores:Goethite, hematite

- Deeper-more reducing- Ores: siderite and chamosite

- Iron transported in fluids and replaced calcium carbonate, fossils or oolites

- Phanerozoic-type deposits

- Goethite ooids in an ooidal ironstone (Lr Jurassic)

- Musselband Ironstone-non-marine bivalve, Scotland

## Slide 20

### 3) Banded Iron formations (BIFs)

- Bulk of the worlds iron ore reserves

- Grade 30-50% Fe

- Occur only in Precambrian rocks 1.8-3.8 billion years ago

- Large: 30-700m thick and can be 1000’s km extent

## Slide 21

### BIFs: mineralogy and origin

- Origin is somewhat enigmatic

- Marine basin setting

- Runoff from continents of Fe2+ rich weathering and erosion material also from submarine hydrothermal fluids

- Free oxygen was rare or absent on the Precambrian Earth

- Oxygen (produced by cyanobacteria or photoferrotrophs) was combining with abundant iron in oceans of the early Earth to form BIFs

- Formation of abundant BIFs stopped once the atmosphere contained more oxygen from bacterial photosynthesis

- Fine-grained magnetite/ hematite with quartz, Fe silicate and Fe carbonate minerals

- Later metamorphosis of most due to age

- Possible model for BIF deposition

- Deformed BIF

## Slide 35

### Sediment-hosted Mn ore deposits

**Notes:**

Manganese ore deposits, all dating from the Oligocene, form an arc around the north side of the Black Sea, stretching from Turkey through Bulgaria, the Ukraine, Russia and Georgia and finally in Kazakhstan. They constitute the third great episode of Mn deposition, in the Early Oligocene.
Black Sea deposits (click for larger image). Not shown is Mangyshlak in Kazakhstan.
Table of average ore compositions
 
 
These and most other large Mn deposits likely formed around the margins of euxinic basins (those with free H2S in deep water). Such basins -- exemplified by the modern Black Sea -- have a thin zone of oxygen-containing water overlying a large reservoir of oxygen-free, sulfidic water. Elements like Mn that are soluble under reducing, sulfidic conditions but insoluble under oxidizing conditions precipitate along the interface between these water masses. Generally the Mn solids simply sink back into the sulfidic water and redissolve, but along basin margins, where the transition impinges on the bottom, Mn oxide accumulates in the bottom sediment where it reacts with organic matter to form Mn carbonate (Force and Cannon,1988; Okita, 1987; Okita et al. 1988; Maynard et al., 1990; Okita 1992, and Okita and Shanks, 1992).

The overall process can be represented schematically by the reaction
2MnO2 + CH2O + HCO3-  → 2MnCO3 + H2O + OH- .
1/2 the C incorporated in the MnCO3 comes from organic matter, which has an isotopic composition of ~ -27 permil, while the remaining 1/2 comes from oceanic HCO3 with an isotopic composition close to 0 permil.
The resulting Mn deposit will have a good correlation between Mn grade and d13C with high grade samples approaching -15 permil.

## Slide 41

**Notes:**

Crushing: Bauxite ore is crushed, washed, and dried. 
Digestion: The bauxite is heated in a caustic solution, usually sodium hydroxide, at high temperatures and pressures. 
Separation: The hot sodium aluminate slurry is separated from the undissolved residue, called "red mud". 
Precipitation: Aluminum hydroxide is precipitated from the hot solution by adding aluminum hydroxide seeds. 
Separation again: The aluminum hydroxide is separated from the spent caustic liquor. 
Washing and drying: The aluminum hydroxide is washed and heated to remove excess water. 
Calcination: The aluminum hydroxide is heated to form alumina.

## Slide 52

### Porphyry copper

- Intrusive magma- containing ~4% water

- A crust forms around the edge of the intrusion as it cools

- As the rising magma cools the hot water is released

- Pressure increases, steam explosions shatter the overlying rock

- Escaping hydrothermal solutions carrying ore metals more along the numerous fractures and cool depositing the minerals

- Fluids then continue to circulate causing alteration of the initial ore deposit
