## **CCUS: Summary**

###### **What CCUS is and why it matters (ILO 1)**

- a suite of tools aimed at the capture, utilisation and permanent storage (disposal!) of CO2

- CCUS is critical for achieving Net Zero emissions and avoiding the worst climate change impacts

###### **CCUS is an interconnected toolbox (ILO 2)**

- Includes a wide range of approaches – nature-based and/or disruptive technologies – designed to reduce CO2 emissions

- Capture, utilisation and storage are inherently linked – CCUS is not a single solution

###### **Harnessing the natural world to deliver Nature-Based Solutions (ILO 3)**

- Established nature-based solutions include:

   - soil carbon sequestration, afforestation / reforestation, biochar, enhanced weathering, habitat restoration

- Emerging disruptive biotechnologies – particularly microbial and microbiome CO2 sequestration and utilisation – are redefining and evolving nature-based solutions, including at UoM

###### **How CCUS technologies work (ILO 4)**

- Capture: direct air capture (DAC), biological fixation, enhanced weathering, biochar…

- Utilisation: CO2 to biofuels and commodity chemicals (e.g. using _Clostridium_ and _Halomonas_ species, or with pre-evolved microbiomes

- Storage: permanent geological storage targeted at depleted O&G reservoirs, saline aquifers and other (less volumetrically significant) formation types. Safe storage via physical, chemical and biological trapping mechanisms

###### **Uncertainties in CCS (ILO 5)**

- Risks: caprock integrity, leakage pathways, deleterious microbial activity (esp. sulfidogenesis, methanogenesis, bioclogging…), monitoring challenges…

- Opportunities: microbially-mediated mineralisation; deep biosphere C-fixation applied to CCU?

###### _Glossary of terms_

**isotope:** each of two or more forms of the same element that contain equal numbers of protons but different numbers of neutrons in their nuclei, and hence differ in relative atomic mass but not in chemical properties; in particular, a radioactive form of an element

**isotopic fractionation:** the separation or enrichment of one isotope of an element over another during a natural process. Methanogens preferential use lighter isotopes of CO 2 (<sup>12</sup> C) over heavier isotopes (<sup>13</sup> C) during methane production, resulting in a relative depletion of<sup>13</sup> C in biogenic methane (and enrichment in<sup>13</sup> C in the residual CO2 pool)

**clumped isotope:** heavy isotopes that are bonded to other heavy isotopes. methane clumped isotope can record its formation temperature, and therefore can be used to identify the origins of methane

**noble gases / isotopes:** The noble gases are the members of group 18 of the periodic table: helium (He), neon (Ne), argon (Ar), krypton (Kr), xenon (Xe), radon (Rn) and, in some cases, oganesson (Og). The relative isotopic abundances of noble gases serve as an important geochemical tracing tool in earth science

**methanogenesis:** the formation of methane coupled to energy conservation by Archaea known as methanogens

**hydrogenotrophic methanogenesis:** microbially-mediated conversion of CO2 and H2 to methane (below and right)

CO2 + 4H2 → CH4 + 2H2O

**AOM (Anaerobic Oxidation of Methane):** microbial methane consumption in the absence of oxygen; part of the broader methane-implicated carbon cycle in the study site

**CO2-EOR (Enhanced Oil Recovery):** a technique used to extract additional oil from reservoirs after primary and secondary recovery methods have become ineffective, by injecting CO2

#### **Tyne et al 2021** **_Nature_**

The **Olla Oil Field** (Louisiana, in the northern Gulf of Mexico) was CO2 injected between 1983 and 1986 for enhanced oil recovery (CO2-EOR)

- total 2.2 × 10<sup>8</sup> m<sup>3</sup> of CO2 was injected; ~9 x 10<sup>7</sup> m<sup>3</sup> remained in the reservoir after EOR project ended

- • water flooding in 1984

This study integrates noble gas and clumped isotope data with stable isotope and microbiological data to investigate the fate of the injected CO2

The Nebo-Hemphill Oil Field (20 km SE of Olla) served as a control, having had no CO2 injected

- significantly higher CO2 concentrations in the injected Olla Oil Field (18 ± 12mol%) vs the Nebo-Hemphill Oil Field (1.02 ± 0.69 mol%)

- CH4 concentrations at Olla are 77 ± 13 mol% (STP) compared with 92.6 ± 8.2 mol% (STP) at Nebo-Hemphill

Stable isotopes of carbon are frequently modified by chemical or biological processes; the inert nature of noble gases means that they are ideal tracers for injected CO2

#### **Tyne et al 2021:** clumped isotopes

Clumped isotope compositions provide an **independent constraint on the origin of CH4 in petroleum systems** . Thermogenic CH4 appears to dominantly form under internal isotopic equilibrium whereas microbial CH4 is highly variable in its clumped isotope compositions, with both non-equilibrium and apparent equilibrium signatures
