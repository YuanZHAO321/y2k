The Archean Earth: _4000-2500 Ma_

‘That dark backward and abysm of time’ ( _William Shakespeare_ )

_The birth of the continents and life_

## Key events in the Archean

- The rock record begins at 4030 Ma

- Most continental crust formed (60-70% current volume)

- The first continents stabilised

- The tectonic style of the Earth evolved from lid tectonics to early form of plate tectonics

- Thick sequences of sediments were deposited in the deep oceans and on shallow continental shelves

- The first life developed (microbial).

- Enigmatic iron-rich sedimentary rocks formed that have not formed since.

#### Archean crustal growth (evidence for onset of subduction)

**By 3000 Ma the mantle cooled sufficiently to develop a lithosphere of sufficient strength and rigidity for subduction to be possible**

Key evidence for timing of onset of subduction around 3.0 Ga:

- slow down in rate continental crust formation (increase in rate of destruction)

- crustal recycling (Rb-Sr, Hf, Oxygen isotope ratios of magmatic zircons)

- volumetric increase in TTG granitoids

- relative shift from Na to  K-rich granites

Onset of subduction

- change in compositional of continental crust from mafic to intermediate

The mafic upper continental crust in the early Archean was gradually replaced by a felsic upper continental crust and reached an average composition much like today around the Archean-Proterozoic boundary

MgO content ~4 wt% (present day 2-3 wt%)

MgO content >11 wt%

### The road to modern plate tectonics

- in the Hadean / early Archean subduction was inhibited and magmatism was driven by rising mantle plumes that “percolated the lithosphere”

- as the mantle cooled, lithospheric strength and rigidity increased, subduction became possible

- early subduction was probably episodic and short-lived: weak slabs → early slab break-off → loss of slab-pull → subduction ceases

- considerable variability in the structural style of orogenic belts depending on the thermal state of the deforming units

Subduction enhanced continental collisions → the creation of larger continents

### Formation of the continents

In the late Archean the continents were perhaps assembled into a supercontinent (e.g. Kenorland 2750-2500 Ma)

###### Alternative ideas:

- Based on distinctive geology: 3 separate continents (Vaalbara, Superia and Nunavutia)

- Based on palaeomagnetic grounds – single stable supercontinent (Protopangaea) 2700-600 Ma

### Deposition in the Archean

- Development of deep buoyant cratonic roots, stiffened the continents allowing them to emerge above seal level and support mountain chains

- Erosion of the mountains delivered large volumes of clastic sediments to oceans

- The continents provided a basement for sedimentary platform successions several kilometres thick and shallow, stable continental shelves:

Volcanogenic units: volcanicalstics and lavas Greywackes: mixture of sand and mud eroded from the volcanic areas

Quartzites: texturally mature quartz rich sandstones that have been recrystallised Carbonates: predominantly dolomitic (chemogenic rocks) but later limestone (biogenic)

### Origin of BIFs: Source of soluble reduced iron (Fe<sup>2+)</sup>

- Chert and iron minerals were precipitated on widespread continental shelves or shallow basins.

- The lack of obvious current or wave generated structures suggest they were deposited below the wave base at >200 m depth.

- The thick accumulations of BIFs require huge quantities of dissolved Fe2+ to be present in the oceans.

- There are two ways to achieve this:

   - oxygen-free ocean, or

   - stratified oceans with anoxic lower part containing Fe in solution

- Early in Archean, Fe<sup>2+</sup> oxidation in anoxic oceans by anoxygenic phototrophs

### Summary of key events in the Archean

- Period of voluminous increase in addition of new continental crust

- 65-70% of current continental crust in place by 3.0 Ga

- Early crust mafic with felsic intrusions

- As Earth cooled, the lithosphere thickened and more rigid, allowing episodic,

   - shallow subduction to begin around 3.0 Ga

- Subduction enhanced collisions leading to the formation of larger continents

- The continents became subaerial and mountains formed and were eroded

- Thick deposits of volcanogenic, chemogenic and clastic sediments deposited in

   - basins

- Interactions between tectonic processes (volcanism, creation and uplift of the continents), life and the atmosphere resulted in the precipitation of thick BIFs which are the major source of iron today
