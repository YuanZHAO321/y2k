## Forming the Solar System: Nebula Hypothesis

- Sun and planets formed by gravitational collapse of an interstellar gas and dust (a molecular cloud) at about same time

- Sun and planets about the same age

• Eagle nebula

- Solar nebula: huge volume of thinly dispersed gas and dust

- Gas is mainly hydrogen and helium

- Dust is fine-grained 0.1 micron – mineral grains (contain heavier elements of silica and iron)

- Temperature 10-50K

- Within this cold, dense, magnetized cloud lumps of higher density form due to turbulent motions

## Gravitational collapse and contraction of  the Solar nebula

- A nearby supernova shock wave triggered gravitational collapse and fragmentation of the cloud

- The fragments themselves collapsed and fragmented into smaller and smaller pieces

- As individual fragments continued to collapse, energy released by the collapse was radiated away and so temperature did not rise much

- Initial molecular cloud: 10-100 light years across, but fragment < 1 light year across and < 2 solar masses

- A fragment of the size required to form a Sun-sized star took a few 10 kyr to collapse to the size of the Solar System

- At this point, the core became too dense for the radiation to escape and the temperature started to rise

## Solar wind, bipolar jets and disc development

- T Tauri protostars are characterized by strong winds driven by heat of gravitational contraction, not fusion

- a bipolar outflow expel two jets of matter out in directions perpendicular to the protoplanetary disc

- inner part of disc cleared of finer dust and gas and proto-Sun become visible

- widening of jets to become spherical wind

- in 10,000 yr jets and winds expel ½ a solar mass, clear inner Solar system of orbiting bodies <10 m in size and gas, helping to redistribute mass outwards

## Stages in the formation of the planets

- Starting from tiny dust particles, larger and larger objects formed to create the asteroids and terrestrial planets

- This growth process is generally divided into three stages:

   - aggregation of micron-sized dust to 1- to 10-km-diameter planetesimals through coagulation, accretion and gravitational attraction

   - • runaway growth of the largest planetesimals to form planetary embryos

   - • aggregation of embryos to form the terrestrial planets

## Gravitational Differentiation of the Earth’s Interior

Early on, the Earth was fairly homogeneous inside

When the temperature got hot enough, iron began to melt

The iron accumulated at the center of the planet to form a metallic core

Melting resulted in the formation of magma ocean, separation of metals and molten silicates and differentiation of the Earth’s interior into a mantle and core

Key characteristics of metal and silicate

1. The high density of metal relative to silicate means the metal would sink through the silicate under the force of gravity

2. Density separation is facilitated because metal and silicate are immiscible

Immiscibility and density contrasts lead to discrete layer with a sharp boundary between them

From the perspective of the age of the solar system (4567 Ma), planetary formation and differentiation, powered by impacts and extinct radionuclides, occurred within the first 1% of solar system history

## The Formation of the Moon

The Moon formed ~4500 Ma (before the oldest lunar rocks, 4460 Ma) as the result of a ‘giant impact’

## The Giant Impact Hypothesis

Late in the Earth’s accretionary history, a protoplanet (Theia) collided with the Earth and vaporized the mantles of both bodies; some of this material was retained in Earth orbit to form a circumterrestrial disc from which the Moon accreted shortly afterward
