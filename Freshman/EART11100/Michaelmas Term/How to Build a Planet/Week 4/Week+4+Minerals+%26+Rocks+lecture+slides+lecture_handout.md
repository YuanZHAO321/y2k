### Minerals can be identified from their physical properties

- Colour: determined by chemical composition (ions and trace elements). Visual appearance plus streak.  White, clear, colourless

- Lustre: surface appearance (e.g., vitreous, resinous, silky, pearly, metallic, earthy)

- Hardness: strong chemical bonds result in hard minerals.  Relative scale (Mohs scale of hardness) 1. Talc to 10. Diamond (quartz = 7)

- Habit: the shape it takes. Depends on planes in crystal structure and typical speed and direction of growth

- Cleavage: planes of weakness in mineral related to atomic structure. Inversely related to bond strength

- Fracture: tendency to break along irregular surfaces other than cleavage planes

- Density: depends on atomic weight of atoms or ions and their closeness of packing in crystal structure

- Other special properties: taste, smell, magnetic, radioactivity

_Physical properties reflect their composition and crystal structure_

Minerals as natural resources

### Summary: Minerals

- Minerals are the building blocks of rocks

- Defined as a naturally occurring, solid, crystalline substance, usually inorganic, with a specific chemical composition

- Minerals are classified according to their chemical composition into one of 8 groups

- The silicates group are quantitatively the most important group within the Earth’s crust

- Different minerals melt and crystallize at different temperatures

- As crystals form, they extract certain elements from the melt

- The chemical composition of the melt progressively changes from mafic (low silica) to felsic (high silica)

- The physical properties of minerals are due to their composition and crystal structure, and can be used to identify them

### Summary: Rocks

Rocks are naturally occurring, solid aggregates of minerals, or in some cases, non-mineral solid matter

Identity is determined by: texture and composition

Rocks are classified according to their origin, or genesis, into three types: igneous, sedimentary and metamorphic

The three families of rocks (igneous, sedimentary and metamorphic) are related through the Rock Cycle

The Rock Cycle is the result of interactions between the plate tectonic and climate systems
