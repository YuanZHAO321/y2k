- The core of the star that has undergone a supernova explosion collapses to a neutron star or, if it is over 25 solar masses, a black hole

### Back to our story:  Towards the formation of the first galaxies

• The intense ultraviolet radiation emitted by the super-massive Population III stars re-ionized the surrounding medium (stripped electrons from hydrogen atoms)

- Shock waves from the supernovae that marked the end of these stars compressed the surrounding ionized gas triggering a burst of star formation

- Black holes formed from core collapse

- By 250 myr: black holes of at least 400 solar masses were in place

- Ongoing gravitational collapse around these black holes led to the formation of the first galaxies by 400

myr

## Milky Way galaxy- geography and geometry

- a rotating, barred spiral galaxy

- disc of stars ~ 100000 light years

   - across by ~ 1000 light years thick

- bulges near the centre

- surrounded by spherical halo containing globular star clusters

- It has a supermassive black hole (Sagittarius A*) at its centre

Artist’s impression looking down on the galactic disc (above), and a section through the plane of the disc (left)

## MW Stellar populations- age of stars and orbits

- Young stars are in the disc

- Older stars are distributed within the disc, bulge and halo

- Very old stars are in the globular clusters of the halo

The galactic disc contains most of the galaxy’s 200-400 billion stars and nearly all its gas and dust

- Disc stars have approximately circular orbits within the mid-plane of the disc

- Bulge and halo stars have elliptical orbits that are randomly oriented to the galactic plane

######

• The Milky Way galaxy began as several overdensities in mass distribution; globular clusters were seeded within these (~13200 Ma)

• Gravitational attractions led to the merger of these over-densities and to the initiation of spinning; stars in the inner halo were forming by ~11500 Ma

• Conservation of angular momentum led to collapse of the gaseous interstellar medium from a spheroid shape to a disc (by 8800 ± 1700 Ma)

## Changing interstellar environment as the Sun orbits

• The Sun is currently in the Local Bubble, a very low density region of the interstellar medium that was sculpted by supernovae

• It is passing through the Local Interstellar Cloud, a slightly higher density region that comprises material flowing away from the sites of several supernovae that have occurred within the Scorpius-Centaurus Star Association over the past 15 myr

500 light yr

• Material from these supernovae, dated at 3.2-1.5 Ma and 8.7-6.5 Ma has been found in deep sea sediments and in lunar soils
