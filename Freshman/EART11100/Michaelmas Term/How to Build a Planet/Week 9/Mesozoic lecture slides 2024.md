## Mesozoic Era: summary

- Mesozoic era lasted 186 Ma (between 252-66 Ma) and comprises the Triassic, Jurassic and Cretaceous periods

- The supercontinent Pangea rifted into separate land masses leading to sea level transgression and episodic volcanism (LIPs) resulting in major climatic changes

- Cool greenhouse conditions alternated with warm greenhouse and even 

-

- hothouse conditions, global average temperatures 6–9 C warmer than present

- Global sea levels were mainly high, and the content of atmospheric O2 was varying between 15 and 25%

- Degassing from volcanism linked to the rifting process of Pangaea increased atmospheric CO2 levels by up to 16x higher than present, exerting a major climate control

- Continental break-up modified landmass configurations generating huge epicontinental seas and altering oceanic circulation patterns

- Mesozoic extinction events have been related to the effects of rifting and flood volcanism and/or asteroid impacts (K-Pg)

## Calcite compensation depth

###### The CCD controls the distribution of siliceous and calcareous oozes on the ocean floor

- CaCO3 solubility increases with depth (due to temperature decrease and pressure increase)

- At CCD rate of calcium carbonate material forming and sinking is equal to the rate the material is dissolving

- • Most opaline silica dissolves in surface waters (warmer, Al/Si of shells, high surface area) only about 3% of global silica flux is buried in sediment

## The depth of the CCD has changed through time

Distribution of pelagic and neritic sediments today

- Cretaceous - early Eocene the CCD was much shallower due to intense volcanic activity = higher atmospheric CO2 concentrations (higher pCO2 in oceans) → increased dissolved CO 2 in surface waters

- Palaeozoic and early Mesozoic, prior to evolution of coccolithophores, siliceous oozes may have deposited at much shallower depths than when there was calcareous plankton available for sedimentation
