### Key points of the lecture

-  The Earth is differentiated into layers, not only according to their chemistry, but also based on material strength.

-  The Earths shell is fragmented into plates that are constantly moving.

-  The boundaries between the plates are classified based on observations of their relative motions and geologic activity.

-  The motion of plates over the Earth has led to the creation and destruction of supercontinents through time and this has impacted on global climate, sea level and the biosphere (Earth system).

#### **Continental crust:**

 underlies continents  thicker and less dense  av. thickness 40 km

 seismic wave velocities ~6 km/s  composition more felsic: granite-like  derived by a sequence of melting processes

### What Drives Plate Tectonics?

Plate motion is driven by mantle convection

_Is this the way plates move; passively dragged along by convection currents in the mantle?_

Old and modern images of mantle convection

Two forces believed to move plates: • Ridge push • Slab pull (including basal drag)

Common analogy for mantle convection

### What Drives Plate Tectonics?

Slab pull: plates descend into the mantle under their own weight (cold, dense plates into warm, less dense asthenosphere)

Ridge push: the plate over the mid ocean ridge is elevated, leading to gravitational sliding

### Mantle plumes and hot spots

Hot-spot volcanoes are probably a consequence of mantle plumes (areas of hot, upwelling mantle).

As a plate moves over a hot spot a chain of volcanoes is left behind

The Hawaiian Islands are an example of such a hot-spot track The islands increase in age with distance from the present hot spot
