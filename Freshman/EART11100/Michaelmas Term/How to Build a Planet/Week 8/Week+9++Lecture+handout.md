###### _Timing and amplitudes of geologic mechanisms of sea level change_

Eustatic sea level (ESL) changes in mass and volume of oceans (1) climatic changes in continental water storage (as liquid or ice), ocean salinity or temperature

- (2) geological forces changing ocean basin volume (continental collision, sedimentation or sea floor spreading)

Relative sea level change = land uplift and subsidence (vertical tectonics) does not affect ocean water volume and causes relative sea level changes that vary over a range of spatial and temporal scales

### Indicators of Phanerozoic sea level change

Sea level

- When sea levels rise the shoreline migrates inland and the low-lying coastal plains or margins of the continents are submerged. These times of rising sea level are called **transgressions** and during these periods shallow marine sediments buried the continental deposits.

- Falling sea levels are called **regressions** and regional unconformities develop due to erosion or non-deposition.

###### **Evidence for global eustatic variations:**

- satellite measurements (<10 yrs),

- tide gauges (<150 yrs)

- shoreline markers - tropical reefs and atolls formed by shallow dwelling corals (< few 100 ka)

- oxygen isotopes (<sup>18</sup> O) of foraminifera (<200 Ma)

- Similar ages of unconformities on different continents have been interpreted as indicating that the surfaces were caused by a global process, eustasy (long term Phanerozoic record)

### Ordovician sea level and climate

- Continent dispersal led to a pronounced transgression in mid-Ordovician creating large areas of tropical continental shelves on the margins of the continents.

- Throughout the Ordovician the climate was generally warm until a severe Late Ordovician glacial event.

- **Calcrete** is the weathered crust in arid and semiarid regions, sand and silt cemented by calcite, dolomite, gypsum, halite, and ferric oxide.

- **Evaporite** largely bedded halite, sylvite, gypsum, anhydrite, and various potash salts form from the precipitation of solid mineral crystals from a concentrated solution of salt- or freshwater. Evaporite deposits may be either marine or nonmarine (lacustrine) in origin.

### Late Carboniferous coal measure formation

- Plant matter accumulates rapidly in stagnant waters e.g. swamps or lakes on a river floodplain

- Oxygen is quickly used up in the oxidation process

- The water becomes anoxic and partly decomposed plant material remains

- Swamps are periodically inundated by sediment during times of flood of the rivers

- In lakes starved of large amounts of sediment input, organic-rich muds accumulate

- Burial and compression of sediment and plant material expels water, forming peat

- As burial continues the temperature and pressure increases and the ‘grade ‘ of the coal increases
