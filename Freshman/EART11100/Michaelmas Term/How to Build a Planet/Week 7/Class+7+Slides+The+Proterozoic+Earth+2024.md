## Palaeoproterozoic versus Neoproterozoic Glaciations

- The Palaeoproterozoic Huronian glaciations are thought to have resulted mainly from the combined effects of supercontinentality and weak solar radiation

- Neoproterozoic glaciations occurred ~1.4 Ga later when solar radiation was greater, lower CO2 content of the atmosphere (drawdown from weathering and mantle cooling), and the location of the fragmenting supercontinent Rodinia at the equator, are thought to be the main causes

- Amalgamation of the supercontinent Columbia in the ‘Barren Billion’ shows little evidence of significant climate change, and suggests there was a balance maintained between weathering intensity, solar luminosity and mantle temperatures that did not lead to dramatic changes in climate or biological productivity
