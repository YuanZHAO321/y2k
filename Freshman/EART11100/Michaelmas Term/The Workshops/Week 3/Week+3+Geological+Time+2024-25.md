# Understanding the Earth Workshop

## Week 3 Stratigraphic correlation using absolute dating methods

The aims of this week's workshop are to:

- compare the subdivision of geologic time using relative and absolute dating methods

- introduce the geologic time scale and its major subdivisions

- introduce methods used to determine the absolute age of rock units including the concept of radioactive decay

- compare and contrast the most appropriate dating methods for different types and ages of material

The fundamental unit of<br>biostratigraphy is the biozone<br>Biozones are defined by their<br>zone fossils<br><!-- End of picture text -->

The boundaries of the biozone may be specified in different ways Total range biozone = biozone defined by the total (or local) range of one taxon Consecutive range biozone = biozone defined by the range of one taxon, B, in the sequence A → B → C Partial range biozone = biozone defined as that part of the range of fossil group B that is above the last appearance of fossil group A but below the first appearance of fossil group C Assemblage biozone = biozone defined by the coexistence of a particular set of taxa Acme biozone = biozone defined by the exceptional abundance of one taxon

### Correlation combining lithostratigraphy and biostratigraphy

- (a) Using the γ-ray log as a sand/mud indicator, complete the lithological logs at localities 1 and 2, using the symbols in the key.

(b) Using a coloured pen or pencil suggest lithological correlations between the two localities. The succession you are looking at lies between two volcanic ash bands which have been correlated, and are thus both lithological and time markers.

(c) Look at the distribution of microfossils at locality 2, and define some ‘zones’ based on assemblages of fossils. Which lithology contains the fossils? Why do fossils only occur in certain parts of the succession?

- (d) Now look at the microfossil assemblages at Locality 1.  Can you recognise the same zones you defined at Locality 2?

(e)  Using a different coloured pen or pencil from that used for your lithostratigraphic correlations, suggest some tentative biostratigraphic correlations between the two localities.

(f) Comment briefly on the similarities and differences in the zones between the two localities and the significance of the correlations you have made.
